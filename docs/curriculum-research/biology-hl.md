### 1.2 External Assessment Structure (80% of Final Grade)

**Paper 3 and all Elective Options (A–D) are permanently eliminated in the 2025 syllabus.** All students sit two external papers (Paper 1 and Paper 2).

| ▸ **Paper 1A** (MCQ) | **SL**<br>**HL** | *Included above* | **30**<br>**40** | *Part of 36%* | Multiple-choice questions (4 options A–D; **no negative marking**). Tests breadth across SL (or SL+AHL for HL). |

| **Paper 2** | **SL**<br>**HL** | **1 h 30 min**<br>**2 h 30 min** | **50**<br>**80** | **44%**<br>**44%** | Single booklet divided into Section A and Section B. |
| ▸ **Paper 2 Section A** | **SL**<br>**HL** | *Included above* | **34**<br>**48** | *Part of 44%* | **All compulsory:** 1 major data-based question (unfamiliar experimental data) + structured short-answer questions across SL (or SL+AHL). |

| **Internal Assessment (IA)** | **SL / HL** | ~10 hours | **24** | **20%** | Individual Scientific Investigation report (max 3,000 words). Identical rubric at SL and HL. |

#### Scaling Formula (HL Final Grade Calculation)
Raw marks total $75 + 80 + 24 = 179$ at HL (and $55 + 50 + 24 = 129$ at SL), scaled to **100**:
$$\text{Final Scaled \% (HL)} = \left(\frac{\text{Paper 1}}{75} \times 36\right) + \left(\frac{\text{Paper 2}}{80} \times 44\right) + \left(\frac{\text{IA}}{24} \times 20\right)$$

#### Calculator & Data Booklet Policy (Critical 2025 Rule Change)
* **Calculators:** An approved **scientific calculator or graphic display calculator (GDC)** is **permitted and required across BOTH Paper 1 (1A and 1B) and Paper 2**. Unlike the pre-2025 syllabus (where calculators were banned on Paper 1), quantitative data analysis and statistics appear throughout Paper 1A and 1B. GDCs must have CAS disabled, memory cleared, and be placed in IB **Exam Mode / Press-to-Test mode**.
* **No Data / Formula Booklet (2025–2027 Cohorts):** There is **no separate IB Biology data or formula booklet** provided during 2025–2027 examinations (IB has announced a Biology data booklet starting for May 2028 first assessment). Students **must memorize** all required biological formulas (magnification, $\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$, Lincoln index, Hardy-Weinberg, $R_f$, mitotic index, percentage change/difference, SI prefixes) and standard conversions; when codon tables or $\chi^2$ critical value tables are required for a specific item, they are printed inside the question stimulus.

### 1.3 Internal Assessment (Scientific Investigation) Overview (20% of Final Grade)

* **Time Allocation & Weighting:** 10 hours recommended; **20%** of final grade (SL and HL identical).
* **Maximum Word Count:** **3,000 words** hard cap.
  * **Excluded from word count:** Data tables, graphs/charts, diagrams/annotated photos, equations/formulae/sample calculations, in-text citations and reference list/bibliography, structural headings/titles, and candidate code header.
  * **No cover page or contents page required; appendices are NOT read or moderated** — all essential analysis and justification must sit in the main report.
* **Investigation Formats Allowed:**
  1. Hands-on laboratory experimentation
  2. Ecological fieldwork
  3. Spreadsheet modeling / simulation analysis
  4. Secondary database extraction and analysis
  5. Computer simulation investigations
* **Group vs. Individual Rules:**
  * Students may collaborate in small groups (**up to 3 students**) during planning and data collection using a **shared general methodology or apparatus**.
  * However, **every student must formulate a unique, distinct research question** (e.g., varying a different independent/dependent variable or biological system), collect **unique raw data** (no shared data tables unless explicitly structured as distinct subsets per IB protocol and appropriately cited), conduct independent analysis, and write a **100% individual report**.
* **Assessment Criteria (24 Marks Total, Equal Weighting — 25% of IA each):**
  * **Criterion A: Research Design** (0–6 marks)
  * **Criterion B: Data Analysis** (0–6 marks)
  * **Criterion C: Conclusion** (0–6 marks)
  * **Criterion D: Evaluation** (0–6 marks)
  * *(Legacy 2016 criteria "Personal Engagement" and "Communication" were removed; communication clarity/precision is now embedded inside Criteria A–D.)*

### Theme A: Unity and Diversity
*Common ancestry has given living organisms many shared features while evolution has resulted in the rich biodiversity of life on Earth.* *(19h SL / 33h HL)*

#### `A1.1` Water *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `A1.1.1–A1.1.6` `[SL/Core]`; `A1.1.7–A1.1.8` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What physical and chemical properties of water make it essential for life? What are the challenges and opportunities of water as a habitat?
* **Linking Questions:** How do the various intermolecular forces of attraction affect biological systems? What biological processes only happen at or near surfaces? What are the implications of solubility differences between chemical substances for living organisms?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `A1.1.1` `[SL]` **Medium for life:** First cells originated in water; water remains the aqueous medium for nearly all metabolic reactions.
  * `A1.1.2` `[SL]` **Polarity & hydrogen bonds:** Unequal electron sharing between O ($\delta^-$) and H ($\delta^+$) creates polar covalent bonds; electrostatic attraction between $\delta^+$ H and $\delta^-$ O of adjacent molecules forms hydrogen bonds (must draw $\ge 2$ molecules with $\delta^+/\delta^-$ and dashed H-bonds).
  * `A1.1.3` `[SL]` **Cohesion:** H-bonding binds water molecules together, enabling transport under negative tension in plant xylem and creating surface tension (habitat for water striders / surface organisms).
  * `A1.1.4` `[SL]` **Adhesion:** H-bonding/attraction between polar water and polar/charged surfaces drives capillary action in soil pores and cellulose plant cell walls.
  * `A1.1.5` `[SL]` **Solvent properties:** Polarity forms hydration shells around ions and polar solutes (enabling enzyme catalysis in solution and transport in blood plasma/phloem/xylem), while hydrophobic/insoluble molecules (lipids, membrane cores, steroid hormones) form barriers and require carriers.
  * `A1.1.6` `[SL]` **Physical properties vs. air (aquatic habitats):** Higher buoyancy, viscosity, thermal conductivity, and specific heat capacity ($4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$) than air. Required species contrast: **black-throated loon (*Gavia arctica*)** (dense bones reduce buoyancy for diving, streamlined shape overcomes high water viscosity, waterproof plumage insulates against high thermal conductivity) and **ringed seal (*Pusa hispida*)** (thick blubber layer minimizes heat loss due to water's high thermal conductivity; hydrodynamic body reduces drag; buoyancy aids swimming).
  * `A1.1.7` `[HL-only]` **Extraplanetary origin & retention:** Water arrived via **asteroids** (limit hypothesis to asteroids); retained on Earth over billions of years due to sufficient **gravity** and **temperatures low enough** to condense water as liquid.
  * `A1.1.8` `[HL-only]` **Search for extraterrestrial life:** Presence of liquid water as primary biosignature; concept of the **"Goldilocks zone"** (circumstellar habitable zone where surface temperatures allow liquid water).

#### `A1.2` Nucleic Acids *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `A1.2.1–A1.2.10` `[SL/Core]`; `A1.2.11–A1.2.15` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How does the structure of nucleic acids allow hereditary information to be stored? How does the structure of DNA facilitate accurate replication?
* **Linking Questions:** What explains the use of certain molecular building blocks in all living cells? What makes RNA more likely to have been the first genetic material, rather than DNA? How can polymerization result in emergent properties?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `A1.2.1` `[SL]` **Universal genetic material:** DNA is the genetic material of all living organisms (RNA viruses exist, e.g., HIV, coronaviruses, but viruses are non-living).
  * `A1.2.2–A1.2.5` `[SL]` **Nucleotides & polymerization:** Nucleotide = phosphate (circle) + pentose sugar (pentagon) + nitrogenous base (rectangle). Condensation reactions link sugar ($3'$) to phosphate ($5'$) forming a strong covalent **sugar–phosphate backbone**. Bases: DNA (`A, T, C, G`), RNA (`A, U, C, G`).
  * `A1.2.6–A1.2.7` `[SL]` **DNA double helix vs. RNA:** DNA is double-stranded, **antiparallel** ($5'\to 3'$ opposite $3'\to 5'$), joined by H-bonds (`A=T`, `G≡C`; note: memorizing exact H-bond counts or purine/pyrimidine ring sizes is NOT required at SL). RNA is single-stranded, uses **ribose** (extra `-OH` on C2') vs. **deoxyribose**, and **uracil (U)** vs. **thymine (T)**.
  * `A1.2.8–A1.2.10` `[SL]` **Information capacity & universal code:** Complementary base pairing enables exact replication and transcription; limitless possible sequences/lengths give massive data density; conservation of the triplet genetic code across all life supports universal common ancestry.
  * `A1.2.11` `[HL-only]` **Directionality:** $5'$ phosphate-to-$3'$ hydroxyl phosphodiester linkages dictate $5'\to 3'$ directionality of DNA replication, transcription, and mRNA reading during translation.
  * `A1.2.12` `[HL-only]` **Purine–pyrimidine pairing:** Double-ring **purines** (A, G) always pair with single-ring **pyrimidines** (T, C), giving uniform helix diameter regardless of base sequence.
  * `A1.2.13` `[HL-only]` **Nucleosome structure (`AOS` molecular visualization):** DNA wrapped around an **octamer of 8 histone proteins**, locked by an additional **H1 linker histone** attached to linker DNA.
  * `A1.2.14` `[HL-only]` **Hershey–Chase experiment (`NOS`):** Bacteriophage T2 labeled with $^{35}\text{S}$ (protein capsid) vs. $^{32}\text{P}$ (DNA); centrifugation showed $^{32}\text{P}$ inside bacterial pellets while $^{35}\text{S}$ remained in supernatant, proving DNA is genetic material. *NOS: Availability of radioisotopes opened new experimental possibilities.*
  * `A1.2.15` `[HL-only]` **Chargaff's data (`NOS`):** Across species, $\%A \approx \%T$ and $\%G \approx \%C$, while $(A+T)/(G+C)$ varies by species. *NOS: Problem of induction addressed by certainty of falsification — falsified Levene's tetranucleotide hypothesis (repeating 4-base unit).*

#### `A2.1` Origins of Cells **[HL-only Subtopic]**
* **Level:** 2 (Cells) | **Scope:** `A2.1.1–A2.1.9` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What plausible hypothesis could account for the origin of life? What intermediate stages could there have been between non-living matter and the first living cells?
* **Linking Questions:** For what reasons is heredity an essential feature of living things? What is needed for structures to be able to evolve by natural selection?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `A2.1.1` `[HL]` **Early Earth conditions:** Reducing/anoxic atmosphere lacking free $\text{O}_2$ and ozone ($\text{O}_3$), rich in $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ vapor; high temperatures and intense UV radiation drove abiotic synthesis of organic carbon compounds.
  * `A2.1.2–A2.1.3` `[HL]` **Self-sustaining cells & requirements (`NOS`):** Viruses are non-living (lack independent metabolism/reproduction). First cells required four thresholds: catalysis, molecular self-replication, self-assembly of polymers, and compartmentalization. *NOS: Claims must be testable, yet pre-biotic conditions cannot be fully replicated and protocells left no fossils.*
  * `A2.1.4` `[HL]` **Miller–Urey experiment:** Spark-discharge apparatus cycling $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2$ produced amino acids/organic monomers; evaluate strengths (demonstrated abiotic monomer synthesis) vs. limitations (actual early atmosphere likely contained more $\text{CO}_2/\text{N}_2$ and less $\text{CH}_4/\text{NH}_3$).
  * `A2.1.5` `[HL]` **Spontaneous vesicles:** Amphipathic fatty acids spontaneously coalesce into spherical bilayers (protocells), separating internal aqueous chemistry from external environment.
  * `A2.1.6` `[HL]` **RNA World hypothesis:** RNA stores genetic information, self-replicates via complementary base pairing, and acts as catalytic **ribozymes** (evidence: peptidyl transferase ribozyme in modern ribosomes still catalyzes peptide bonds).
  * `A2.1.7–A2.1.9` `[HL]` **LUCA & hydrothermal vents:** Evidence for Last Universal Common Ancestor (LUCA): universal genetic code and ~355+ shared conserved genes; competing early lineages likely went extinct. Dated (~3.5–4.0+ bya) using ancient seafloor hydrothermal vent precipitate microfossils, stromatolites, carbon isotope ratios, and molecular phylogenomics; LUCA conserved genes align with anaerobic chemolithoautotrophic metabolism near alkaline hydrothermal vents.

#### `A2.2` Cell Structure *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 2 (Cells) | **Scope:** `A2.2.1–A2.2.11` `[SL/Core]`; `A2.2.12–A2.2.14` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What are the features common to all cells and the features that differ? How is microscopy used to investigate cell structure?
* **Linking Questions:** What explains the use of certain molecular building blocks in all living cells? What are some features of a compelling theory? What are examples of structure–function correlations at each level of biological organization? How is the structure of specialized cells related to function?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `A2.2.1` `[SL]` **Cell theory (`NOS`):** Cells are the basic structural unit of all living organisms. *NOS: Deductive reasoning generates predictions (any newly discovered organism will consist of $\ge 1$ cell).*
  * `A2.2.2` `[SL]` **Microscopy skills (`AOS` + `NOS`):** Temporary mounts, staining, **eyepiece graticule** calibration, coarse/fine focus, calculating actual size and magnification ($M = \frac{\text{Image}}{\text{Actual}}$), scale bars. *NOS: Instrument measurement = quantitative observation.*
  * `A2.2.3` `[SL]` **Developments in microscopy:** Electron microscopy (TEM internal ultrastructure / SEM 3D surface; ~0.1–2 nm resolution vs. ~200 nm light limit), **freeze-fracture EM** (splits bilayers to reveal transmembrane proteins), **cryogenic EM (cryo-EM)** (flash-frozen native protein/virus complexes), **fluorescent stains & immunofluorescence** (antibody-linked fluorophores localize specific molecules in live/fixed cells).
  * `A2.2.4` `[SL]` **Universal cell features:** DNA genetic material, aqueous cytoplasm, lipid plasma membrane.
  * `A2.2.5` `[SL]` **Prokaryotes:** Exemplified by Gram-positive eubacteria (*Bacillus*, *Staphylococcus*): peptidoglycan cell wall, plasma membrane, cytoplasm, naked circular DNA in nucleoid, **70S ribosomes** (no membrane-bound organelles). Variations like wall-less mycoplasmas acknowledged but not tested in detail.
  * `A2.2.6–A2.2.8` `[SL]` **Eukaryotes & kingdom comparison:** Compartmentalized cytoplasm with **80S ribosomes**, double-membrane nucleus with pores & DNA+histone chromosomes, mitochondria, rER/sER, Golgi, vesicles/lysosomes, microtubule/microfilament cytoskeleton. Differences: **Plants** (cellulose wall, large permanent sap vacuole, chloroplasts/plastids, no centrioles/cilia); **Fungi** (chitin wall, vacuoles, no chloroplasts, mostly no centrioles/flagella); **Animals** (no wall, small temporary vacuoles, no plastids, centrioles, cilia/flagella in some cells).
  * `A2.2.7` `[SL]` **8 Processes of life in unicellular organisms:** Homeostasis, metabolism, nutrition, movement, excretion, growth, response to stimuli, reproduction.
  * `A2.2.9` `[SL]` **Atypical eukaryotic cells (nuclear number examples):** **Aseptate fungal hyphae** (multinucleated continuous cytoplasm), **skeletal muscle fibres** (multinucleated syncytium), **mammalian red blood cells** (anucleate when mature), **phloem sieve tube elements** (anucleate, supported by companion cell).
  * `A2.2.10–A2.2.11` `[SL]` **Micrograph identification & drawing (`AOS`):** Distinguish prokaryote/plant/animal in LM & EM; identify and draw+annotate with functions: nucleoid, prokaryotic cell wall, nucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast, sap vacuole, Golgi apparatus, rER, sER, chromosomes, ribosomes, cell wall, plasma membrane, secretory vesicles, microvilli.
  * `A2.2.12` `[HL-only]` **Endosymbiosis (`NOS`):** Common unicellular nucleated sexually-reproducing ancestor engulfed aerobic bacterium $\to$ mitochondria; some later engulfed photosynthetic cyanobacterium $\to$ chloroplasts. Evidence: double membranes, **70S ribosomes**, **naked circular DNA**, semi-autonomous binary division, inner membrane chemistry/antibiotic sensitivity. *NOS: Theory strength rests on explanatory scope and predictive power.*
  * `A2.2.13–A2.2.14` `[HL-only]` **Differentiation & multicellularity:** Differentiation arises via differential gene expression triggered by chemical/environmental gradients; multicellularity evolved independently multiple times across fungi, algae, plants, animals, enabling larger size and division of labor.

#### `A2.3` Viruses **[HL-only Subtopic]**
* **Level:** 2 (Cells) | **Scope:** `A2.3.1–A2.3.6` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How can viruses exist with so few genes? In what ways do viruses vary?
* **Linking Questions:** What mechanisms contribute to convergent evolution? To what extent is the natural history of life characterized by increasing complexity or simplicity?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `A2.3.1–A2.3.2` `[HL]` **Viral features & diversity:** Small fixed size; nucleic acid (ss/ds DNA or ss/ds RNA); protein **capsid**; no cytoplasm; few/no enzymes (obligate intracellular parasites). Enveloped vs. non-enveloped. Required examples: **bacteriophage lambda** (dsDNA, non-enveloped, head-tail), **coronaviruses** (ssRNA, enveloped with spike proteins), **HIV** (retrovirus ssRNA with reverse transcriptase, enveloped).
  * `A2.3.3–A2.3.4` `[HL]` **Lytic vs. lysogenic cycles (bacteriophage lambda):**
    * *Lytic:* Attachment $\to$ injection of viral DNA $\to$ host machinery hijacked for replication/transcription/translation $\to$ assembly $\to$ cell lysis releasing phages.
    * *Lysogenic:* Phage DNA integrates into host chromosome as a **prophage**, replicating silently with host cell divisions until stress triggers excision and entry into the lytic cycle.
  * `A2.3.5` `[HL]` **Multiple origins & convergent evolution:** High structural/genetic diversity implies polyphyletic origins (escaped cellular DNA/RNA elements vs. reduced parasitic cells); shared universal genetic code with host cells; similarities reflect convergent evolution toward obligate parasitism.
  * `A2.3.6` `[HL]` **Rapid viral evolution:** Driven by high mutation rates (RNA polymerases/reverse transcriptase lack proofreading), short generation times, huge population sizes, and genetic reassortment/recombination. Required examples: **Influenza** (antigenic drift/shift requiring annual vaccine updates) and **HIV** (rapid mutation within a single host creates drug-resistant strains and defeats vaccines, requiring combination antiretroviral therapy).

#### `A3.1` Diversity of Organisms *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 3 (Organisms) | **Scope:** `A3.1.1–A3.1.11` `[SL/Core]`; `A3.1.12–A3.1.15` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What is a species? What patterns are seen in the diversity of genomes within and between species?

#### `B1.1` Carbohydrates and Lipids *(SL/Core Only — No HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `B1.1.1–B1.1.13` `[SL/Core]`
* **Guiding Questions:** In what ways do variations in form allow diversity of function in carbohydrates and lipids? How do carbohydrates and lipids compare as energy storage compounds?
* **Linking Questions:** How can compounds synthesized by living organisms accumulate and become carbon sinks? What are the roles of oxidation and reduction in biological systems?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `B1.1.1` `[SL]` **Carbon chemistry (`NOS`):** Carbon forms up to 4 covalent bonds (single/double, branched/unbranched chains, single/multiple rings). *NOS: Scientific conventions rely on international agreement — SI metric prefixes `kilo` ($10^3$), `centi` ($10^{-2}$), `milli` ($10^{-3}$), `micro` ($10^{-6}$), `nano` ($10^{-9}$).*
  * `B1.1.2–B1.1.3` `[SL]` **Condensation & hydrolysis:** Condensation joins monomers into polymers (polysaccharides, polypeptides, nucleic acids) releasing $\text{H}_2\text{O}$; hydrolysis splits polymers by adding $-\text{H}$ and $-\text{OH}$ from $\text{H}_2\text{O}$.
  * `B1.1.4` `[SL]` **Monosaccharides:** Recognize ring diagrams of pentoses (ribose/deoxyribose) and hexoses ($\alpha/\beta$-glucose). Glucose links structure to use: polar/soluble (blood transport), chemically stable, high ATP energy yield on oxidation.
  * `B1.1.5–B1.1.6` `[SL]` **Polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose):**
    * **Starch** (amylose helical + amylopectin branched $\alpha$-glucose) & **glycogen** (highly branched $\alpha$-glucose): compact coiling/branching, osmotically inactive/insoluble due to large size, rapid enzymatic hydrolysis of terminal glucose units.
    * **Cellulose:** Alternating inverted $\beta$-glucose monomers form straight unbranched chains bundled into microfibrils cross-linked by H-bonds $\to$ high tensile strength in plant cell walls.
  * `B1.1.7` `[SL]` **Glycoproteins in cell–cell recognition:** Example: **ABO blood group antigens** on erythrocytes.
  * `B1.1.8–B1.1.11` `[SL]` **Lipids & fatty acids:** Hydrophobic/non-polar (fats, oils, waxes, steroids). Condensation of 1 glycerol + 3 fatty acids $\to$ **triglyceride** (3 ester bonds); 1 glycerol + 2 fatty acids + 1 phosphate $\to$ **phospholipid**. **Saturated** (0 `C=C` bonds, straight packing, high melting point $\to$ solid fats in endotherms) vs. **monounsaturated** (1 `C=C`) vs. **polyunsaturated** ($\ge 2$ `C=C`, kinks prevent packing, low melting point $\to$ liquid plant oils). Triglycerides provide long-term energy storage (~2× energy density per gram vs. carbohydrates, anhydrous/lightweight) and thermal insulation (blubber/adipose in cold habitats).
  * `B1.1.12–B1.1.13` `[SL]` **Amphipathic phospholipids & steroids:** Amphipathic head (hydrophilic phosphate) + hydrophobic fatty acid tails spontaneously self-assemble into bilayers. Non-polar **steroids** (4-ring structure; recognize **oestradiol** and **testosterone**) diffuse directly across the hydrophobic bilayer core.

Measure/calculate initial rates ($\Delta y/\Delta t$ or $1/t$). Sketch & interpret activation energy ($E_a$) barrier profiles. *NOS: Sketch graphs are idealized biological models evaluated against experimental data.*
  * `C1.1.11–C1.1.13` `[HL-only]` **Pathway localization, thermogenesis & topology:** Intracellular (glycolysis, Krebs) vs. extracellular (gut digestion). Metabolic inefficiency (<100%) inevitably releases **heat** (used by endotherms/birds/mammals). **Linear** (glycolysis) vs. **cyclical** (Krebs cycle, Calvin cycle) pathways.
  * `C1.1.14–C1.1.17` `[HL-only]` **Enzyme Inhibition (4 types):**
    1. **Competitive (reversible):** Competes at active site (e.g., **statins** inhibiting HMG-CoA reductase); high $[S]$ restores $V_{\max}$ (increases apparent $K_m$).
    2. **Non-competitive / allosteric (reversible):** Binds distinct **allosteric site**, causing conformational change distorting active site; high $[S]$ CANNOT restore $V_{\max}$.
    3. **End-product / feedback inhibition:** Final pathway product allosterically inhibits first committed enzyme (required example: **isoleucine** inhibiting threonine deaminase).

#### `C1.2` Cell Respiration *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `C1.2.1–C1.2.6` `[SL/Core]`; `C1.2.7–C1.2.17` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What are the roles of hydrogen and oxygen in the release of energy in cells? How is energy distributed and used inside cells?
* **Linking Questions:** In what forms is energy stored in living organisms? What are the consequences of respiration for ecosystems?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `C1.2.1–C1.2.4` `[SL]` **ATP currency & respiration vs. gas exchange:** Adenosine triphosphate (nucleotide) transfers energy for active transport, anabolism, and movement via hydrolysis ($\text{ATP} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{ADP} + \text{P}_i$, releasing energy) and is resynthesized via phosphorylation. Principal substrates: glucose and fatty acids. Clearly distinguish intracellular **cell respiration** from organismal **gas exchange**.
  * `C1.2.5–C1.2.6` `[SL]` **Aerobic vs. anaerobic respiration in humans (`AOS`):** Compare substrates (glucose only anaerobically vs. glucose/lipids aerobically), $\text{O}_2$ requirement, relative ATP yield (2 net vs. ~30–32), waste products (lactate vs. $\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$), and location (cytoplasm vs. cytoplasm + mitochondria). Measure/calculate respiration rates (respirometer data, $\text{O}_2$ uptake or $\text{CO}_2$ production).
  * `C1.2.7–C1.2.10` `[HL-only]` **Redox, Glycolysis & Fermentation:** Oxidation = loss of electrons / hydrogen (dehydrogenation); $\text{NAD}^+$ accepts $2e^- + \text{H}^+$ $\to$ **reduced NAD ($\text{NADH}$)**.
    * **Glycolysis** (cytoplasm): Glucose (6C) $\to$ phosphorylation (consumes 2 ATP) $\to$ lysis into two 3C triose phosphates $\to$ oxidation ($\text{NAD}^+ \to \text{NADH}$) & substrate-level ATP formation (4 ATP gross $\to$ **2 net ATP** + **2 NADH** + **2 pyruvate**; intermediate names NOT required).
    * **Anaerobic NAD regeneration:** Humans reduce pyruvate $\to$ **lactate**; **yeast** decarboxylates pyruvate $\to$ ethanal + $\text{CO}_2$, then reduces to **ethanol** (brewing/baking), regenerating $\text{NAD}^+$ so glycolysis can continue yielding 2 ATP/glucose.
  * `C1.2.11–C1.2.16` `[HL-only]` **Link Reaction, Krebs Cycle, ETC & Chemiosmosis:**
    * **Link reaction** (matrix): Pyruvate (3C) undergoes oxidative decarboxylation $\to$ **acetyl-CoA** (2C) + $\text{CO}_2$ + $\text{NADH}$.
    * **Krebs cycle** (matrix): Acetyl (2C) + **oxaloacetate (4C)** $\to$ **citrate (6C)**; cyclic regeneration of oxaloacetate via **4 oxidations (dehydrogenations)** and **2 decarboxylations**, producing $\text{CO}_2$, ATP (1 per turn), and reduced NAD (note: 2025 guide specifies naming ONLY citrate and oxaloacetate and emphasizes reduced NAD; FAD/FADH$_2$ is supplementary detail).
    * **ETC & Oxidative phosphorylation** (cristae): Reduced NAD donates $e^-$ to inner membrane carriers (complex names NOT required); $e^-$ flow pumps $\text{H}^+$ into intermembrane space; **chemiosmosis** via **ATP synthase** phosphorylates ADP $\to$ ATP. **Oxygen** is the **terminal electron acceptor** ($\frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2 + 2e^- + 2\text{H}^+ \to \text{H}_2\text{O}$ metabolic water), preventing chain backup.
  * `C1.2.17` `[HL-only]` **Lipids vs. carbohydrates:** Lipids yield ~2× energy per gram (less oxygen, more oxidizable C–H bonds); fatty acids break into 2C acetyl-CoA units entering Krebs directly (cannot support glycolysis or anaerobic respiration).

#### `C1.3` Photosynthesis *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `C1.3.1–C1.3.8` `[SL/Core]`; `C1.3.9–C1.3.19` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How is energy from sunlight absorbed and used in photosynthesis? How do abiotic factors interact with photosynthesis?
* **Linking Questions:** What are the consequences of photosynthesis for ecosystems? What are the functions of pigments in living organisms?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `C1.3.1–C1.3.3` `[SL]` **Energy conversion & $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ splitting:** Light energy $\to$ chemical energy in carbon compounds (plants, algae, cyanobacteria). Word equation: $\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light, chlorophyll}} \text{glucose} + \text{O}_2$. Crucial fact: **$\text{O}_2$ originates from splitting $\text{H}_2\text{O}$**, NOT $\text{CO}_2$.
  * `C1.3.4–C1.3.6` `[SL]` **Pigments, chromatography & spectra (`AOS`):** Separate photosynthetic pigments via paper/TLC chromatography and calculate $R_f = \frac{\text{pigment distance}}{\text{solvent front distance}}$. **Absorption spectra** (chlorophylls absorb red ~650–680 nm & blue ~430–450 nm, reflecting green) vs. **action spectra** (plot $\text{O}_2$ evolution / $\text{CO}_2$ uptake vs. wavelength/color).
  * `C1.3.7–C1.3.8` `[SL]` **Limiting factors & $\text{CO}_2$ enrichment (`AOS` + `NOS`):** Investigate light intensity, $\text{CO}_2$ concentration, temperature (*NOS: Hypothesis-driven vs. exploratory testing*). Compare **enclosed greenhouse** vs. **Free-Air $\text{CO}_2$ Enrichment (FACE)** field experiments (*NOS: Variable control in lab vs. ecological realism in field*).
  * `C1.3.9–C1.3.14` `[HL-only]` **Photosystems & Light-Dependent Reactions (Thylakoid membrane):**
    * **Photosystems (PSII & PSI):** Membrane-embedded arrays of chlorophyll + accessory pigments funneling excitation energy to a reaction-centre chlorophyll (single isolated chlorophyll molecules cannot sustain photosynthesis).
    * **Photolysis at PSII:** Splits $\text{H}_2\text{O} \to 2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- + \frac{1}{2}\text{O}_2$ (oxygen released as waste; transformed Earth's atmosphere & banded iron geology).
    * **Non-cyclic photophosphorylation:** Excited $e^-$ from PSII travel down ETC $\to$ pump $\text{H}^+$ into thylakoid lumen $\to$ **chemiosmosis** through ATP synthase makes ATP in stroma $\to$ $e^-$ reach PSI, re-excited by light $\to$ reduce $\text{NADP}^+ + 2e^- + \text{H}^+ \to \text{NADPH}$ (pair terminology consistently: `NADP / reduced NADP` or `NADP+ / NADPH`).
    * **Cyclic photophosphorylation:** Excited $e^-$ from PSI cycle back through ETC to pump $\text{H}^+$ and make extra ATP (no NADPH, no $\text{O}_2$).
  * `C1.3.15–C1.3.19` `[HL-only]` **Calvin Cycle (Stroma) & Interdependence:**
    * **Carbon fixation:** **Rubisco** fixes $\text{CO}_2$ onto **RuBP (5C)** $\to$ unstable 6C $\to$ two **glycerate 3-phosphate (GP, 3C)**. (Rubisco is Earth's most abundant enzyme because it is slow and inefficient at low $\text{CO}_2$).
    * **Reduction:** GP reduced to **triose phosphate (TP, 3C)** consuming ATP + NADPH.
    * **Regeneration:** **5 of every 6 TP molecules** regenerate 3 RuBP using ATP; 1 TP per 3 $\text{CO}_2$ exits toward glucose/carbohydrates, amino acids, lipids (traced back to cycle intermediates).
    * **Interdependence:** Darkness halts ATP/NADPH supply $\to$ stops Calvin cycle; lack of $\text{CO}_2$ stops NADPH/ATP consumption $\to$ depletes $\text{NADP}^+$ $\to$ stalls PSII/ETC.

#### `C2.2` Neural Signaling *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 2 (Cells) | **Scope:** `C2.2.1–C2.2.7` `[SL/Core]`; `C2.2.8–C2.2.16` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How are electrical signals generated and moved within neurons? How can neurons interact with other cells?
* **Linking Questions:** In what ways are biological systems regulated? How is the structure of specialized cells related to function?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `C2.2.1–C2.2.4` `[SL]` **Neuron structure, resting potential & conduction speed (`AOS`):** Cell body (nucleus + cytoplasm), multiple short **dendrites**, single long **axon**. **Resting potential (~$-70\text{ mV}$)** maintained by $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$ pump ($3\text{ Na}^+$ out, $2\text{ K}^+$ in using ATP) + leak $\text{K}^+$ permeability + intracellular negative proteins. Action potential = rapid reversal of membrane polarization via ion movement. Conduction speed rises with **axon diameter** (e.g., squid giant axon vs. small unmyelinated fibres) and **myelination**. *AOS:* Quantify positive/negative trends via Pearson $r$ and coefficient of determination $R^2$ (conduction speed positively correlates with axon diameter, negatively with animal size across scaling comparisons).
  * `C2.2.5–C2.2.7` `[SL]` **Chemical synapses & EPSPs:** Unidirectional signal transfer.   * `C2.2.8–C2.2.11` `[HL-only]` **Action potential mechanism, oscilloscope traces (`AOS`) & saltatory conduction:**

    * $\text{Na}^+$ channels inactivate; voltage-gated $\text{K}^+$ channels open $\to$ $\text{K}^+$ efflux (**repolarization** and brief **hyperpolarization** / refractory period ensuring unidirectional propagation).

Interpret oscilloscope traces (resting, threshold, spike, refractory, impulse frequency in $\text{s}^{-1}$).
  * `C2.2.12–C2.2.16` `[HL-only]` **Exogenous chemicals, IPSPs, summation, pain & consciousness:**

    * **Pain perception:** Free nerve endings in skin express cation channels opened by heat, acid ($\text{H}^+$), or **capsaicin** $\to$ depolarization $\to$ impulses to brain.
    * **Consciousness:** An **emergent property** arising from complex network interactions of billions of individual neurons.

#### `D1.1` DNA Replication *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `D1.1.1–D1.1.5` `[SL/Core]`; `D1.1.6–D1.1.9` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How is new DNA produced? How has knowledge of DNA replication enabled applications in biotechnology?
* **Linking Questions:** How is genetic continuity ensured between generations? What biological mechanisms rely on directionality?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D1.1.1–D1.1.3` `[SL]` **Semi-conservative replication:** Produces exact DNA copies for reproduction, growth, and tissue repair. Each daughter duplex retains one original template strand + one newly synthesized complementary strand (high fidelity via `A=T`, `G≡C` pairing). Core SL enzymes: **helicase** (unwinds double helix, breaks H-bonds) and **DNA polymerase** (synthesizes new strands from free nucleotides).
  * `D1.1.4–D1.1.5` `[SL]` **PCR & Gel Electrophoresis (`NOS`):**
    * **PCR:** Thermal cycling (denaturation ~95°C $\to$ primer annealing ~55°C $\to$ extension ~72°C) using primers and thermostable ***Taq* polymerase** to amplify target DNA exponentially.
    * **Gel electrophoresis:** Separates negatively charged DNA fragments by **size** through agarose gel under electric field (smaller fragments travel faster). Applications: **DNA profiling** for paternity and forensics. *NOS: Increasing number of tandem-repeat markers raises reliability and reduces false-match probability.*
  * `D1.1.6–D1.1.9` `[HL-only]` **Prokaryotic replication fork & proofreading:**
    * **Directionality:** DNA polymerases ONLY add the $5'$ phosphate of an incoming dNTP to the free $3'$ $-\text{OH}$ end (obligate **$5'\to 3'$ synthesis**).
    * **Leading vs. lagging strand:** **Leading strand** synthesized **continuously** toward the replication fork (needs 1 RNA primer); **lagging strand** synthesized **discontinuously** away from fork as **Okazaki fragments** (requires repeated RNA primers).
    * **Prokaryotic enzyme division of labor:** **DNA primase** (synthesizes short RNA primers), **DNA polymerase III** (main $5'\to 3'$ polymerase extending from primers), **DNA polymerase I** (removes RNA primers and replaces with DNA), **DNA ligase** (seals nicks in sugar–phosphate backbone).
    * **DNA proofreading:** DNA polymerase III's $3'\to 5'$ exonuclease activity excises mismatched nucleotides at the $3'$ end and replaces them with the correct match.

#### `D1.2` Protein Synthesis *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 1 (Molecules) | **Scope:** `D1.2.1–D1.2.11` `[SL/Core]`; `D1.2.12–D1.2.19` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How does a cell produce a sequence of amino acids from a sequence of DNA bases? How is the reliability of protein synthesis ensured?
* **Linking Questions:** What biological processes depend on hydrogen bonding? How does the diversity of proteins produced contribute to the functioning of the cell?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D1.2.1–D1.2.4` `[SL]` **Transcription:** **RNA polymerase** binds DNA, unwinds helix locally, and pairs free ribonucleotides to the **template (antisense) strand** (`A` on DNA pairs with **`U`** on RNA). DNA base sequence remains unaltered and conserved across cell life. Transcription is the primary control point turning gene expression on/off.
  * `D1.2.5–D1.2.11` `[SL]` **Translation & Genetic Code:** **mRNA** binds small ribosomal subunit; two **tRNAs** bind large subunit simultaneously via complementary **codon–anticodon** H-bonding. **Triplet code** ($4^3 = 64$ codons for 20 amino acids + 3 stop) is **degenerate** (multiple codons per amino acid) and **universal**. Deduce polypeptide sequences from an mRNA codon table. Focus on ribosome translocation & peptide bond elongation (initiation/termination details are HL). Point mutation example changing protein structure (e.g., sickle-cell $\beta$-globin `GAG` $\to$ `GTG` / Glu $\to$ Val).
  * `D1.2.12–D1.2.16` `[HL-only]` **Directionality, non-coding DNA & RNA processing:**
    * **$5'\to 3'$ transcription & translation:** RNA polymerase synthesizes $5'\to 3'$; ribosome reads mRNA $5'\to 3'$.
    * **Promoter initiation:** **Transcription factors** bind the **promoter** to recruit RNA polymerase (specific factor names NOT required).
    * **Non-coding DNA:** Regulators of gene expression (promoters/enhancers), **introns**, **telomeres**, and **rRNA / tRNA genes**.
    * **Eukaryotic post-transcriptional modification:** Addition of **$5'$ cap** and **$3'$ poly-A tail** (stability/nuclear export) + spliceosome excision of **introns** and joining of **exons**. **Alternative splicing** joins different exon combinations to produce multiple protein isoforms from a single gene.
  * `D1.2.17–D1.2.19` `[HL-only]` **Translation initiation, ribosome sites, post-translational modification & proteasomes:**
    * **Initiation & A/P/E sites:** Small subunit binds $5'$ end of mRNA $\to$ scans to start codon (`AUG`) $\to$ initiator tRNA + second tRNA bind $\to$ large subunit joins. **A** (aminoacyl), **P** (peptidyl), and **E** (exit) sites drive elongation.
    * **Post-translational modification:** Two-stage proteolytic cleavage of **pre-proinsulin $\to$ proinsulin $\to$ active insulin** (signal peptide removal + C-peptide excision leaving A and B chains linked by disulfide bonds).
    * **Proteasomes:** Ubiquitin-tagged damaged/obsolete proteins are degraded by **proteasomes**, recycling amino acids to sustain a dynamic proteome.

#### `D2.1` Cell and Nuclear Division *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*

#### `D2.3` Water Potential *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 2 (Cells) | **Scope:** `D2.3.1–D2.3.7` `[SL/Core]`; `D2.3.8–D2.3.11` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What factors affect the movement of water into or out of cells? How do plant and animal cells differ in their regulation of water movement?
* **Linking Questions:** What are the implications of solubility differences between chemical substances for living organisms? What variables influence the direction of movement of materials in tissues?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D2.3.1–D2.3.4` `[SL]` **Solvation, tonicity & osmometry lab (`AOS`):** Polar water shells surround ions/polar solutes, lowering free water movement. Express osmosis strictly in terms of **solute concentration** (NOT "water concentration") using **hypotonic**, **hypertonic**, and **isotonic** (dynamic equilibrium: equal forward/reverse rates, zero net change). Measure mass/length % change of plant tissue (potato) across serial solute concentrations to deduce **isotonic osmolarity**; analyze using **SD** and **SE error bars** (`AOS`, formulas not memorized).
  * `D2.3.5–D2.3.7` `[SL]` **Animal vs. plant cells & medical isotonicity:** Wall-less cells **swell/lyse** in hypotonic and **shrink/crenate** in hypertonic media (freshwater protists use **contractile vacuoles**; multicellular animals keep tissue fluid isotonic).   * `D2.3.8–D2.3.11` `[HL-only]` **Water potential ($\psi_w$):** Potential energy of water per unit volume relative to pure water at atmospheric pressure & 20°C ($\psi_{w,\text{pure}} = 0\text{ kPa}$). Water always moves from **higher (less negative) $\psi_w$ to lower (more negative) $\psi_w$**.

    * **Solute potential ($\psi_s$):** Always $\le 0\text{ kPa}$ (adding solute makes $\psi_s$ more negative).
    * **Pressure potential ($\psi_p$):** Usually $\ge 0\text{ kPa}$ inside turgid plant cells ($\psi_p = 0$ at incipient plasmolysis), **negative** in xylem vessels under transpirational tension.

    * **Unlinked dihybrid crosses:** Independent assortment yields **9:3:3:1** (dihybrid self) and **1:1:1:1** (test cross). *NOS: Mendel's second law has exceptions when genes are linked on the same chromosome.*
    * **Autosomal gene linkage (`AOS`):** Locate gene loci in genomic databases; write linked genotypes alongside vertical homologous chromosome bars (`AB // ab`). Explain **recombinant** gametes/offspring via crossing over (<50% recombinants indicates linkage).
    * **$\chi^2$ goodness-of-fit test (`NOS`):** Compare observed F2/test-cross counts to Mendelian expected ratios ($df = k - 1$, $p = 0.05$). *NOS: F2 sample represents a statistical sample of a theoretical population.*

#### `D3.3` Homeostasis *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 3 (Organisms) | **Scope:** `D3.3.1–D3.3.6` `[SL/Core]`; `D3.3.7–D3.3.11` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** How are constant internal conditions maintained in humans? What are the benefits to organisms of maintaining constant internal conditions?
* **Linking Questions:** For what reasons do organisms need to distribute materials and energy? What biological systems are sensitive to temperature changes?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D3.3.1–D3.3.4` `[SL]` **Negative feedback & blood glucose:** Maintain core temperature, blood pH, blood glucose, and blood osmolarity within narrow preset limits via **negative feedback** (counteracts deviations both above and below set point). **Insulin** ($\beta$-cells of pancreatic islets: stimulates liver/muscle glucose uptake & glycogenesis) vs. **glucagon** ($\alpha$-cells: stimulates glycogenolysis/gluconeogenesis). **Type 1 diabetes** (autoimmune $\beta$-cell destruction, insulin deficiency, insulin injections) vs. **Type 2 diabetes** (target-cell insulin resistance, obesity/sedentary/genetic risk factors, diet/exercise/medication management).
  * `D3.3.5–D3.3.6` `[SL]` **Thermoregulation:** Peripheral & hypothalamic thermoreceptors $\to$ hypothalamus + pituitary $\to$ **thyroxin** (basal metabolic rate) + muscle & adipose effectors. Mechanisms: **vasodilation**, **vasoconstriction**, **shivering**, **sweating** (latent heat), **uncoupled respiration in brown adipose tissue** (non-shivering thermogenesis via UCP1/thermogenin), **hair erection** (piloerection).
  * `D3.3.7–D3.3.11` `[HL-only]` **Kidney, Osmoregulation & Organ Blood Flow:**
    * Distinguish **excretion** (removal of metabolic waste e.g. urea) from **osmoregulation** (regulating osmotic concentration in $\text{osmol L}^{-1}$).
    * **Ultrafiltration** (glomerulus $\to$ Bowman's capsule) & **selective reabsorption** (PCT reabsorbs all glucose/amino acids via SGLT/active transport, most ions/water).
    * **Loop of Henle:** Active $\text{Na}^+$ transport out of ascending limb builds steep hyperosmotic medullary gradient, enabling osmotic water reabsorption from collecting ducts.
    * **ADH & aquaporins:** Hypothalamic **osmoreceptors** detect high blood osmolarity $\to$ posterior pituitary releases **ADH** $\to$ triggers vesicle fusion inserting **aquaporins** into apical collecting duct membranes $\to$ concentrated urine (and removal of aquaporins via endocytosis when hydrated).
    * **Regional blood flow shifts:** Compare blood supply distribution to **skeletal muscle, gut, brain, and kidneys** across **sleep, wakeful rest, and vigorous exercise** (brain stays nearly constant; muscle surges massively during exercise while gut/kidneys vasoconstrict).

#### `D4.1` Natural Selection *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 4 (Ecosystems) | **Scope:** `D4.1.1–D4.1.8` `[SL/Core]`; `D4.1.9–D4.1.15` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What processes can cause changes in allele frequencies within a population? What is the role of reproduction in the process of natural selection?
* **Linking Questions:** How do intraspecific interactions differ from interspecific interactions? What mechanisms minimize competition?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D4.1.1–D4.1.6` `[SL]` **Mechanism of natural selection (`NOS`):** Mutation creates new alleles; sexual reproduction recombines alleles $\to$ overproduction of offspring + resource competition & **density-independent abiotic selection pressures** $\to$ differential survival & reproductive **fitness** of **heritable** traits (acquired traits excluded). *NOS: Darwin's natural selection replacing Lamarckism exemplifies a **paradigm shift**.*
  * `D4.1.7–D4.1.8` `[SL]` **Sexual selection & Endler's guppies (`AOS`):** Mate choice favors fitness-signaling traits (e.g., **birds-of-paradise** plumage). Interpret **John Endler's guppy (*Poecilia reticulata*) experiments** (`AOS`): high predation selects camouflaged drab males; low predation shifts equilibrium toward bright sexual-selected males.
  * `D4.1.9–D4.1.15` `[HL-only]` **Gene pools, selection modes & Hardy–Weinberg:**
    * **Gene pool** = all alleles in population; **neo-Darwinism** integrates Mendelian genetics with natural selection. Search human allele frequency databases across geographically isolated populations (`AOS`).
    * **3 Selection patterns:** **Directional** (shifts mean), **stabilizing** (narrows variance around mean), **disruptive** (favors both extremes $\to$ bimodal).
    * **Hardy–Weinberg equations:** $p + q = 1$ and $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$ ($p^2 = AA$, $2pq = Aa$, $q^2 = aa$). **5 Equilibrium conditions:** large/infinite population (no genetic drift), random mating, no mutation, no migration/gene flow, no natural selection. Deviation indicates evolution/selection/non-random mating.
    * **Artificial vs. natural selection:** Deliberate selective breeding of crops/livestock = artificial selection; **unintended antibiotic resistance** from human drug use = **natural selection**!

#### `D4.2` Stability and Change *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 4 (Ecosystems) | **Scope:** `D4.2.1–D4.2.11` `[SL/Core]`; `D4.2.12–D4.2.15` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What features of ecosystems allow stability over unlimited time periods? What changes caused by humans threaten the stability of ecosystems?
* **Linking Questions:** What is the distinction between artificial and natural processes? Over what timescales do things change in different biological systems?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D4.2.1–D4.2.5` `[SL]` **Stability, tipping points, mesocosms (`NOS` + `AOS`):** Long-term persistence requires continuous energy supply, nutrient cycling, genetic diversity, and climate within tolerance. **Amazon rainforest deforestation tipping point:** Large forest area generates transpired atmospheric water vapor driving regional rainfall; cutting below uncertain threshold risks irreversible savanna transition (calculate % deforestation change, `AOS`). **Sealed glass aquatic/microbial mesocosms** isolate matter while allowing energy flow (*NOS: Adhere to IB experimental ethics*). **Keystone species** exert disproportionate community-structuring influence (removal risks trophic cascade/collapse).
  * `D4.2.6–D4.2.10` `[SL]` **Sustainability, eutrophication, biomagnification & plastics (`NOS`):**
    * **Renewable harvesting:** Rate of harvest < rate of replacement (study 1 terrestrial plant + 1 marine fish). Sustainable agriculture balances soil erosion, leaching, fertilizer inputs, agrochemicals, carbon footprint.
    * **Eutrophication:** N/P fertilizer leaching $\to$ algal bloom $\to$ light blocked $\to$ plant death $\to$ aerobic bacterial decomposition spikes **biochemical oxygen demand (BOD)** $\to$ hypoxia/fish kills.
    * **Biomagnification:** Non-biodegradable lipophilic toxins (**DDT**, **mercury**) concentrate exponentially up trophic levels.
    * **Marine plastics:** Non-biodegradable **macroplastics** (entanglement/ingestion) and **microplastics** (*NOS: Media dissemination of scientific findings shifted global public perception and policy*).
  * `D4.2.11` `[SL]` **Rewilding:** Restoring apex predators/keystone species, habitat corridors, and reducing human interference — required example: **Hinewai Reserve (New Zealand)** (gorse used as nurse canopy to regenerate native forest).
  * `D4.2.12–D4.2.15` `[HL-only]` **Ecological succession:** Triggered by abiotic and biotic changes. **Primary succession:** Pioneer colonization on bare substrate $\to$ progressive rises in plant height/biomass, primary production, species diversity, food web complexity, and nutrient cycling toward **climax community**. **Cyclical succession** alternates naturally without one static endpoint. **Arrested succession** caused by **livestock grazing** or **wetland drainage**.

#### `D4.3` Climate Change *(SL/Core + HL Extension)*
* **Level:** 4 (Ecosystems) | **Scope:** `D4.3.1–D4.3.8` `[SL/Core]`; `D4.3.9–D4.3.12` `[HL-only]`
* **Guiding Questions:** What are the drivers of climate change? What are the impacts of climate change on ecosystems?
* **Linking Questions:** What are the impacts of climate change at each level of biological organization? What processes determine the distribution of organisms on Earth?
* **Core Content & Statements:**
  * `D4.3.1–D4.3.2` `[SL]` **Anthropogenic drivers & positive feedbacks (`NOS`):** Limit anthropogenic greenhouse gases to **$\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{CH}_4$**. *NOS: Antarctic ice cores show strong positive correlation between $\text{CO}_2$ and temperature over millennia — correlation alone does NOT prove causation, though radiative physics confirms causality.* **5 Positive feedback loops:** (1) warmer oceans release dissolved $\text{CO}_2$, (2) melting snow/ice lowers albedo $\to$ more solar absorption, (3) permafrost thaw accelerates peat decomposition ($\text{CO}_2$), (4) permafrost releases trapped $\text{CH}_4$, (5) drought & wildfires release legacy carbon.
  * `D4.3.3–D4.3.7` `[SL]` **Ecosystem tipping points & habitat impacts:**
    * **Boreal forests (taiga) tipping point:** Warming + reduced winter snow $\to$ summer drought $\to$ reduced primary production, forest browning, intense fires burning legacy peat/soil carbon $\to$ switch from net sink to net carbon source.
    * **Polar ice loss:** Early breakup of Antarctic **landfast ice** threatens **emperor penguin (*Aptenodytes forsteri*)** breeding; Arctic **sea ice** loss threatens **walrus** haul-out/foraging habitat.
    * **Ocean stratification:** Warmer surface layers suppress **nutrient upwelling**, cutting marine primary production.
    * **Poleward & upslope range shifts:** Upslope shift in **New Guinea tropical montane birds**; range contraction / northward shift in **North American trees**.
    * **Coral reef collapse:** Rising $\text{CO}_2$ dissolves forming carbonic acid $\to$ **ocean acidification** (lowers $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$, suppressing coral **calcification**); warming causes expulsion of zooxanthellae (**coral bleaching**).
  * `D4.3.8` `[SL]` **Carbon sequestration (`NOS`):** **Afforestation**, **forest regeneration**, and **peatland restoration** (waterlogged anaerobic soils accumulate peat in boreal/temperate and tropical peatlands). *NOS: Active scientific debate over monoculture non-native tree plantations vs. native rewilding.*
  * `D4.3.9–D4.3.12` `[HL-only]` **Phenology, trophic mismatch, pest voltinism & rapid evolution:**
    * **Phenology:** Timing of seasonal events cued by **photoperiod** (invariant day length) vs. **temperature** (warming earlier).
    * **Phenological mismatch (2 required pairs):**
      1. Temperature-cued spring growth of **Arctic mouse-ear chickweed (*Cerastium arcticum*)** advances earlier, out of sync with photoperiod-cued migration of **reindeer (*Rangifer tarandus*)**.
      2. Earlier temperature-cued **peak caterpillar biomass** in European forests misses optimal timing for **great tit (*Parus major*)** chick rearing.
    * **Increased insect generations/year:** **Spruce bark beetle (*Ips typographus* / *Dendroctonus micans*)** completes extra life cycles per warmer year, driving tree mortality.
    * **Climate-driven evolution:** Reduced snow cover selects **brown plumage over pale grey plumage** in **tawny owl (*Strix aluco*)** due to higher camouflage survival.

| **High-Power Microscope FOV** | $\text{FOV}_{\text{HP}} = \text{FOV}_{\text{LP}} \times \frac{\text{Total Mag}_{\text{LP}}}{\text{Total Mag}_{\text{HP}}}$ | Inverse proportionality; Total Mag = Ocular $\times$ Objective. |
| **SA:V Ratio** | Cube: $\frac{6s^2}{s^3} = \frac{6}{s}$ \| Sphere: $\frac{4\pi r^2}{\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3} = \frac{3}{r}$ | Express simplified to $:1$ (e.g., `6:1`). |
| **Percentage Change** | $\%\Delta = \frac{\text{Final} - \text{Initial}}{\text{Initial}} \times 100\%$ | Use when tracking change from a clear baseline/initial state (`D2.3`, `C3.2`, `D4.2`). |
| **Percentage Difference** | $\%\text{Diff} = \frac{\|A - B\|}{(A + B)/2} \times 100\%$ *(or $\frac{A-B}{\text{ref}}\times 100\%$)* | Use when comparing two peer values without a directional before/after (`C3.2.18`). |
| **Chromatography $R_f$** | $R_f = \frac{d_{\text{pigment}}}{d_{\text{solvent front}}}$ | Always $0 \le R_f \le 1$; unitless (`C1.3.4`). |
| **Stomatal Density** | $\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Number of stomata}}{\text{Area of FOV }\left(\pi r^2\right)}$ | Units: $\text{stomata mm}^{-2}$ (`B3.1.10`). |
| **Rate of Reaction / Process** | $\text{Rate} = \frac{\Delta \text{DV}}{\Delta t}$ or $\frac{1}{t}$ | Units match DV per unit time (`C1.1.9`, `C1.2.6`). |
| **Cardiac Output** | $\text{CO} = \text{Heart Rate} \times \text{Stroke Volume}$ | $\text{mL min}^{-1}$ or $\text{L min}^{-1}$ (`C3.1.14`). |
| **Mitotic Index (`HL`)** | $\text{MI} = \frac{\text{Cells in mitosis (P+M+A+T)}}{\text{Total cells counted}}$ | Unitless decimal (or $\times 100\%$) (`D2.1.17`). |
| **Water Potential (`HL`)** | $\psi_w = \psi_s + \psi_p$ | Units: $\text{kPa}$; pure water $\psi_w = 0$; $\psi_s \le 0$ (`D2.3.10`). |
| **Lincoln Index** | $N_{\text{pop}} = \frac{M \times N}{R}$ | $M$ = marked initially, $N$ = total recaptured, $R$ = marked recaptured (`C4.1.4`). |
| **Simpson's Reciprocal Index** | $D = \frac{N(N - 1)}{\sum n(n - 1)}$ | $N$ = total individuals all species; $n$ = individuals of species $i$. Min $D=1$; max $D = S$ (species richness) (`Tool 3`). |
| **Hardy–Weinberg (`HL`)** | $p + q = 1$ and $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$ | Start from homozygous recessive phenotype frequency $q^2 \to q = \sqrt{q^2} \to p = 1 - q$ (`D4.1.13`). |
| **Chi-Squared ($\chi^2$)** | $\chi^2 = \sum \frac{(O - E)^2}{E}$ | **Counts only** (never %, means, or densities); all $E \ge 5$, $n \ge 20$. GOF $df = k - 1$; Independence $df = (r-1)(c-1)$. |
| **Standard Error of Mean** | $\text{SEM} = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}$ | Precision of sample mean estimate; most reliable when $n \ge 30$ (Central Limit Theorem). |
| **Outlier Threshold (IQR)** | $< Q_1 - 1.5(\text{IQR})$ or $> Q_3 + 1.5(\text{IQR})$ | $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$ (`D3.2.15`). |
| **Percentage Uncertainty** | $\%\text{Uncertainty} = \frac{\text{Absolute Uncertainty}}{\text{Measured Value}} \times 100\%$ | Analog half-division ($\pm \frac{1}{2}$ smallest scale) or digital ($\pm 1$ last digit); double if reading two endpoints (e.g., ruler/burette). |

#### Inferential Statistical Test Selection Decision Tree
1. **Categorical Frequency / Count Data?** (NEVER percentages/means)
   * Compare observed counts vs. theoretical expected ratios (e.g., 9:3:3:1 dihybrid or HW) $\to$ **$\chi^2$ Goodness-of-Fit** ($df = \text{categories} - 1$).
   * Test association between two categorical variables (e.g., species A presence/absence vs. species B in quadrats) $\to$ **$\chi^2$ Test of Independence** ($E = \frac{\text{row total} \times \text{col total}}{\text{grand total}}$, $df = (r-1)(c-1)$).
2. **Continuous Measurement Data — Comparing Groups?**
   * **2 groups (normal distribution):** **Student's $t$-test** (two-tailed unless directional justified; **unpaired** for independent control vs. treatment; **paired** for before/after same subjects; **one-sample** vs. known population value).
   * **3+ groups (normal distribution):** **One-way ANOVA** $\to$ if $p < 0.05$, follow with **Tukey's HSD post-hoc** pairwise test.
   * **3+ groups (skewed / non-parametric):** **Kruskal–Wallis test**.
3. **Continuous Variables — Testing Relationship / Trend?**
   * **Linear relationship (parametric):** **Pearson's correlation coefficient ($r \in [-1, +1]$)** and **Coefficient of determination ($R^2 \in [0, 1]$)** (% variance in DV explained by IV).
   * **Monotonic non-linear / ordinal:** **Spearman's rank correlation ($r_s$)**.
4. **Significance Rule:** Standard $\alpha = 0.05$. If **$p \le 0.05$** (or $\chi^2_{\text{calc}} > \chi^2_{\text{crit}}$), **reject $H_0$** and accept $H_1$ (statistically significant difference/association — unlikely due to chance alone). If **$p > 0.05$**, **fail to reject $H_0$** (not significant). **CRITICAL TRAP:** Never claim overlapping SD error bars *prove* non-significance or non-overlapping bars *prove* significance — error bars only give visual clues; formal statistical testing is mandatory!

#### Core Lab Protocols & Biochemical Tests
* **Serial Dilutions:** Transfer fixed aliquot $V_t$ into diluent volume $V_d$ iteratively (e.g., 1 mL into 9 mL = $10^{-1}$ dilution per step) for calibration standard curves in colorimetry/spectrophotometry, osmosis osmolarity series, or CFU microbial counts.
* **Colorimetry / Spectrophotometry:** Beer–Lambert principle; always **zero/blank** with pure solvent/reagent blank at complementary wavelength filter before measuring absorbance ($A$) or transmittance ($\%T$).
* **Eyepiece Graticule Calibration:** Align stage micrometer (known $\mu\text{m}$ divisions) with arbitrary eyepiece graticule units at the **exact objective lens magnification** being used; recalibrate whenever objective changes.
* **Biochemical Identification Tests (Practical Work):**
  * *Reducing sugars (glucose):* **Benedict's reagent** + heat $\ge 80^\circ\text{C}$ water bath: Blue $\to$ Green $\to$ Yellow $\to$ Orange $\to$ Brick-red precipitate.
  * *Starch:* **Iodine in potassium iodide ($\text{I}_2/\text{KI}$)**: Orange-brown $\to$ Blue-black.
  * *Protein (peptide bonds):* **Biuret test** ($\text{NaOH} + \text{dilute CuSO}_4$): Blue $\to$ Violet/Lilac/Purple.
  * *Lipids:* **Ethanol emulsion test**: Dissolve in ethanol, pour into cold water $\to$ Cloudy white emulsion.
* **Potometer & Respirometer Key Controls:** Cut plant stem **underwater** at an angle (prevent xylem air embolism); seal joints airtight with petroleum jelly; respirometer uses **KOH / soda lime** to absorb evolved $\text{CO}_2$ so manometer fluid movement measures $\text{O}_2$ consumption directly, with a **thermobarometer** control tube correcting ambient temperature/pressure drift.
* **Graphing Standards Checklist:** Descriptive numbered title naming organism (binomial) + both variables; IV on $x$-axis, DV on $y$-axis; axis labels = `Variable (Unit)`; uniform linear scale filling $\ge 50\%$–$75\%$ of plot area (origin `(0,0)` NOT required unless extrapolating intercept); **scatter plot** with smooth **best-fit line/curve** for continuous IV (NEVER connect-the-dots), **bar chart** for discrete nominal categories, **histogram** for continuous frequency bins, **box-and-whisker** for quartiles + $1.5\times\text{IQR}$ outliers, **semi-log** for exponential population growth; error bars explicitly labeled in caption (Range for $n < 5$; $\pm 1\text{ SD}$ for spread when $n \ge 5$; $\pm 1\text{ SEM}$ for mean precision when $n \ge 30$).

### 4.2 Paper 1B Experimental & Data-Analysis Question Archetypes
Paper 1B (HL: 4 multi-part questions, 35 marks; SL: 4 questions, 25 marks) focuses exclusively on unfamiliar contexts, practical techniques, and data interpretation:

1. **Unfamiliar Protocol Critique & Variable Audit (`Tool 1` + `Inquiry 1/3`)**

   * *Typical Prompts:* Identify IV/DV; state 2 controlled variables and *how* to control them; identify a systematic error (e.g., temperature drift without thermobarometer, air bubble in xylem, unblanked colorimeter); suggest a realistic modification.
2. **Quantitative Processing, Error Bars & Inferential Verdict (`Tool 3` + `Inquiry 2`)**
   * *Stimulus:* Raw/processed table or multi-series graph with SD/SEM error bars, $r$/$R^2$, or statistical test output ($t$-test, $\chi^2$, ANOVA+Tukey).
   * *Typical Prompts:* Calculate rate / % change / % difference / $R_f$ / magnification / Lincoln index / $\psi_w$; describe trend quoting specific $x,y$ data with units; evaluate strength of relationship ($R^2$) or whether error bars/p-values support rejecting $H_0$; distinguish correlation from causation (`B3.2.6`, `D3.1.20`, `D4.3.1`).
3. **Microscopy, Micrometry & Biological Drawing Audit (`A2.2`, `B3.1`, `B3.2`, `D2.1`)**
   * *Stimulus:* Light micrograph or TEM/SEM with scale bar or eyepiece graticule calibration, or student plan diagram with errors.
   * *Typical Prompts:* Calculate actual size or magnification; calculate stomatal density ($\text{mm}^{-2}$) or mitotic index; identify cell/organelle/vessel type (prokaryote vs. plant vs. animal; artery vs. vein); diagnose drawing errors (shading present, individual cells drawn in a tissue plan).
4. **Ecological Field Sampling & Population Modeling (`B4.1`, `C4.1`, `D4.1`, `D4.2`)**
   * *Stimulus:* Belt transect + abiotic sensor table, $2\times 2$ quadrat species presence matrix, or semi-log population curve / capture-recapture data.
   * *Typical Prompts:* Compute Lincoln index and state 2 violated assumptions; compute or interpret $\chi^2$ association against critical value at $p=0.05$; interpret box-and-whisker outlier thresholds ($1.5\times\text{IQR}$).

### 4.3 Paper 2 Section B Extended-Response Essay Archetypes (16 Marks Each)
At HL, candidates answer **2 of 3** extended-response questions (32 marks total; SL chooses **1 of 2**, 16 marks). Each 16-mark question comprises **15 content marks** across subparts `(a), (b), (c)` (markschemes provide ~1.5× as many valid bullet points, ~22–23 points, capped at 15) plus **1 quality mark** (`Q`).

1. **Sequential Mechanistic Chain (`Explain how...` — 6 to 8 marks)**
   * *Examples:* Action potential propagation & synaptic transmission (`C2.2`); chemiosmosis in oxidative phosphorylation (`C1.2`) or photophosphorylation (`C1.3`); hormonal regulation of menstrual cycle (`D3.1`); ADH aquaporin osmoregulation (`D3.3`); transcription + eukaryotic splicing + translation (`D1.2`).
   * *Grading Rule:* Rewards strict step-by-step causality (stimulus $\to$ channel/enzyme/hormone $\to$ intermediate $\to$ cellular consequence) without skipped links.
2. **Structured Comparison (`Compare` / `Contrast` / `Compare and contrast` — 5 to 7 marks)**
   * *Examples:* Prokaryotes vs. eukaryotes (`A2.2`); DNA vs. RNA (`A1.2`); spermatogenesis vs. oogenesis (`D3.1`); competitive vs. non-competitive inhibition (`C1.1`); mitosis vs. meiosis (`D2.1`); arteries vs. veins (`B3.2`); cyclic vs. non-cyclic photophosphorylation (`C1.3`).
   * *Grading Rule:* **Every point must reference BOTH items explicitly** (`whereas`, `while`, `both...`); half-statements naming only one item receive 0 marks.
3. **Structure–Function Integration (`Outline/Explain how the structure of [X] relates to its function` — 6 to 8 marks)**
   * *Examples:* Mitochondrion/chloroplast compartmentalization (`B2.2`); alveoli & pneumocytes (`B2.3`/`B3.1`); mammalian heart & double circulation (`B3.2`); xylem & phloem sieve tubes/companion cells (`B3.2`); sarcomere & titin (`B3.3`); nephron & Loop of Henle (`D3.3`).
   * *Grading Rule:* Requires `Structure Feature` $\to$ `Biophysical/Biochemical Mechanism` $\to$ `Physiological Advantage`.
4. **Balanced Evaluation / Discussion (`Discuss` / `Evaluate` — 5 to 8 marks)**
   * *Examples:* Endosymbiotic theory evidence (`A2.2`); Miller–Urey prebiotic evidence (`A2.1`); conservation *in situ* vs. *ex situ* / EDGE (`A4.2`); biological vs. morphological species concept (`A3.1`); CRISPR germline ethics (`D1.3`); epidemiological correlation vs. RCT evidence (`D3.1.20`).
   * *Grading Rule:* Answers giving only supporting arguments or only limitations are capped; requires balanced weighing + concluding judgment.
5. **The 16th Mark (Quality of Construction — `1 mark`):**
   * Awarded when the response is clear, logically sequenced, concise, free of self-contradictions (`con`), uses precise biological terminology, and stays strictly on-topic without irrelevant padding.

### 4.4 Common Markscheme Pitfalls & Terminology Traps (App "Mistakes Log")
Simple Revision's AI grader and Mistakes Log must automatically flag and penalize these high-frequency IB examiner traps:

| Topic Area | Student Error / Forbidden Phrasing | Why Examiners Penalize It | Required Markscheme Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bioenergetics** (`C1.2`, `C1.3`, `C4.2`) | *"Energy is created / produced / destroyed"* or *"respiration makes energy"* | Violates conservation of energy (First Law of Thermodynamics). | Energy is **released** from organic carbon compounds / **transferred** to ATP / light energy is **converted** to chemical energy. |
| **Enzymes & Proteins** (`B1.2`, `C1.1`) | *"The enzyme was killed / died / destroyed by heat"* | Enzymes are non-living molecules; peptide bonds stay intact during denaturation. | Enzyme is **denatured**: non-covalent H/ionic bonds break $\to$ 3D tertiary structure & **active site shape change** $\to$ substrate no longer fits/binds. |
| **Enzyme Collisions** (`C1.1`) | *"The substrate is attracted to the active site"* | Implies magnetic/conscious pull rather than Brownian kinetics. | Substrate and active site collide via **random thermal molecular motion** and bind due to **complementary shape and charge**. |
| **Osmosis & Water Potential** (`B2.1`, `D2.3`) | *"Water moves from high concentration to low concentration"* | Ambiguous ("concentration" means solute concentration, which goes low $\to$ high!). | **Net movement** of water across a **partially permeable membrane** from **lower solute concentration (hypotonic / higher $\psi_w$)** to **higher solute concentration (hypertonic / lower $\psi_w$)**. |
| **Osmosis at Isotonic** (`D2.3`) | *"No water moves in isotonic solutions"* | Confuses zero *net* flow with molecular stasis. | **Dynamic equilibrium**: water continues moving in both directions at **equal rates** (zero net movement). |
| **Teleological Language** (All Topics) | *"The plant wants light"*, *"bacteria mutate to survive antibiotics"*, *"cells try to balance"* | Attributes intent/purpose; mutations are random (`D1.3.5`). | Random mutation pre-exists; selection pressure increases survival/reproduction of resistant phenotypes (**natural selection**, NOT artificial/intentional). |
| **Cell Division** (`D2.1`) | *"Mitosis is cell division"* | Mitosis is strictly nuclear division. | **Mitosis** = division of the **nucleus** into two genetically identical nuclei; **cytokinesis** = division of cytoplasm/cell. |
| **Compare / Contrast** (Paper 2B) | Writing two separate paragraphs (*"Prokaryotes have..."* then *"Eukaryotes have..."*) | Unpaired statements fail IB comparative marking rules. | Pair every point explicitly (*"Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes **whereas** eukaryotes have 80S cytoplasmic ribosomes"*). |
| **Correlation vs. Causation** (`B3.2`, `D3.1`, `D4.3`) | *"The high $R^2$ proves X causes Y"* | Observational correlation never proves causation (`NOS`). | *"Shows a strong positive correlation; further controlled/mechanistic evidence is needed to establish causation."* |
| **Error Bars & Stats** (`Tool 3`, IA) | *"Overlapping SD error bars prove no significant difference"* | SD does not test significance or account for sample size $n$. | Overlapping error bars suggest difference *may* not be significant, **but an inferential statistical test ($t$-test/ANOVA) is required** to accept/reject $H_0$. |
| **Vague Pronouns / Missing Units** (Paper 1B, 2A) | *"Stuff/particles move through it"*, quoting `"from 20 to 45"` without units | Examiners ignore vague nouns and withhold data-quote marks without units. | Name specific ion/molecule + transport mechanism ($\text{Na}^+$ via voltage-gated channel); quote `"from 20°C to 45°C"`. |
| **Photosynthesis $\text{O}_2$ Source** (`C1.3`) | *"$\text{O}_2$ comes from splitting $\text{CO}_2$"* | Factually false; $\text{CO}_2$ oxygen goes to carbohydrates/water. | $\text{O}_2$ comes exclusively from **photolysis of water** at Photosystem II. |
| **Nerve Impulse Chemistry** (`C2.2`) | *"Electrons flow down the axon"* | Neurons conduct ionic currents, NOT metallic electron flow. | Nerve impulses propagate via **movement of positively charged ions ($\text{Na}^+$, $\text{K}^+$)** across the axon membrane. |

| **5–6** | • The research question is **described within a specific and appropriate context**.<br>• Methodological considerations associated with collecting relevant and sufficient data to answer the RQ are **explained**.<br>• Description of methodology allows the investigation to be **reproduced** (unambiguously). |

#### Criterion B: Data Analysis (0–6 Marks)
*Assesses evidence that the student recorded, processed, and presented data in ways relevant to the research question.*

| Band | Official Level Descriptors |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0** | Does not reach the standard described below. |
| **1–2** | • Recording and processing of data is communicated but is **neither clear nor precise**.<br>• Shows **limited evidence** of consideration of uncertainties.<br>• Some processing relevant to RQ is carried out with **major omissions, inaccuracies or inconsistencies** *(impedes drawing a valid conclusion)*. |
| **3–4** | • Communication of recording and processing is **either clear or precise**.<br>• Shows evidence of consideration of uncertainties but with **some significant omissions or inaccuracies**.<br>• Processing relevant to RQ is carried out with **some significant omissions, inaccuracies or inconsistencies** *(allows conclusion but limits validity/detail)*. |
| **5–6** | • Communication of recording and processing is **both clear and precise**.<br>• Shows evidence of **appropriate consideration of uncertainties**.<br>• Processing relevant to RQ is carried out **appropriately and accurately**. |

#### Criterion C: Conclusion (0–6 Marks)
*Assesses how successfully the student answers their research question with regard to their analysis and accepted scientific context.*

| Band | Official Level Descriptors |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0** | Does not reach the standard described below. |
| **1–2** | • A conclusion is **stated** that is relevant to the RQ **but is not supported by the analysis presented**.<br>• Makes **superficial comparison** to the accepted scientific context. |
| **3–4** | • A conclusion is **described** that is relevant to the RQ **but is not fully consistent with the analysis presented**.<br>• Makes **some relevant comparison** to the accepted scientific context. |
| **5–6** | • A conclusion is **justified** that is relevant to the RQ and **fully consistent with the analysis presented**.<br>• Justified through **relevant comparison to the accepted scientific context** (with traceable citations). |

#### Criterion D: Evaluation (0–6 Marks)
*Assesses evaluation of the investigation methodology and suggested realistic improvements.*

| Band | Official Level Descriptors |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0** | Does not reach the standard described below. |
| **1–2** | • Reports **states generic** methodological weaknesses or limitations.<br>• Realistic improvements are **stated**. |
| **3–4** | • Reports **describes specific** methodological weaknesses or limitations.<br>• Realistic improvements relevant to identified weaknesses/limitations are **described**. |
| **5–6** | • Reports **explains the relative impact** of specific methodological weaknesses or limitations.<br>• Realistic improvements relevant to identified weaknesses/limitations are **explained**. |

### 5.3 AI Essay / IA Studio Automated Audit Rules
Simple Revision's IA Studio should run a structured pre-submission diagnostic auditing every `5–6` band failure mode:

1. **Research Design Audit (`Criterion A`):**
   * **Focused RQ Check:** Verifies presence of exact IV (with units/range), DV (with units/measurement proxy), and organism **scientific binomial** (*Genus species* italicized).
   * **Justification Check:** Detects mere *listing* of controlled variables vs. *explaining* why each variable biologically affects the DV + **explicit method** to keep it constant/monitored (distinguish **Controlled Variables** from **Validity Measures**).
   * **Sufficiency & Reproducibility Check:** Flags `< 5` IV levels or `< 5` trials per level (minimum 5×5 rule unless ecological/database constraint justified); flags vague apparatus ("beaker", "thermometer") lacking capacity, graduation, and precision ($\pm$ uncertainty); checks preliminary/pilot trial mention, negative/positive controls, and **safety + ethics + environmental disposal** (human consent / IBO animal welfare compliance).
2. **Data Analysis Audit (`Criterion B`):**
   * **Raw vs. Processed Separation:** Checks that raw quantitative measurements and qualitative observations sit separately from processed means/SDs/rates, with **worked sample calculations**.
   * **Precision & Uncertainty Check:** Audits consistent decimal places matching instrument uncertainty across columns, unit notation in headers (`Variable (unit)`), explicit instrument $\pm$ uncertainty + percentage uncertainty evaluation, and **outlier rule justification** ($1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ + qualitative evidence — never silently deleting data!).
   * **Graph & Statistical Test Audit:** Verifies IV on $x$-axis, DV on $y$-axis, appropriate chart type (scatter + smooth best-fit for continuous vs. bar for categorical), explicit error-bar legend (`SD` / `SEM` / `Range`), and valid **inferential test** ($t$-test, ANOVA+Tukey, $\chi^2$ on *raw counts only*, Pearson $r$ / Spearman $r_s$) stating $H_0$, $H_1$, $\alpha = 0.05$, test statistic, $df$, and exact $p$-value.
3. **Conclusion Audit (`Criterion C`):**
   * **Consistency & Calibration Check:** Flags overclaiming words (**"proves"**, **"definite"**) and checks whether conclusion explicitly cites processed numeric values, error bars, $R^2$, and $p$-value without contradicting Criterion B.
   * **Literature Comparison Check:** Checks that findings are compared against **cited, traceable published values/literature** (not generic textbook mentions), explaining mechanistic reasons for agreement or divergence.
4. **Evaluation Audit (`Criterion D`):**
   * **Anti-Generic Filter:** Flags weak/generic excuses (*"human error"*, *"reaction time"*, *"equipment might be dirty"*, *"more time/money"*).
   * **Impact Direction & Relative Magnitude Check:** Verifies each weakness explains *how* (systematic bias direction or random variance inflation) and *how much* (relative impact compared to other errors) it distorted the conclusion, paired with a **realistic, school-lab-achievable improvement** directly targeting that mechanism.
