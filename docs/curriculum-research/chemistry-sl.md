## 1. Course Overview & 2025 SL Assessment Blueprint

### STRAND 1: STRUCTURE

#### Structure 1. Models of the particulate nature of matter (17 Hours SL)

##### `S1.1` — Structure 1.1: Introduction to the particulate nature of matter
- **Guiding Question:** *How can we model the particulate nature of matter?*
- **SL Hours:** 2 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 0 hours (`None for Structure 1.1`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S1.1.1` Elements, compounds, and mixtures:** Elements cannot be chemically broken down into simpler substances; compounds contain atoms of different elements chemically bonded in a fixed ratio; mixtures contain >1 element or compound in no fixed ratio, not chemically bonded, separable by physical methods.
    - *Outcomes:* Distinguish properties of elements, compounds, and mixtures; understand homogeneous vs. heterogeneous mixtures; cover separation techniques: **solvation, filtration, recrystallization, evaporation, distillation, and chromatography** (note May 2023 guide update: *"paper"* was removed before chromatography so both paper and TLC/other methods apply).
  - **`S1.1.2` Kinetic molecular theory & states of matter:** Explains physical properties of solids, liquids, and gases and changes of state.
    - *Outcomes:* Distinguish `(s)`, `(l)`, `(g)` particle arrangement, spacing, motion, and forces; use state symbols **`(s)`, `(l)`, `(g)`, `(aq)`** in chemical equations.
    - *Required state changes:* **melting, freezing, vaporization (evaporation & boiling), condensation, sublimation, deposition**.
  - **`S1.1.3` Kelvin temperature & kinetic energy:** Temperature $T$ in kelvin ($\text{K}$) measures average kinetic energy $E_k$ of particles.
    - *Outcomes:* Interpret heating/cooling curves (flat plateaus during phase changes where added energy overcomes intermolecular/interparticle attractions rather than increasing $E_k$); convert between Celsius and Kelvin:
      $$T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$$
    - *Unit note:* Kelvin ($\text{K}$) is the SI base unit of temperature; $\Delta T(^\circ\text{C}) = \Delta T(\text{K})$ because increment size is identical.
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - *No HL subtopics exist for `S1.1`.* Do **not** include quantitative root-mean-square speed / Boltzmann constant $E_k = \frac{3}{2} k_B T$ derivations (outside IB Chem SL scope) or phase diagrams (triple/critical points).

##### `S1.2` — Structure 1.2: The nuclear atom
- **Guiding Question:** *How do the nuclei of atoms differ?*
- **SL Hours:** 2 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 1 hour (`S1.2.3`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S1.2.1` Nuclear structure & subatomic particles:** Positively charged, dense nucleus of protons and neutrons (**nucleons**); negatively charged electrons outside the nucleus.
    - *Outcomes:* Use nuclear symbol $^A_Z\text{X}$ (and ion charge) to deduce numbers of protons ($Z$), neutrons ($A - Z$), and electrons ($Z - \text{charge}$).
    - *Required constants:* Relative masses ($p = 1$, $n = 1$, $e^- \approx 0$ / negligible) and relative charges ($p = +1$, $n = 0$, $e^- = -1$); exact kg and C values are in Data Booklet Section 2 ($e = 1.602177 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$, $m_e = 9.109384 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$, $m_p = 1.672622 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$, $m_n = 1.674927 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$).
  - **`S1.2.2` Isotopes & relative atomic mass ($A_r$):** Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same $Z$) with different numbers of neutrons (different $A$).
    - *Outcomes:* Calculate non-integer $A_r$ from given isotopic masses and percentage abundances (and deduce isotopic abundance from given $A_r$):
      $$A_r = \frac{\sum (\text{isotopic mass} \times \%\text{ abundance})}{100}$$
    - *Properties:* Same chemical properties (same electron configuration); different physical properties (density, rate of diffusion/effusion, boiling/melting points due to mass differences). Specific isotope names need not be memorized.
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`S1.2.3` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** Interpreting **mass spectra** (e.g., $m/z$ sticks/peaks) of elements to determine relative atomic mass and isotopic abundances. *(In SL, isotopic mass/abundance data are given directly in text/tables, NOT as mass spectra; organic MS fragmentation `S3.2.8` is also HL-only).*

##### `S1.3` — Structure 1.3: Electron configurations
- **Guiding Question:** *How can we model the energy states of electrons in atoms?*
- **SL Hours:** 3 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 3 hours (`S1.3.6`–`S1.3.7`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S1.3.1` Emission spectra & electromagnetic radiation:** Atoms emit photons when excited electrons transition back to lower energy levels.
    - *Outcomes:* **Qualitatively** describe relationships between colour, wavelength ($\lambda$), frequency ($f$), and photon energy ($E$) across the electromagnetic spectrum (Data Booklet Section 5; shorter $\lambda \leftrightarrow$ higher $f \leftrightarrow$ higher $E$; visible spectrum $400\text{–}700\text{ nm}$ violet $\rightarrow$ red); distinguish **continuous spectra** (all wavelengths present) from **line spectra** (only discrete wavelengths emitted).
  - **`S1.3.2` Hydrogen line emission spectrum:** Provides evidence for discrete energy levels that **converge** at higher energies (lines become closer together at higher frequency / shorter wavelength).
    - *Outcomes:* Describe the relationship between lines and electron transitions returning to:
      - **$n = 1$** (ultraviolet region — largest energy gaps),
      - **$n = 2$** (visible region),
      - **$n = 3$** (infrared region — smallest energy gaps).
    - *Examiner guardrail:* Names of series (*Lyman, Balmer, Paschen*) are **NOT assessed**.
  - **`S1.3.3` Main energy levels ($n$):** Maximum electron capacity $= 2n^2$ ($n = 1 \rightarrow 2$; $n = 2 \rightarrow 8$; $n = 3 \rightarrow 18$; $n = 4 \rightarrow 32$).
  - **`S1.3.4` Sublevels & atomic orbital shapes:** Main energy levels divide into $s, p, d, f$ sublevels of successively higher energy ($s < p < d < f$ for a given $n$).
    - *Outcomes:* Recognize the spherical shape of an **$s$ orbital** and the dumbbell shape + orthogonal axes ($p_x, p_y, p_z$) of the three **$p$ orbitals**.
  - **`S1.3.5` Orbital filling & electron configurations up to $Z = 36$:** Each orbital holds max **2 electrons of opposite spin**; sublevels contain fixed orbital counts ($s: 1$, $p: 3$, $d: 5$, $f: 7$).
    - *Principles:* **Aufbau principle** ($1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p$), **Hund's rule** (degenerate orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing), **Pauli exclusion principle** (max 2 electrons per orbital with opposite spins).
    - *Required representations:* Full electron configurations (e.g., $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 \dots$), condensed noble-gas-core configurations (e.g., $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^1$), and **arrow-in-box orbital diagrams** for atoms and ions up to **$Z = 36$ ($\text{Kr}$)**.
    - *Mandatory Exceptions (SL!):* **Chromium ($\text{Cr}, Z=24$):** $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^1$ (or $[\text{Ar}]\,4s^1 3d^5$); **Copper ($\text{Cu}, Z=29$):** $[\text{Ar}]\,3d^{10} 4s^1$.
    - *Ion rule:* When forming positive ions from transition elements ($Z=21\text{–}30$), **$4s$ electrons are removed BEFORE $3d$ electrons** (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{2+} = [\text{Ar}]\,3d^6$; $\text{Cu}^+ = [\text{Ar}]\,3d^{10}$).
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`S1.3.6` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** Quantitative calculations of first ionization energy from the **convergence limit** using $E = hf$ and $c = f\lambda$, and explaining **discontinuities** in first IE across periods (linked to `S3.1.7` HL). *(In SL, $E=hf$ and $c=f\lambda$ are NOT assessed quantitatively; only qualitative inverse relationship $E \propto f \propto 1/\lambda$ in `S1.3.1`).*
  - **`S1.3.7` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** Analyzing **successive ionization energy** graphs/data to deduce group numbers or explain transition metal variable oxidation states.
  - Also exclude: shapes of $d$ or $f$ orbitals, quantum numbers $(l, m_l, m_s)$, and electron configurations beyond $Z = 36$.

##### `S1.4` — Structure 1.4: Counting particles by mass: The mole
- **Guiding Question:** *How do we quantify matter on the atomic scale?*
- **SL Hours:** 7 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 0 hours (`None for Structure 1.4`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S1.4.1` Mole & Avogadro constant:** $1\text{ mol}$ contains $N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$ elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions, electrons, formula units).
    - *Formula:* $N = n \times N_A$ (distinguish molecules vs atoms inside molecules, e.g., $1\text{ mol }\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has $3 N_A$ atoms).
  - **`S1.4.2` Relative masses:** Scale relative to $^{12}\text{C}$; **relative atomic mass ($A_r$)** and **relative formula mass ($M_r$)** are **dimensionless (no units)**.
    - *Strict Data Booklet Rule:* Must use $A_r$ values given to **two decimal places** from Data Booklet Section 7 (e.g., $\text{H} = 1.01$, $\text{C} = 12.01$, $\text{O} = 16.00$, $\text{Cl} = 35.45$).
  - **`S1.4.3` Molar mass ($M$):** Units $\text{g mol}^{-1}$.
    - *Formula (Data Booklet Sec. 1):* $n = \frac{m}{M}$.
  - **`S1.4.4` Empirical & molecular formulas:** Empirical = simplest whole-number ratio of atoms; Molecular = actual number of atoms per molecule.
    - *Outcomes:* Interconvert percentage composition by mass (or combustion analysis masses of $\text{CO}_2$ and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and empirical formula; determine molecular formula via $\text{factor} = \frac{M_{\text{molecular}}}{M_{\text{empirical}}}$.
  - **`S1.4.5` Molar concentration:** Square brackets $[\text{X}]$ denote molar concentration ($\text{mol dm}^{-3}$).
    - *Formula (Data Booklet Sec. 1):* $n = CV$ (where $V$ is in $\text{dm}^3$; $1\text{ dm}^3 = 1000\text{ cm}^3$).
    - *Unit conversion:* $\text{g dm}^{-3} = \text{mol dm}^{-3} \times M(\text{g mol}^{-1})$; also dilution $C_1 V_1 = C_2 V_2$. Standard solution & serial dilution glassware awareness (`Tool 1`).
  - **`S1.4.6` Avogadro's law:** Equal volumes of ideal gases at the same $T$ and $P$ contain equal numbers of molecules ($V \propto n$). Solve reacting gas volume ratio problems directly from balanced coefficients.
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - *No HL subtopics exist for `S1.4`.* (Keep titration/back-titration math within SL stoichiometry scope without HL equilibrium/electrochemistry extensions).

##### `S1.5` — Structure 1.5: Ideal gases
- **Guiding Question:** *How does the model of ideal gas behaviour help us to predict the behaviour of real gases?*
- **SL Hours:** 3 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 0 hours (`None for Structure 1.5`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S1.5.1` Kinetic assumptions of the ideal gas model:**
    1. Gas particles are in constant, random motion.
    2. **Particle volume is negligible** compared to the container volume.
    3. **No intermolecular forces** exist between particles.
    4. Collisions between particles and container walls are **perfectly elastic** (total kinetic energy conserved).
  - **`S1.5.2` Real gas deviations (SL in 2025!):** Real gases deviate most from ideal behavior at **low temperature** (particles move slower, intermolecular attractions become significant) and **high pressure** (particles forced closer, finite particle volume is no longer negligible relative to container volume; IMFs also become significant). Stronger IMFs (e.g., $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CO}_2$ vs $\text{He}$, $\text{H}_2$) cause greater deviation.
    - *Explicit Guide Boundary:* **"No mathematical coverage is required."**
  - **`S1.5.3` Molar volume & gas variable graphs:** At fixed $T$ and $P$, molar volume $V_m$ is constant.
    - *2025 STP Value (Data Booklet Sec. 2 & 4):* **$T = 273.15\text{ K}$ ($0^\circ\text{C}$), $P = 100\text{ kPa}$ $\Rightarrow V_m = 22.7\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1} = 2.27 \times 10^{-2}\text{ m}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$**.
    - *Graphical analysis:* Sketch and analyze graphs of $P$ vs $V$ (hyperbolic), $P$ vs $1/V$ (linear through origin), $V$ vs $T(\text{K})$ (linear through origin), $P$ vs $T(\text{K})$ (linear through origin), and $PV/RT$ vs $P$ (horizontal line at 1 for ideal gas vs real gas curve). *Names of specific laws (Boyle's, Charles's, Gay-Lussac's) are NOT assessed.*
  - **`S1.5.4` Ideal gas equation & combined gas law (Data Booklet Sec. 1):**
    $$PV = nRT \qquad \frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$$
    - *Strict SI Unit Rule (Guide p. 38):* **"Units of volume and pressure should be SI only."** With $R = 8.31\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$: $P$ MUST be in **$\text{Pa}$ ($\text{N m}^{-2}$)**, $V$ in **$\text{m}^3$**, $T$ in **$\text{K}$**, $n$ in **$\text{mol}$**. (Note practical conversion shortcut: if $P$ is in $\text{kPa}$ and $V$ in $\text{dm}^3$, numerically $\text{kPa} \times \text{dm}^3 = \text{Pa} \times \text{m}^3$, *but* students should show SI unit conversions explicitly when asked for SI units).
    - *Lab application (`Tool 1`):* Calculate molar mass of a volatile liquid/gas via $M = \frac{mRT}{PV}$.
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - *No HL subtopics exist for `S1.5`.* Do **not** include the van der Waals equation of state $(P + an^2/V^2)(V - nb) = nRT$, root-mean-square speed equations, or Graham's law of effusion.

#### Structure 2. Models of bonding and structure (20 Hours SL)

##### `S2.1` — Structure 2.1: The ionic model
- **Guiding Question:** *What determines the ionic nature and properties of a compound?*
- **SL Hours:** 4 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 0 hours (`None for Structure 2.1`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S2.1.1` Ion formation:** Metal atoms lose valence electrons $\rightarrow$ **cations** ($+$); non-metal atoms gain electrons $\rightarrow$ **anions** ($-$). Predict ion charge from electron configuration/periodic group; include variable charges formed by transition elements (e.g., $\text{Fe}^{2+}/\text{Fe}^{3+}$, $\text{Cu}^+/\text{Cu}^{2+}$).
  - **`S2.1.2` Ionic bond & naming:** Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in all directions. Binary ionic compounds named cation first, anion with **"-ide"** suffix; transition metal salts use Roman numerals for oxidation state (e.g., iron(III) chloride). Deduce formulas using charge neutrality.
    - *7 Mandatory Polyatomic Ions (must be memorized by name & formula):*
      - Ammonium: $\text{NH}_4^+$
      - Hydroxide: $\text{OH}^-$
      - Nitrate: $\text{NO}_3^-$
      - Hydrogencarbonate: $\text{HCO}_3^-$
      - Carbonate: $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$
      - Sulfate: $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$
      - Phosphate: $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$
  - **`S2.1.3` 3D Lattice structures & physical properties:** Ionic compounds form 3D giant ionic lattices represented by empirical formulas.
    - *Properties to explain:*
      - **Low volatility / high melting & boiling points:** Strong electrostatic attractions throughout the 3D lattice require large energy to overcome.
      - **Electrical conductivity:** **Do NOT conduct** when solid (ions fixed in lattice); **DO conduct** when molten `(l)` or aqueous `(aq)` (mobile ions carry charge).
      - **Brittleness:** Shifting layers brings like-charged ions adjacent $\rightarrow$ electrostatic repulsion cleaves crystal.
      - **Solubility:** Often soluble in polar solvents like water (ion–dipole interactions overcome lattice enthalpy); insoluble in non-polar solvents.
    - *Lattice enthalpy in SL (`S2.1.3` guidance + Data Booklet Sec. 16):* Qualitatively compare lattice enthalpy (strength of ionic bond) across compounds using **ionic charge** (higher $|q_+ q_-| \rightarrow$ much stronger attraction, e.g., $\text{MgO} > \text{NaF}$) and **ionic radius** (smaller distance $r_+ + r_- \rightarrow$ stronger attraction, e.g., $\text{LiF} > \text{CsI}$).
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - *No HL subtopics exist inside `S2.1`*, **HOWEVER** do **not** include **Born–Haber cycles** (`R1.2.5` HL-only), **formal charge of polyatomic ions like $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$** (`S2.2.14` HL-only), or **conjugate acid $K_a$ / stability links** (`R3.1.10–3.1.11` HL-only).

##### `S2.2` — Structure 2.2: The covalent model
- **Guiding Question:** *What determines the covalent nature and properties of a substance?*
- **SL Hours:** 10 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 8 hours (`S2.2.11`–`S2.2.16`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S2.2.1` Covalent bonding & Lewis formulas (up to 4 electron pairs):** Electrostatic attraction between a **shared pair of electrons** and the **positively charged nuclei** of both bonded atoms. Octet rule (8 valence electrons; duet for H).
    - *Outcomes:* Deduce Lewis formulas (dots, crosses, or dashes for pairs — MUST show **all** valence electron pairs, bonding and non-bonding!) for organic and inorganic molecules/ions with **up to 4 electron pairs** on each atom.
    - *Mandatory incomplete-octet examples:* $\text{BeCl}_2$ (4 valence $e^-$ around Be), $\text{BF}_3$ / $\text{BCl}_3$ / $\text{AlCl}_3$ monomer (6 valence $e^-$ around B/Al).
  - **`S2.2.2` Bond multiplicity, length & strength:** Single (1 shared pair), double (2), triple (3). As bond multiplicity increases between the same two atoms, **bond length decreases** and **bond strength (bond enthalpy) increases** (e.g., $\text{C}-\text{C}$ 154 pm / $346\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ vs $\text{C}=\text{C}$ 134 pm / $614\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$ vs $\text{C}\equiv\text{C}$ 120 pm / $839\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$; Data Booklet Sec. 11 & 12).
  - **`S2.2.3` Coordination (dative covalent) bonds:** Both electrons in the shared pair originate from the **same atom**.
    - *SL examples to identify:* $\text{NH}_4^+$, $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$, $\text{CO}$, $\text{Al}_2\text{Cl}_6$ dimer, $\text{NH}_3\text{BF}_3$.
  - **`S2.2.4` VSEPR model (up to 4 electron domains ONLY):** Electron domains (single/double/triple bonds and lone pairs each count as **one electron domain**) repel to maximize separation.
    - *Repulsion hierarchy:* $\text{Lone pair–lone pair} > \text{lone pair–bonding pair} > \text{bonding pair–bonding pair}$; multiple bonds exert slightly greater repulsion than single bonds.
    - *Complete SL VSEPR Table (2, 3, and 4 Electron Domains ONLY):*
      - **2 ED (0 LP):** Electron domain geometry = **Linear**; Molecular geometry = **Linear**; Bond angle = **$180^\circ$** (e.g., $\text{CO}_2$, $\text{BeCl}_2$, $\text{HCN}$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2$).
      - **3 ED (0 LP):** ED geometry = **Trigonal planar**; Molecular geometry = **Trigonal planar**; Bond angle = **$120^\circ$** (e.g., $\text{BF}_3$, $\text{CO}_3^{2-}$, $\text{NO}_3^-$, $\text{HCHO}$, $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4$).
      - **3 ED (1 LP):** ED geometry = **Trigonal planar**; Molecular geometry = **Bent / V-shaped**; Bond angle = **$\approx 117^\circ\text{–}118^\circ$** (e.g., $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{O}_3$, $\text{NO}_2^-$).
      - **4 ED (0 LP):** ED geometry = **Tetrahedral**; Molecular geometry = **Tetrahedral**; Bond angle = **$109.5^\circ$** (e.g., $\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_4^+$, $\text{CCl}_4$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$).
      - **4 ED (1 LP):** ED geometry = **Tetrahedral**; Molecular geometry = **Trigonal pyramidal**; Bond angle = **$\approx 107^\circ$** (e.g., $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{PCl}_3$, $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$).
      - **4 ED (2 LP):** ED geometry = **Tetrahedral**; Molecular geometry = **Bent / V-shaped**; Bond angle = **$\approx 104.5^\circ$** (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{OF}_2$, $\text{SCl}_2$).
  - **`S2.2.5` Bond polarity:** Caused by electronegativity difference $\Delta\chi$ between bonded atoms (Pauling values in Data Booklet Sec. 9). Represent dipoles with $\delta^+ / \delta^-$ or dipole arrows/vectors ($\longrightarrow$ with $+$ tail on $\delta^+$).
  - **`S2.2.6` Molecular polarity:** Depends on **both** bond polarity **and** molecular geometry (vector sum of bond dipoles).
    - *Examples where dipoles cancel (non-polar despite polar bonds):* $\text{CO}_2$ (linear), $\text{BF}_3$ (trigonal planar), $\text{CCl}_4$ / $\text{CH}_4$ (tetrahedral).
    - *Examples where dipoles do NOT cancel (polar):* $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{CHCl}_3$, $\text{SO}_2$.
  - **`S2.2.7` Covalent network structures (Allotropes of C, Si, $\text{SiO}_2$):**
    - **Diamond:** Each C tetrahedrally bonded to 4 C ($109.5^\circ$); hard, very high MP, non-conductor (all valence $e^-$ localized).
    - **Graphite:** Hexagonal planar layers; each C bonded to 3 C ($120^\circ$); 1 delocalized $e^-$ per C conducts electricity parallel to layers; weak London forces between layers allow sliding (lubricant).
    - **Graphene:** Single 2D hexagonal monolayer of $3$-coordinate C; extremely high tensile strength, thermal & electrical conductivity, very thin/transparent.
    - **Fullerenes (e.g., $\text{C}_{60}$ buckminsterfullerene):** Discrete molecular hollow spheres/tubes (simple molecular lattice with weak London forces between $\text{C}_{60}$ balls — lower MP/sublimes, soluble in some organic solvents, semiconductor/poor bulk conductor because delocalized $e^-$ do not easily jump between cages).
    - **Silicon ($\text{Si}$) & Silicon dioxide ($\text{SiO}_2$):** Giant 3D tetrahedral networks (like diamond; $\text{SiO}_2$ has each Si bonded to 4 O, each O to 2 Si; $\text{Si}-\text{Si}$ bonds weaker than $\text{C}-\text{C}$ due to larger atomic radius).
  - **`S2.2.8` Intermolecular forces (IMFs):**
    - **van der Waals forces** (2025 IB mandatory umbrella term): includes **London (dispersion) forces** (temporary/instantaneous induced dipoles; increase with molar mass / electron count / surface area contact), **dipole–induced dipole**, and **dipole–dipole** forces (between permanent polar molecules).
    - **Hydrogen bonding:** Special strong dipole–dipole interaction when H is covalently bonded directly to highly electronegative **N, O, or F** and interacts attractively with a lone pair on a neighbouring **N, O, or F** atom.
  - **`S2.2.9` Relative IMF strengths & physical properties:** For comparable molar mass: $\text{London dispersion} < \text{dipole–dipole} < \text{hydrogen bonding}$.
    - *Properties:* **Volatility/BP/MP** (higher IMFs $\rightarrow$ lower volatility, higher BP); **electrical conductivity** (covalent molecular = non-conductors; graphite/graphene conduct); **solubility** (*"like dissolves like"* — polar/H-bonding solutes dissolve in water; non-polar solutes dissolve in non-polar solvents; chain-length effect on alcohol solubility in water).
  - **`S2.2.10` Chromatography ($R_F$ values):** Separation based on differential partitioning / IMF attractions between **mobile phase** and **stationary phase**.
    - *Formula:* $R_F = \frac{\text{distance moved by component}}{\text{distance moved by solvent front}}$ ($0 \le R_F \le 1$, dimensionless).
    - *Boundary:* Locating agents and GC/HPLC technical/operational details are **NOT assessed**.
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`S2.2.11` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Resonance structures** and formal delocalization models (e.g., ozone, carbonate, nitrate, carboxylate resonance hybrids).
  - **`S2.2.12` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_6$)** Kekulé vs delocalized model, physical/thermochemical/chemical evidence for resonance energy. *(Note: SL `S3.2.2` only identifies the phenyl functional group name/structure, NOT benzene bonding theory or reactions).*
  - **`S2.2.13` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Expanded octets & 5 or 6 electron domains** (trigonal bipyramidal, seesaw, T-shaped, linear $\text{I}_3^-$, octahedral, square pyramidal, square planar; $90^\circ / 120^\circ$ angles).
  - **`S2.2.14` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Formal charge** ($\text{FC} = V - N - \frac{1}{2}B$) to choose preferred Lewis formulas.
  - **`S2.2.15` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Sigma ($\sigma$) and pi ($\pi$) bonds** (head-on vs lateral $p$-orbital overlap, counting $\sigma/\pi$ bonds).
  - **`S2.2.16` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Hybridization** ($sp, sp^2, sp^3$).

##### `S2.3` — Structure 2.3: The metallic model
- **Guiding Question:** *What determines the metallic nature and properties of an element?*
- **SL Hours:** 2 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 1 hour (`S2.3.3`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S2.3.1` Metallic bonding & properties:** Electrostatic attraction between a **lattice of positive metal ions (cations)** and **delocalized valence electrons**.

Down Group 1 ($\text{Li} > \text{Na} > \text{K} > \text{Rb} > \text{Cs}$): MP decreases because ionic radius increases (same $+1$ charge) $\rightarrow$ weaker attraction to delocalized electrons.

##### `S3.1` — Structure 3.1: The periodic table: Classification of elements
- **Guiding Question:** *How does the periodic table help us to predict patterns and trends in the properties of elements?*
- **SL Hours:** 7 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 4 hours (`S3.1.7`–`S3.1.10`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`S3.1.1` Organization of the periodic table:** Periods (1–7), Groups (1–18), Blocks ($s, p, d, f$), positions of metals, metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te), and non-metals.
  - **`S3.1.2` Electron configurations & group families:** Period number = highest occupied main energy level ($n$). Group elements share valence electron count. Deduce electron configuration up to $Z = 36$ from position and vice versa. Know family terms: **alkali metals** (Group 1, excluding H), **halogens** (Group 17), **transition elements** ($d$-block), **noble gases** (Group 18).
  - **`S3.1.3` Periodic trends (general periodicity across periods & down groups):** Explain using **nuclear charge (number of protons)**, **shielding / number of occupied main energy levels**, and **effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$)**:
    - **Atomic (covalent) radius:** Decreases across a period (same main energy level, increasing nuclear charge pulls valence electrons closer); increases down a group (more occupied main energy levels/shells).
    - **Ionic radius:** Cations smaller than parent atoms (loss of outer shell / fewer $e^-$ for same $Z$); anions larger than parent atoms (increased $e^-$–$e^-$ repulsion). Across isoelectronic series (e.g., $\text{Na}^+ > \text{Mg}^{2+} > \text{Al}^{3+}$ and $\text{P}^{3-} > \text{S}^{2-} > \text{Cl}^-$), radius decreases as nuclear charge increases.
    - **First ionization energy ($\text{IE}_1$):** $\text{X(g)} \rightarrow \text{X}^+\text{(g)} + e^-$. General increase across a period (greater nuclear charge, similar shielding, smaller radius); decreases down a group (outer $e^-$ further from nucleus, greater shielding).
    - **First electron affinity ($\text{EA}_1$):** $\text{X(g)} + e^- \rightarrow \text{X}^-\text{(g)}$. Generally becomes more exothermic across a period (non-metals) and less exothermic down a group (exceptions like F vs Cl due to small 2p repulsion can be referenced qualitatively via Data Booklet Sec. 9).
    - **Electronegativity ($\chi$):** Ability of an atom to attract shared electrons in a covalent bond. Increases across a period, decreases down a group (noble gases omitted).
  - **`S3.1.4` Group 1 & Group 17 chemical reactivity trends:**
    - **Group 1 metals + water:** Reactivity increases down the group ($\text{Li} < \text{Na} < \text{K}$) as $\text{IE}_1$ decreases and outer $e^-$ is lost more easily:
      $$2\text{M(s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow 2\text{MOH(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}$$
      *(Observations: fizzing/effervescence, metal floats/melts, K ignites with lilac flame; alkaline solution).*
    - **Group 17 halogens + halide ions (redox displacement):** Oxidizing ability / reactivity decreases down the group ($\text{Cl}_2 > \text{Br}_2 > \text{I}_2$) as atomic radius increases and attraction for an incoming electron decreases:
      $$\text{Cl}_2\text{(aq)} + 2\text{Br}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)} + \text{Br}_2\text{(aq)} \quad (\text{orange/brown})$$
      $$\text{Cl}_2\text{(aq)} + 2\text{I}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Cl}^-\text{(aq)} + \text{I}_2\text{(aq)} \quad (\text{brown; violet in non-polar solvent})$$
      $$\text{Br}_2\text{(aq)} + 2\text{I}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\text{Br}^-\text{(aq)} + \text{I}_2\text{(aq)}$$
  - **`S3.1.5` Continuum of metal to non-metal oxides (Period 3 & environmental oxides):** Bonding shifts from ionic metal oxides (basic) $\rightarrow$ intermediate/amphoteric ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$) $\rightarrow$ covalent non-metal oxides (acidic).
    - *Required Oxide + Water Equations:*
      - Group 1 oxide (basic, $\text{pH} \approx 14$): $\text{Na}_2\text{O(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH(aq)}$ (general $\text{M}_2\text{O} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{MOH}$)
      - Group 2 oxide (basic, $\text{pH} \approx 10\text{–}12$): $\text{MgO(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow \text{Mg(OH)}_2\text{(aq)}$ (general $\text{MO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{M(OH)}_2$)
      - Amphoteric oxide: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\text{(s)}$ is insoluble in water; reacts with acid ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 6\text{H}^+ \rightarrow 2\text{Al}^{3+} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and strong base ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{OH}^- + 3\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2[\text{Al(OH)}_4]^-$).
      - Silicon dioxide: $\text{SiO}_2\text{(s)}$ insoluble giant covalent oxide (reacts with hot concentrated alkali).
      - Carbon oxide (**ocean acidification**): $\text{CO}_2\text{(g)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3\text{(aq)}$ (weak carbonic acid lowers ocean $\text{pH}$).
      - Sulfur oxides (**acid rain**): $\text{SO}_2\text{(g)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{SO}_3\text{(aq)}$; $\text{SO}_3\text{(g)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq)}$. (Also recognize $\text{NO}_x$ contributing to acid rain: e.g., $2\text{NO}_2\text{(g)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow \text{HNO}_3\text{(aq)} + \text{HNO}_2\text{(aq)}$).
      - *(Note: $\text{P}_4\text{O}_{10} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 4\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ completes Period 3 trend, though `S3.1.5` specifically mandates Group 1, Group 2, carbon, and sulfur oxides).*
  - **`S3.1.6` Oxidation states (oxidation numbers):** Format MUST use **sign before Arabic numeral** (`+2`, `−1`) in formulas/deductions; Roman numerals in oxyanion/compound IUPAC names (e.g., sulfate(VI)).
    - *Mandatory rules & exceptions:* Free elements $= 0$; monatomic ion $=$ ion charge; $\text{F} = -1$; $\text{H} = +1$ (**$-1$ in metal hydrides** e.g., $\text{NaH}$); $\text{O} = -2$ (**$-1$ in peroxides** e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O}_2$, $+2$ in $\text{OF}_2$). Generic oxyanion names acceptable: nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$), nitrite ($\text{NO}_2^-$), sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$), sulfite ($\text{SO}_3^{2-}$).
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`S3.1.7` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Discontinuities in first ionization energy** across a period (e.g., $\text{Be}\rightarrow\text{B}$ and $\text{N}\rightarrow\text{O}$, $\text{Mg}\rightarrow\text{Al}$ and $\text{P}\rightarrow\text{S}$) explained by sublevel energies ($2p$ vs $2s$) and paired-electron repulsion.

### STRAND 2: REACTIVITY

#### Reactivity 1. What drives chemical reactions? (12 Hours SL)

##### `R1.1` — Reactivity 1.1: Measuring enthalpy changes
- **Guiding Question:** *What can be deduced from the temperature change that accompanies chemical or physical change?*
- **SL Hours:** 5 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 0 hours (`None for Reactivity 1.1`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`R1.1.1` Energy conservation, system vs surroundings, heat vs temperature:** Total energy conserved. **Heat ($Q$)** = thermal energy transferred due to a temperature difference (extensive, $\text{J}$ or $\text{kJ}$); **Temperature ($T$)** = measure of average kinetic energy of particles (intensive, $\text{K}$ or $^\circ\text{C}$).
  - **`R1.1.2` Endothermic vs exothermic reactions:**
    - **Exothermic:** Releases heat from system to surroundings; surroundings temperature **increases** ($\Delta T > 0$); $\Delta H < 0$ (negative).
    - **Endothermic:** Absorbs heat from surroundings into system; surroundings temperature **decreases** ($\Delta T < 0$); $\Delta H > 0$ (positive).
  - **`R1.1.3` Potential energy profile diagrams:**
    - *Mandatory Axis Labels (Guide p. 48):* **x-axis = `reaction coordinate`**, **y-axis = `potential energy`**.
    - *Features:* Reactants higher than products for exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$, products more energetically stable); reactants lower than products for endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$, reactants more stable); activation energy $E_a$ from reactants to peak.
  - **`R1.1.4` Standard enthalpy change ($\Delta H^\ominus$) & Calorimetry:** Heat transferred at constant pressure under standard conditions ($100\text{ kPa}$, specified temperature usually $298.15\text{ K}$, $1\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ solutions, standard states).
    - *Formulas (Data Booklet Sec. 1 & 2):*
      $$Q = mc\Delta T \qquad \Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n}$$
    - *Constants & Units:* $\Delta H^\ominus$ in **$\text{kJ mol}^{-1}$**; $c_w = 4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1} = 4.18\text{ kJ kg}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$.
    - *Calorimetry assumptions & error analysis (`Tool 1`, `Inquiry 3`):* Assume dilute aqueous solution has density $1.00\text{ g cm}^{-3}$ and specific heat capacity $4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$, negligible heat capacity of calorimeter, negligible heat loss. Experimental $|\Delta H|$ values are lower than theoretical due to **heat loss to surroundings**, **incomplete combustion** (soot/CO in spirit burners), and **heat absorbed by calorimeter/beaker**. Use cooling-curve graphical extrapolation back to mixing time ($t=0$) to correct for heat loss!
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - *No HL subtopics exist for `R1.1`.*

##### `R1.2` — Reactivity 1.2: Energy cycles in reactions
- **Guiding Question:** *How does application of the law of conservation of energy help us to predict energy changes during reactions?*
- **SL Hours:** 3 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 5 hours (`R1.2.3`–`R1.2.5`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`R1.2.1` Average bond enthalpies (Data Booklet Sec. 12):** Bond breaking is **endothermic** ($+$); bond making is **exothermic** ($-$).
    - *Formula:*
      $$\Delta H^\ominus = \sum E(\text{bonds broken in reactants}) - \sum E(\text{bonds formed in products})$$
    - *Mandatory Limitations (2 reasons why bond-enthalpy $\Delta H$ differs from experimental/calorimetric $\Delta H$):*
      1. Data Booklet values are **average** bond enthalpies measured across many different molecular environments (except diatomic molecules like $\text{H}-\text{H}$ or $\text{H}-\text{Cl}$).
      2. Bond enthalpies strictly apply ONLY to species in the **gaseous state** (ignore intermolecular forces / enthalpy of vaporization when reactions involve liquids or solids, e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$ vs $\text{H}_2\text{O(g)}$).
  - **`R1.2.2` Hess's Law (multistep reaction cycles):** Enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the pathway between initial and final states ($\Delta H_{\text{overall}} = \sum \Delta H_{\text{steps}}$).
    - *Outcomes:* Manipulate given thermochemical equations (reversing flips the sign of $\Delta H$; multiplying by $k$ multiplies $\Delta H$ by $k$) or construct enthalpy cycles to find an unknown $\Delta H$.

##### `R2.2` — Reactivity 2.2: How fast? The rate of chemical change
- **Guiding Question:** *How can the rate of a reaction be controlled?*
- **SL Hours:** 9 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 6 hours (`R2.2.6`–`R2.2.13`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`R2.2.1` Rate of reaction & measurement:** Rate $= \frac{\Delta [\text{product}]}{\Delta t} = -\frac{\Delta [\text{reactant}]}{\Delta t}$ (always quoted as a positive quantity, units typically $\text{mol dm}^{-3}\text{ s}^{-1}$).
    - *Outcomes:* Calculate average rate and instantaneous rate from **tangents** on concentration/volume/mass vs. time graphs. Understand experimental rate-tracking methods (`Tool 1`): gas volume (gas syringe/inverted cylinder), mass loss (open flask), pH change, colorimetry/spectrophotometry (absorbance), titration of quenched aliquots, electrical conductivity, clock reactions (time as dependent variable).
  - **`R2.2.2` Collision theory:** Particles must collide with **(1) kinetic energy $\ge E_a$** AND **(2) proper collision geometry/orientation**. Temperature in $\text{K}$ is proportional to average particle $E_k$.
  - **`R2.2.3` Factors affecting reaction rate:**
    - **Concentration (solutions) / Pressure (gases):** More particles per unit volume $\rightarrow$ higher frequency of collisions $\rightarrow$ higher rate.
    - **Surface area (particle size of solids):** Smaller pieces / powder expose more surface particles $\rightarrow$ higher collision frequency.
    - **Temperature:** Increases average $E_k$ (higher speed $\rightarrow$ slightly higher collision frequency) AND **dramatically increases the fraction/proportion of particles with $E \ge E_a$** (primary cause of rate increase!).
    - **Catalyst:** Provides an alternative pathway with lower $E_a$ $\rightarrow$ larger fraction of particles have $E \ge E_a$.
  - **`R2.2.4` Activation energy & Maxwell–Boltzmann curves:** $E_a$ is the minimum energy required for a successful collision.
    - *Curve rules:* Axes = `kinetic energy` (x) vs `fraction/number of particles` (y); starts at origin $(0,0)$; asymptotic to x-axis (never touches zero); **area under curve = total number of particles (constant at different $T$)**; at higher $T$, peak is lower and shifted right, giving a much larger shaded area $\ge E_a$.
  - **`R2.2.5` Catalysts & energy profiles:** Catalysts increase rate by providing an alternative pathway with lower $E_a$ without being chemically consumed; biological catalysts are **enzymes**. Sketch catalyzed vs uncatalyzed energy profiles for both exo- and endothermic reactions (showing $\Delta H$ is unchanged, only $E_a$ decreases equally for forward and reverse paths).
    - *Guide boundary:* *"The different mechanisms of homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts will not be assessed."*

  - **`R2.2.6`–`R2.2.8` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** Multistep reaction mechanisms, **rate-determining step (RDS)**, reaction **intermediates vs transition states**, molecularity (unimolecular, bimolecular, termolecular), multistep energy profiles.

##### `R2.3` — Reactivity 2.3: How far? The extent of chemical change
- **Guiding Question:** *How can the extent of a reversible reaction be influenced?*
- **SL Hours:** 5 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 4 hours (`R2.3.5`–`R2.3.7`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`R2.3.1` Dynamic equilibrium:** In a **closed system**, forward rate **equals** backward rate ($r_f = r_b > 0$), and macroscopic properties (concentrations, pressure, colour) remain **constant** (not necessarily equal!). Applies to both physical equilibria (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{O(g)}$, $\text{I}_2\text{(s)} \rightleftharpoons \text{I}_2\text{(aq)}$) and chemical equilibria.
  - **`R2.3.2` Equilibrium law & $K$ expression:** For homogeneous equilibrium $a\text{A} + b\text{B} \rightleftharpoons c\text{C} + d\text{D}$:
    $$K = \frac{[\text{C}]^c[\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a[\text{B}]^b}$$
    *(Pure solids and pure liquids are omitted; IB 2025 uses $K$ without subscript, though $K_c$ is standard for molar concentration expressions. Note: $K$ is treated as dimensionless in IB 2025 or units are not stressed).*
  - **`R2.3.3` Magnitude of $K$, temperature dependence & reverse $K$:**
    - *Extent scale (explicitly required in Guide p. 55):* $K \ll 1$ (essentially no reaction / reactants strongly favoured), $K < 1$ (reactants favoured), $K = 1$ (neither strongly favoured), $K > 1$ (products favoured), $K \gg 1$ (reaction nearly goes to completion).
    - *Manipulation rules:* Reversing equation $\Rightarrow K' = \frac{1}{K}$; multiplying coefficients by $n$ $\Rightarrow K' = K^n$ (if $n = \frac{1}{2}$, $K' = \sqrt{K}$); adding steps $\Rightarrow K_{\text{net}} = K_1 \times K_2$.
    - **$K$ depends ONLY on temperature** (NOT concentration, pressure, or catalyst).
    - *Direct substitution:* Given all equilibrium concentrations, students may compute $K$ directly (without ICE tables).
  - **`R2.3.4` Le Châtelier's principle:** When a system at equilibrium is subjected to a stress, the equilibrium shifts to partially oppose the change:
    - **Concentration:** Adding reactant / removing product shifts right (no change in $K$).
    - **Pressure/Volume (gases):** Increasing $P$ (decreasing $V$) shifts toward side with **fewer moles of gas** (no change in $K$). Adding an inert gas at constant volume has **no effect**.
    - **Temperature:** Increasing $T$ shifts in the **endothermic** direction ($\Delta H > 0$). **Changes $K$** (increases $K$ if forward is endothermic; decreases $K$ if forward is exothermic).
    - **Catalyst:** Increases forward and backward rates **equally**; reaches equilibrium faster; **NO effect on equilibrium composition or $K$**.
    - **Heterogeneous equilibria (`R2.3.4` guidance):** Include gas–aqueous equilibria such as $\text{X(g)} \rightleftharpoons \text{X(aq)}$ (e.g., $\text{CO}_2\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons \text{CO}_2\text{(aq)}$ in carbonated drinks/oceans).
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`R2.3.5` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Reaction quotient ($Q$)** calculations and predicting shift direction by comparing $Q$ vs $K$.
  - **`R2.3.6` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Quantitative equilibrium composition / ICE table calculations** starting from initial concentrations and $K$ (including small-$K$ approximations).
  - **`R2.3.7` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** Calculating $K$ from Gibbs energy using $\Delta G^\ominus = -RT\ln K$.
  - Also exclude: Partial-pressure equilibrium constant $K_p$ (not in IB Chemistry SL or HL).

    $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+] \qquad [\text{H}^+] = 10^{-\text{pH}}$$

  - **`R3.1.6` Strong vs weak acids & bases:** Differ in **extent of ionization/dissociation** in water; acid–base equilibria lie toward the **weaker conjugate**.

  - **`R3.2.7` Secondary (rechargeable) cells (SL in 2025!):** Reversible redox reactions recharged using external electrical energy. Deduce charging half-equations/overall equation by **reversing** the given discharge equations (and vice versa). Compare advantages/disadvantages of primary cells, secondary cells (e.g., lead-acid, Li-ion — specific cell chemistries given in prompt), and fuel cells (energy density, portability, rechargeability, lifetime, environmental waste).
  - **`R3.2.8` Electrolytic cells (MOLTEN salts ONLY at SL):** Convert electrical energy into chemical energy via **non-spontaneous** redox reactions.
    - *Components:* DC power supply, inert electrodes (e.g., graphite/Pt), molten electrolyte.
    - *Conduction:* **Delocalized electrons** carry current in external wires/electrodes; **mobile ions** carry current in the molten electrolyte.
    - *Molten salt electrolysis (`MX(l)`):* Cation $\text{M}^{n+}$ migrates to negative **cathode** and is **reduced** to metal $\text{M}$; anion $\text{X}^{m-}$ migrates to positive **anode** and is **oxidized** to non-metal (e.g., $\text{Cl}_2\text{(g)}$).
  - **`R3.2.9` Oxidation of alcohols (SL scope caveats!):**
    - **Primary alcohol ($1^\circ$) $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}], \text{ distillation}}$ Aldehyde $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}], \text{ reflux}}$ Carboxylic acid.** Explain experimental difference: **distillation** removes lower-boiling aldehyde (no H-bonding) as it forms before further oxidation; **heating under reflux** returns condensed vapour to flask for complete oxidation to carboxylic acid.
    - **Secondary alcohol ($2^\circ$) $\xrightarrow{[\text{O}], \text{ reflux}}$ Ketone** (no further oxidation).
    - **Tertiary alcohol ($3^\circ$):** **No oxidation** under similar conditions (lacks $\text{C}-\text{H}$ on the carbinol carbon).
    - *EXPLICIT 2025 SL GUIDE EXCLUSION (Guide p. 60):* **"Names and formulas of specific oxidizing agents, and the mechanisms of oxidation, will not be assessed."** *(Use `[O]` in balanced equations; knowing acidified $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ orange $\rightarrow$ green is helpful lab background but the colour-change chemistry is linked to HL `S3.1`!).*
  - **`R3.2.10` Reduction of carbonyls & carboxylic acids (SL scope caveats!):**
    - **Carboxylic acid $\xrightarrow{[\text{H}]}$ (via aldehyde) $\rightarrow$ Primary alcohol ($1^\circ$).**
    - **Ketone $\xrightarrow{[\text{H}]}$ Secondary alcohol ($2^\circ$).**
    - Include role of **hydride ions ($\text{H}^-$)** donating electrons/hydrogen during reduction.
    - *EXPLICIT 2025 SL GUIDE EXCLUSION (Guide p. 61):* **"Names and formulas of specific reducing agents, and the mechanisms of reduction, will not be assessed."** *(Do NOT require $\text{LiAlH}_4$ in dry ether vs $\text{NaBH}_4$ selectivity at SL; use `[H]` in equations).*
  - **`R3.2.11` Hydrogenation of alkenes & alkynes (Reduction):** Addition of $\text{H}_2$ lowers degree of unsaturation:
    - Alkene $+\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ni catalyst (heat) or Pt/Pd}} \text{Alkane}$
    - Alkyne $+\text{H}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{catalyst}} \text{Alkene}$ (and $+2\text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{Alkane}$).
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`R3.2.12` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)** ($\text{H}^+\text{(aq)} + e^- \rightleftharpoons \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{(g)}$, $E^\ominus = 0.00\text{ V}$) and **Data Booklet Section 19 ($E^\ominus$ table)**.
  - **`R3.2.13` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** Calculating **$E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cathode}} - E^\ominus_{\text{anode}}$** and predicting spontaneity from $E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} > 0$.
  - **`R3.2.14` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **$\Delta G^\ominus = -nFE^\ominus_{\text{cell}}$** and Faraday constant ($F = 9.65 \times 10^4\text{ C mol}^{-1}$).
  - **`R3.2.15` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Electrolysis of aqueous solutions** (competing oxidation/reduction of water, concentration effect on $\text{NaCl(aq)}$, inert vs active Cu electrodes in $\text{CuSO}_4\text{(aq)}$).
  - **`R3.2.16` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Electroplating** electrode equations.
  - Also exclude: Quantitative Faraday stoichiometry ($Q = It$, $n(e^-) = Q/F$) and alkaline-medium half-equation balancing (unless explicitly step-guided as in alkaline fuel cells `R1.3.5`).

##### `R3.3` — Reactivity 3.3: Electron sharing reactions
- **Guiding Question:** *What happens when a species possesses an unpaired electron?*
- **SL Hours:** 2 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 0 hours (`None for Reactivity 3.3`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`R3.3.1` Radicals:** A molecular entity with an **unpaired electron** (e.g., $\bullet\text{CH}_3$, $\text{Cl}\bullet$); highly reactive. Can be an atom, molecule (e.g., $\text{NO}\bullet$), cation, or anion.
  - **`R3.3.2` Homolytic fission & Initiation:** Breaking a covalent bond evenly so one bonding electron goes to each fragment, forming two radicals (e.g., $\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{UV light or heat}} 2\text{Cl}\bullet$).
    - *Mandatory Arrow Notation:* Use **single-barbed (fishhook) arrows** ($\rightharpoonup$) to represent movement of a single electron.
    - *Atmospheric link:* CFCs break $\text{C}-\text{Cl}$ bonds (weaker, $324\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) rather than $\text{C}-\text{F}$ ($492\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$) under UV; $\text{Cl}\bullet$ radicals catalyze ozone ($\text{O}_3$) breakdown.
  - **`R3.3.3` Free radical substitution of alkanes:** Alkanes are kinetically stable (strong non-polar $\text{C}-\text{C}$ and $\text{C}-\text{H}$ bonds, high $E_a$) despite being thermodynamically unstable toward combustion/halogenation; react with halogens ($\text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2$) under UV light to produce a **mixture of mono- and poly-substituted products** (e.g., $\text{CH}_3\text{Cl}, \text{CH}_2\text{Cl}_2, \text{CHCl}_3, \text{CCl}_4$) plus $\text{HCl}/\text{HBr}$:
    - **Initiation:** $\text{Cl}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{UV}} 2\text{Cl}\bullet$
    - **Propagation** (chain-carrying, 1 radical reactant $\rightarrow$ 1 radical product):  
      $\text{CH}_4 + \text{Cl}\bullet \rightarrow \bullet\text{CH}_3 + \text{HCl}$  
      $\bullet\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{Cl} + \text{Cl}\bullet$
    - **Termination** (2 radicals combine $\rightarrow$ non-radical molecule):  
      $\text{Cl}\bullet + \text{Cl}\bullet \rightarrow \text{Cl}_2$; $\bullet\text{CH}_3 + \text{Cl}\bullet \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{Cl}$; $\bullet\text{CH}_3 + \bullet\text{CH}_3 \rightarrow \text{C}_2\text{H}_6$ (explains trace ethane!).
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - *No HL subtopics exist for `R3.3`.*

##### `R3.4` — Reactivity 3.4: Electron-pair sharing reactions
- **Guiding Question:** *What happens when reactants share their electron pairs with others?*
- **SL Hours:** 4 hours | **HL Additional Hours:** 7 hours (`R3.4.6`–`R3.4.13`)
- **SL Understandings & Outcomes:**
  - **`R3.4.1` Nucleophiles:** Electron-pair **donors** that form a covalent bond to an electrophile; possess a lone pair (and often a negative charge or $\delta^-$). Recognize both **neutral** ($\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{NH}_3, \text{ROH}, \text{RNH}_2$) and **negatively charged** ($\text{OH}^-, \text{CN}^-, \text{Cl}^-, \text{Br}^-, \text{I}^-, \text{RO}^-$) nucleophiles.
  - **`R3.4.2` Nucleophilic substitution (SL conceptual & equation level ONLY):** A nucleophile donates an electron pair to an electron-deficient atom (e.g., $\delta^+\text{C}$ in $\text{R}-\text{X}$) to form a new bond while a **leaving group** ($\text{X}^-$) departs with the bonding pair:
    $$\text{R}-\text{X} + \text{Nu}^- \rightarrow \text{R}-\text{Nu} + \text{X}^-$$
    (e.g., $\text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br} + \text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{OH} + \text{Br}^-$, heated under reflux with aqueous $\text{NaOH}/\text{KOH}$).
    - *EXPLICIT 2025 SL GUIDE BOUNDARY (Guide p. 63):* **"Further details of the mechanisms are not required at SL."** *(Do NOT teach $\text{S}_\text{N}1$ vs $\text{S}_\text{N}2$, transition states, carbocation intermediates, stereochemical inversion, or kinetic rate orders at SL! Only general electron-pair movement descriptions/equations).*
  - **`R3.4.3` Heterolytic fission & curly arrows:** Breakage of a covalent bond where **both bonding electrons remain on one fragment**, forming opposite ions (e.g., $\text{A}-\text{B} \rightarrow \text{A}^+ + \text{B}^-$).
    - *Mandatory Arrow Notation:* Use **double-barbed curly arrows** ($\curvearrowright$) showing movement of an **electron pair** (tail at bond or lone pair, head at atom or bond formation site). Contrast explicitly with homolytic fission (`R3.3.2`, fishhook arrows, radicals).
  - **`R3.4.4` Electrophiles:** Electron-pair **acceptors** (electron-deficient species). Recognize both **neutral** ($\text{BF}_3, \text{AlCl}_3, \delta^+\text{H}-\text{Br}, \text{Br}_2$ polarized) and **positively charged** ($\text{H}^+, \text{NO}_2^+, \text{R}_3\text{C}^+$) electrophiles.
  - **`R3.4.5` Electrophilic addition reactions of alkenes (equations & observations ONLY at SL):** High electron density of $\text{C}=\text{C}$ double bond attracts electrophiles. Deduce balanced equations for alkenes with:
    1. **Halogens ($\text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2$):** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{Br}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_2\text{BrCH}_2\text{Br}$ (room temperature, **decolourizes orange-brown bromine water in the dark** — standard unsaturation test vs alkanes!).
    2. **Water / Steam ($\text{H}_2\text{O(g)}$):** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O(g)} \xrightarrow{\text{conc. }\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4, \sim 300^\circ\text{C}, \sim 60\text{ atm}} \text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}$.
    3. **Hydrogen halides ($\text{HCl}, \text{HBr}, \text{HI}$):** $\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{HBr} \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{CH}_2\text{Br}$.
    - *EXPLICIT 2025 SL GUIDE BOUNDARY (Guide p. 63):* **"The mechanisms of these reactions will not be assessed at SL."**
- **HL Exclusions (Do Not Include in SL):**
  - **`R3.4.6`–`R3.4.7` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Lewis acids and bases** (coordination bond formation between Lewis acid/base).
  - **`R3.4.8` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Transition element complex ions** and deducing complex ion charges from metal ion + ligands.
  - **`R3.4.9` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **$\text{S}_\text{N}1$ vs $\text{S}_\text{N}2$ mechanisms** (primary vs tertiary vs secondary halogenoalkanes, 1-step vs 2-step, Walden inversion / stereospecificity, energy profiles, rate laws).
  - **`R3.4.10` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Leaving group effect on rate** ($\text{R}-\text{I} > \text{R}-\text{Br} > \text{R}-\text{Cl}$).
  - **`R3.4.11`–`R3.4.12` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Electrophilic addition curly-arrow mechanisms** (symmetrical & unsymmetrical alkenes) and **carbocation stability / Markovnikov's rule** ($3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ carbocations).
  - **`R3.4.13` (HL ONLY — Exclude):** **Electrophilic substitution of benzene** (e.g., nitration mechanism with $\text{NO}_2^+$).

### 3.2 Calculation Step Checker Rules (Algorithmic Engine Spec)
Simple Revision's step-by-step chemistry grader should enforce these exact IB markscheme & IA conventions:

1. **Significant Figures & Intermediate Precision:**
   - **Rule 1 (Intermediate retention):** Never round intermediate answers; carry full floating-point / calculator precision through all steps.
   - **Rule 2 (Final SF rule for `×` and `÷`):** Round final answer to the **lowest number of significant figures** of any given raw datum (IB problems typically supply 3 SF data $\rightarrow$ **3 SF** default; accept $\pm 1$ SF only if markscheme doesn't fix SF, or flag exact match when required).
   - **Rule 3 (Decimal place rule for `+` and `−`):** Round to the **least number of decimal places** among the operands (e.g., $24.50\text{ cm}^3 - 0.00\text{ cm}^3 = 24.50\text{ cm}^3$).
   - **Rule 4 (Explicit override):** Any prompt instruction (*"Give your answer to 2 decimal places"* or *"to 1 significant figure"*) overrides general SF rules.
   - **Rule 5 (Data Booklet $A_r$):** Always sum molecular/formula masses using **2-decimal-place $A_r$** values from Data Booklet Section 7 (e.g., $\text{H}_2\text{O} = 2(1.01) + 16.00 = 18.02\text{ g mol}^{-1}$, NOT $18$ or $18.015$).
   - **Rule 6 (Logarithms / pH — convention check):** For $\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+]$, the digits **after** the decimal point in $\text{pH}$ correspond to the significant figures in $[\text{H}^+]$ (e.g., $[\text{H}^+] = 1.25 \times 10^{-3}\text{ mol dm}^{-3}$ [3 SF] $\rightarrow \text{pH} = 2.903$ [3 dp], though 2 dp is often accepted in SL exams).

2. **Measurement Precision & Uncertainty Propagation (Paper 1B & IA):**
   - **Analogue instruments** (burette, thermometer, graduated cylinder): Record to **half the smallest scale division** (e.g., $0.1\text{ cm}^3$ burette scale $\rightarrow \pm 0.05\text{ cm}^3$ per reading, so every reading has **2 decimal places** ending in `0` or `5`; a titer $\Delta V = V_{\text{final}} - V_{\text{initial}}$ has uncertainty $\pm 0.10\text{ cm}^3$).
   - **Digital instruments** (balance, pH probe, datalogger): Record to **$\pm$ the smallest displayed increment** (retain trailing zeros, e.g., $2.40\text{ g} \pm 0.01\text{ g}$).
   - **Propagation (Data Booklet Section 23):**
     - Addition / Subtraction ($y = a \pm b$): **Add absolute uncertainties**: $\Delta y = \Delta a + \Delta b$.
     - Multiplication / Division ($y = \frac{ab}{c}$): **Add fractional / percentage uncertainties**: $\frac{\Delta y}{y} = \frac{\Delta a}{a} + \frac{\Delta b}{b} + \frac{\Delta c}{c}$, then convert back $\Delta y = y \times \left(\frac{\%\text{ total}}{100}\right)$.
   - **Uncertainty Rounding & DP Alignment:**
     - Round final absolute uncertainty to **1 SF** (or 2 SF if leading digit is `1` or `2` in IA practice, e.g., $\pm 0.14\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}$, though 1 SF is standard IB rule).
     - **Match decimal places:** Value MUST be rounded to the **exact same decimal place** as its absolute uncertainty (e.g., $24.20 \pm 0.05\text{ cm}^3$, NOT $24.2 \pm 0.05\text{ cm}^3$).
   - **Percentage Error vs Percentage Uncertainty:**
     $$\%\text{ error} = \frac{|\text{experimental} - \text{literature}|}{|\text{literature}|} \times 100 \qquad \%\text{ uncertainty} = \frac{\Delta y}{|y|} \times 100$$
     *(Checker must verify whether $|\%\text{ error}| > \%\text{ uncertainty}$ to distinguish systematic error dominance from random instrument uncertainty!)*

3. **Stoichiometry & Mole Ratio Validation:**
   - Verify balanced stoichiometric coefficients before checking $n_{\text{target}} = n_{\text{known}} \times \frac{\nu_{\text{target}}}{\nu_{\text{known}}}$.
   - If quantities of $\ge 2$ reactants are given, enforce limiting-reagent check: compute $r_i = \frac{n_i}{\nu_i}$ for each reactant; reject any subsequent theoretical yield or calorimetry $n$ calculation that uses the excess reactant!

4. **Ideal Gas Law Unit Enforcement (`S1.5.4`):**
   - For $PV = nRT$ using $R = 8.31\text{ J K}^{-1}\text{ mol}^{-1}$:
     - $T$ MUST be converted: $T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$ (flag error if $+273$ causes drift or if Celsius used directly!).
     - $P$ MUST be in **$\text{Pa}$** ($1\text{ kPa} = 10^3\text{ Pa}$; $1\text{ atm} = 101.325\text{ kPa}$ if ever given).
     - $V$ MUST be in **$\text{m}^3$** ($1\text{ dm}^3 = 10^{-3}\text{ m}^3$; $1\text{ cm}^3 = 10^{-6}\text{ m}^3$).
     - Flag the classic factor-of-1000 bug when students combine $\text{kPa}$ with $\text{m}^3$ or $\text{Pa}$ with $\text{dm}^3$!
   - At STP ($273.15\text{ K}, 100\text{ kPa}$): validate $V_m = 22.7\text{ dm}^3\text{ mol}^{-1}$ (flag outdated `22.4` or `24.0` values!).

5. **Enthalpy Sign, Unit & State Symbol Checks (`R1.1.4`, `R1.2.1`):**
   - Step 1: $Q = mc\Delta T$: If $m$ is in $\text{g}$ and $c = 4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$, $Q$ is in **Joules ($\text{J}$)** $\rightarrow$ MUST divide by $1000$ to obtain **$\text{kJ}$**!
   - Step 2: Check $m$: In aqueous neutralization/displacement calorimetry, $m$ is the **total mass of solution** (not just solute or one reagent volume); in spirit-burner water heating, $m$ is the **mass of water heated** (NOT the mass of fuel burned!), while $n$ in $\Delta H = -Q/n$ is the **moles of fuel burned (or limiting reactant)**!
   - Step 3: Sign convention: $\Delta H = -\frac{Q}{n_{\text{lim}}}$ where $\Delta T = T_{\text{final}} - T_{\text{initial}}$. **Exothermic ($\Delta T > 0$) MUST have an explicit minus sign (`-`)**; **Endothermic ($\Delta T < 0$) MUST have an explicit plus sign (`+`)** (accept unsigned positive only if prompt specifies magnitude, otherwise require explicit `+` or `−`).
   - State symbols `(s)`, `(l)`, `(g)`, `(aq)` are **mandatory** whenever a question asks for a thermochemical equation, combustion equation, ionic precipitation, or acid–oxide/carbonate equation (`S1.1.2`, `R2.1.1`). ### 4.2 Paper 1A / 1B / 2 Question Archetypes
- **Paper 1A (MCQ, 30 marks):**
  - Conceptual particle/atomic diagrams, periodic trend comparisons (Period 3 / Group 1 / Group 17), VSEPR geometry/polarity matrix, IMF boiling-point ranking, stoichiometry limiting/atom-economy mental-plus-calculator checks, Le Châtelier shift vs $K$ invariance, Brønsted–Lowry conjugate pairs, oxidation state changes, radical vs heterolytic step identification.
- **Paper 1B (Data-based & Experimental Work, 25 marks — HIGH PRIORITY IN 2025):**
  - **Calorimetry cooling curve:** Extrapolate linear cooling portion back to $t_{\text{mix}}$ to find $\Delta T_{\text{corr}}$; propagate thermometer uncertainty ($\pm 0.5^\circ\text{C} \times 2 = \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$); critique heat loss & propose insulated lid/calorimeter calibration.
  - **Titration & standard solution:** Read burette menisci to $2\text{ dp}$ ($\pm 0.05\text{ cm}^3$); identify concordant titres (within $0.10\text{ cm}^3$); calculate volumetric flask / pipette / burette `% uncertainty`; diagnose funnel-left-in-burette or air-bubble systematic errors.
  - **Kinetics graphical analysis:** Draw tangent at $t=0$ or $t=t_1$ on gas-volume vs time curve to find rate; identify systematic offset vs random scatter around best-fit curve.
  - **Chromatography:** Measure distances on a scaled chromatogram to calculate $R_F$ and explain polarity partitioning.
  - **Ideal gas / Molar mass determination:** Use volatile liquid syringe/flask $P, V, T, m$ data to calculate $M$ and identify reasons for deviation (incomplete vaporization, air in apparatus, real-gas IMFs).
- **Paper 2 (Short-answer & Extended-response, 50 marks):**
  - Multi-part linked scenarios (e.g., *Ethene $\rightarrow$ Ethanol $\rightarrow$ Ethanoic acid* tying `S3.2` functional groups + `S2.2` IMFs/BP + `R3.4.5` hydration + `R3.2.9` reflux oxidation + `R1.2.1` bond enthalpy + `R2.1.5` atom economy + `R3.1.7` carboxylic acid neutralization).

## 5. Scientific Investigation (IA) Criteria A–D Rubric Breakdown

### 5.1 Core Parameters & Word Count Rules (Guide pp. 71–80)
- **Weighting:** **20%** of final SL grade (10 recommended hours).
- **Total Marks:** **24 marks** (4 criteria $\times$ 6 marks each; best-fit whole-number marking; identical criteria at SL and HL).
- **Maximum Word Count:** **3,000 words** strictly enforced.
  - **Excluded from word count:** Charts and diagrams; Data tables; Equations, formulas and calculations; Citations/references; Bibliography; Headers. *(Note: descriptive prose explaining calculations and column headers/prose captions outside raw data cells do count; candidate code, group member codes if applicable, title, and word count must appear at start).*
- **Collaborative vs Individual Rules:** Max group size = **3 students**. Same work cannot be used for both IA and Extended Essay.

### 5.2 Complete Criteria A–D Level Descriptors & Clarifications

#### Criterion A: Research Design (0–6 Marks, 25% of IA)
*Assesses communication of methodology (purpose and practice) to address the research question.*
- **0:** Does not reach standard described below.
- **1–2:** RQ stated without context; methodological considerations stated; methodology description lacks detail to allow reproduction.
- **3–4:** RQ outlined within a broad context; methodological considerations associated with collecting relevant and sufficient data described; methodology allows reproduction with few ambiguities or omissions.
- **5–6:** **RQ described within a specific and appropriate context; methodological considerations for collecting relevant and sufficient data explained; methodology allows unambiguous reproduction.**
- *Clarifications:* Context must name IV & DV (or two correlated variables), describe the chemical system, and include directly relevant background theory. Methodological considerations = measurement methods for IV/DV (or database/model sampling), scope/quantity/range/interval/repeats/precision, control variables & how controlled, and **safety, ethical, and environmental disposal considerations**.

#### Criterion B: Data Analysis (0–6 Marks, 25% of IA)
*Assesses recording, processing, and presentation of quantitative (and qualitative) evidence.*
- **0:** Does not reach standard described below.
- **1–2:** Recording & processing communicated but neither clear nor precise; limited consideration of uncertainties; processing has major omissions, inaccuracies, or inconsistencies.
- **3–4:** Communication is *either* clear *or* precise; evidence of uncertainty consideration but with significant omissions/inaccuracies; processing has significant omissions/inaccuracies/inconsistencies.
- **5–6:** **Communication is both clear and precise; appropriate consideration of uncertainties; processing carried out appropriately and accurately.**
- *Clarifications:* **Clear** = processing steps are easy to follow (worked sample calculation + summary table). **Precise** = correct units, decimal-place consistency with instrument precision, correct sig figs, annotated tables and graphs (scales, axis labels + units, trendlines, $R^2$ if fitted, uncertainty/error bars).

#### Criterion C: Conclusion (0–6 Marks, 25% of IA)
*Assesses how successfully the student answers the RQ relative to their analysis and accepted scientific context.*
- **0:** Does not reach standard described below.
- **1–2:** Relevant conclusion stated, not supported by analysis; superficial comparison to scientific context.
- **3–4:** Relevant conclusion described, not fully consistent with analysis; some relevant comparison to scientific context.
- **5–6:** **Relevant conclusion justified, fully consistent with the presented analysis (including uncertainties); justified through relevant comparison to accepted scientific context.**
- *Clarifications:* Must interpret processed data **including uncertainty bounds** (do uncertainty bars overlap or allow a clear trend? Does `% error` fall within `% uncertainty`?). Must compare to traceable, cited **literature values** / textbook theory.

#### Criterion D: Evaluation (0–6 Marks, 25% of IA)
*Assesses evaluation of investigation methodology and suggested realistic improvements.*
- **0:** Does not reach standard described below.
- **1–2:** States **generic** methodological weaknesses/limitations; states realistic improvements.
- **3–4:** Describes **specific** methodological weaknesses/limitations; describes realistic improvements relevant to those weaknesses.
- **5–6:** **Explains the relative impact (direction and magnitude) of specific methodological weaknesses or limitations; explains realistic, relevant improvements tied directly to those weaknesses.**
- *Clarifications:* Reject generic excuses (*"human error"*, *"misread burette"*, *"more trials"* without justification). Must distinguish **random errors** (scatter, instrument precision, timing lag) from **systematic errors** (heat loss in calorimetry, indicator overshooting, impure reagent, evaporation) and state **direction of shift** (e.g., heat loss makes experimental $|\Delta H|$ systematically *smaller/less exothermic*).

### 5.3 AI IA Studio Automated Checkpoints (Lab-Specific)
Simple Revision's IA Studio should run rule-based + LLM diagnostics tailored to common SL investigation archetypes:
1. **Global Checks:** Word count $< 3,000$ (auto-strip tables, equations, charts, bibliography); IV/DV/Control table completeness; safety & chemical waste disposal check; raw data DP uniformity; uncertainty DP alignment; sample worked calculation + Section 23 propagation check; traceable literature citation + `% error` vs `% uncertainty` comparison; zero generic *"human error"* phrases in Evaluation.
2. **Acid–Base / Redox Titration:** Burette readings all to `2 dp` ending in `0` or `5` ($\pm 0.05\text{ cm}^3$); $\Delta V$ uncertainty $\pm 0.10\text{ cm}^3$; concordant titres ($\le 0.10\text{ cm}^3$ spread) identified and rough trial excluded from mean; pipette & volumetric flask uncertainties propagated; systematic errors checked (CO₂ absorption in NaOH, rinsing flasks/burettes correctly, endpoint overshoot). *(Flag if student uses HL-only $K_a$ / Henderson–Hasselbalch unnecessarily when SL titration stoichiometry suffices).*
3. **Calorimetry / Enthalpy:** Cooling-curve extrapolation used rather than raw $T_{\max}$ when heat loss occurs; thermometer uncertainty ($\pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$ per reading $\rightarrow \pm 1.0^\circ\text{C}$ for $\Delta T$); spirit-burner evaporation/soot/heat-capacity-of-beaker directionality analyzed; $c = 4.18\text{ J g}^{-1}\text{ K}^{-1}$ and solution density assumptions critically evaluated.
4. **Kinetics (Gas Collection / Mass Loss / Colorimetry):** Initial rate tangent at $t=0$ (or clock-reaction $1/t$ justification); temperature bath control ($\pm$ fluctuation); systematic gas loss before stoppering / $\text{CO}_2$ solubility in water (syringe vs inverted cylinder). *(Flag if student attempts HL Arrhenius linear plot $\ln k$ vs $1/T$ — fine for IA curiosity since IA doesn't forbid going beyond SL, but remind SL student to keep conceptual explanation rooted in collision theory).*
