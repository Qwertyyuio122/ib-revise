## 1. Course Overview & SL Assessment Blueprint

### 1.1 Course Positioning & Recommended Teaching Hours

Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (AA) is designed for students who want strong fluency in algebraic manipulation, analytical methods, mathematical proof, and calculus. Unlike **Math AA HL** (`240 hours`), **Math AA SL** (`150 hours`) omits advanced higher-level theory (complex numbers, proof by induction, vectors, planes, Maclaurin series, differential equations, etc.) while retaining rigorous algebra, trigonometry, functions, statistics, and single-variable calculus.

### 1.5 Prior Learning Prerequisites (SL Scope)

Questions assume mastery of the following **Prior Learning** topics (from *Mathematics: analysis and approaches guide*, pp. 26–27). Items marked *(HL only in Prior Learning)* in the IB guide are explicitly flagged below:

- **Number and Algebra (SL Prior Learning):**
  - Number sets: $\mathbb{N}$ (`{0, 1, 2, ...}`), $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Q}$, irrationals, $\mathbb{R}$.
  - SI units (`mass, time, length`) and derived units (`speed, area, volume`).
  - Rounding, decimal approximations, significant figures, appreciation of errors.
  - Absolute value (modulus) $|a|$ definition and elementary treatment.
  - Arithmetic order of operations with integers, decimals, fractions; prime numbers, factors, multiples; ratio, percentage, proportion.
  - Algebraic manipulation (expansion, factorization), rearranging formulae, substitution.
  - Integer positive exponents; inequalities (`<, ≤, >, ≥`) and number-line intervals; simple surds/radicals; $a \times 10^k$ notation.
  - World currencies; linear equations & inequalities; systems of linear equations in **two variables**; basic set notation (`∪`, `∩`).
  - *Note: In the guide's Prior Learning table, GCF/LCM, rationalizing the denominator, rational-exponent evaluation (covered later in SL 1.7), quadratic equations/inequalities with rational coefficients (covered later in SL 2.7), and algebraic fraction addition/subtraction are marked **(HL only)** as prerequisites—though quadratics and rational exponents are taught directly within AA SL.*
- **Functions (SL Prior Learning):** Graphing linear & quadratic functions using technology; mappings via ordered pairs, tables, diagrams, graphs.
- **Geometry & Trigonometry (SL Prior Learning):** Pythagoras' theorem & converse; 2D midpoint & distance; point, line, plane, angle; degrees & compass directions; triangle angle sum ($180^\circ$); right-angled trig; three-figure bearings; simple geometric transformations (translation, reflection, rotation, enlargement); circle terms (centre, radius, diameter, arc, sector, chord, tangent, segment); area/perimeter of plane shapes (triangles, parallelograms, rhombuses, rectangles, squares, kites, trapezoids, compound figures); familiarity with 3D solids (prisms, pyramids, spheres, cylinders, cones) and volumes/surface areas of cuboids, prisms, cylinders, compound solids.
- **Statistics & Probability (SL Prior Learning):** Bar charts, pie charts, pictograms, line graphs; discrete mean, median, mode, range; simple event probability; Venn diagrams; tree diagrams.
- **Calculus (SL Prior Learning):** $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$.

### Topic 1: Number and Algebra (`19 Hours` · `SL 1.1`–`SL 1.9`)

*`SL 1.1`–`SL 1.5` are Common SL Core; `SL 1.6`–`SL 1.9` are AA-Specific SL.*

#### `SL 1.1` — Scientific Notation (Standard Form) *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Operations with numbers in the form $a \times 10^k$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Calculator/computer syntax (e.g., `5.2E30`) is **strictly unacceptable** on exam scripts and must be rewritten as $5.2 \times 10^{30}$.
- **Key Formulas:** $a \times 10^k,\; 1 \le a < 10,\; k \in \mathbb{Z}$ `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Arithmetic operations (multiply/divide/add) in standard form without a calculator. **P2:** Interpreting large/small calculator outputs and rewriting `E` notation cleanly.

#### `SL 1.2` — Arithmetic Sequences and Series *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** $n^{\text{th}}$ term $u_n$, sum of first $n$ terms $S_n$, sigma notation ($\sum$), simple interest as a linear/arithmetic application, approximating common differences when real-life models are not perfectly arithmetic. Identify $u_1$ and $d$ when using technology.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $u_n = u_1 + (n - 1)d$ `[FB]`
  - $S_n = \frac{n}{2}\big(2u_1 + (n-1)d\big) = \frac{n}{2}(u_1 + u_n)$ `[FB]`
  - $u_n = S_n - S_{n-1}\;(n \ge 2),\; u_1 = S_1$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Algebraic simultaneous equations for $u_1$ and $d$, quadratic equations from $S_n$, evaluating finite sigma sums. **P2:** Checking sequences via GDC table/list mode; solving inequalities on $n$ (e.g., first term exceeding threshold).

#### `SL 1.3` — Geometric Sequences and Series *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** $n^{\text{th}}$ term $u_n$, finite sum $S_n$, sigma notation, real-life modeling (population growth, spread of disease, salary increase/decrease). Identify $u_1$ and $r$ when using technology. Links to exponential models (`SL 2.9`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $u_n = u_1 r^{n-1}$ `[FB]`
  - $S_n = \frac{u_1(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} = \frac{u_1(1 - r^n)}{1 - r},\; r \neq 1$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact fraction/surd common ratios, finding $r$ from non-consecutive terms ($u_k / u_m = r^{k-m}$, watching $\pm r$!). **P2:** Solving $u_n > k$ or $S_n > k$ via logarithms (`SL 1.7`) or GDC graph/table intersection.

#### `SL 1.4` — Financial Applications of Geometric Sequences & Series *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Compound interest (yearly, half-yearly, quarterly, monthly) and annual depreciation. Real value of an investment given interest rate and inflation rate. Contexts may include loans. Questions will **not** ask students to derive the compound interest formula. Technology including built-in GDC Finance/TVM packages may be required. *(Continuous compounding $(1 + 1/n)^n \to e$ is Enrichment only and NOT examined.)*
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $FV = PV \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{100k}\right)^{kn}$ `[FB]` *(where $FV$ = future value, $PV$ = present value, $n$ = years, $k$ = compounding periods/year, $r\%$ = nominal annual rate)*
  - Annual depreciation: $FV = PV\left(1 - \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$ `[MEM]`
  - Inflation-adjusted real rate / real value: $1 + i_{\text{real}} = \frac{1 + i_{\text{nominal}}}{1 + i_{\text{inflation}}}$ or $FV_{\text{real}} = \frac{FV_{\text{nominal}}}{(1 + i_{\text{inf}}/100)^n}$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Rarely numerical; if set, simple powers like $(1.1)^2$ or writing an unsimplified expression. **P2:** Heavy GDC **TVM Finance Solver** usage (`N, I%, PV, PMT, FV, P/Y, C/Y`) and log solving for $n$.

#### `SL 1.5` — Integer Exponents & Introduction to Logarithms *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Laws of exponents with integer indices (e.g., $5^3 \times 5^{-6} = 5^{-3}$, $(2x)^4 = 16x^4$, $2x^{-3} = \frac{2}{x^3}$). Introduction to logarithms base $10$ and $e$; numerical evaluation via technology; awareness that $a^x = b \iff \log_a b = x$, $b > 0$, and $\log_e x = \ln x$.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $a^x = b \iff x = \log_a b\;(a > 0, b > 0, a \neq 1)$ `[FB]`
  - Exponent rules ($a^m a^n = a^{m+n}$, $a^m / a^n = a^{m-n}$, $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$, $a^0 = 1$, $a^{-n} = 1/a^n$) `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact mental log evaluations ($\log_2 8 = 3$, $\log_{10} 0.01 = -2$, $\ln e^3 = 3$) and negative/zero exponent algebra. **P2:** Evaluating non-integer logs and pH/Richter/decibel contexts.

#### `SL 1.6` — Simple Deductive Proof *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Numerical and algebraic deductive proofs; strict **LHS to RHS** (or RHS to LHS) layout; symbols for equality (`=`) vs identity (`≡`). Students must show how to check results, including checking their own results (e.g., verifying algebraic identities such as $\frac{1}{m+1} + \frac{1}{m^2+m} \equiv \frac{1}{m}$ or $(x-3)^2 + 5 \equiv x^2 - 6x + 14$).
- **Key Formulas / Structures:**
  - Never assume the identity to prove it (do NOT work simultaneously on both sides with `=`). Start cleanly with `LHS = ...` and manipulate step-by-step until reaching `RHS` `[MEM]`.
  - Use consecutive integers ($n, n+1$), even ($2n$), odd ($2n+1$ or $2n-1$) forms `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **Predominantly P1** (`Show that` / `Prove` algebraic identity questions).

#### `SL 1.7` — Rational Exponents, Laws of Logarithms & Exponential Equations *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Rational indices $a^{1/m} = \sqrt[m]{a}$ (if $m$ is even, positive principal root, e.g. $16^{3/4} = 8$). Full laws of logarithms, change of base, and solving exponential equations analytically (both equal-base method e.g. $(1/3)^x = 9^{x+1}$ and taking logs e.g. $2^{x-1} = 10$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\log_a(xy) = \log_a x + \log_a y$ `[FB]`
  - $\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_a x - \log_a y$ `[FB]`
  - $\log_a(x^m) = m \log_a x$ `[FB]`
  - $\log_a x = \frac{\log_b x}{\log_b a}$ `[FB]`
  - $a^{p/q} = (\sqrt[q]{a})^p = \sqrt[q]{a^p}$; $\log_a a = 1$; $\log_a 1 = 0$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 (Very High Frequency):** Combining/expanding logs without calculator, change-of-base algebraic equations ($\log_4 x + \log_2 x = 6$), exact exponential equations, rational surd/index simplification. **P2:** Modeling decay/growth equations requiring decimal solutions to 3 sf.

#### `SL 1.8` — Sum of Infinite Convergent Geometric Sequences *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Condition for convergence $|r| < 1$ (modulus notation $-1 < r < 1$) and computing $S_\infty$. Links to geometric series (`SL 1.3`) and repeating decimals/fractals.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $S_\infty = \frac{u_1}{1 - r},\; |r| < 1$ `[FB]` *(Note: Version 1.0 of the 2019 Formula Booklet contained a printing erratum $u_1/(r-1)$; corrected to $u_1/(1-r)$ in IB errata and all mathematical usage.)*
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Finding $r$ in terms of $x$ and stating convergence interval $|r| < 1$; solving exact $S_\infty$ equations. **P2:** Multi-step applied series problems.

#### `SL 1.9` — The Binomial Theorem *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Expansion of $(a + b)^n$ for **$n \in \mathbb{N}$** (positive integers/zero only). Use of Pascal's triangle and ${}^nC_r$ via both formula and GDC tables (e.g., find $r$ when ${}^6C_r = 20$ using GDC table). Finding specific terms or coefficients (including negative powers inside binomials like $(2x + \frac{3}{x^2})^6$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $(a+b)^n = a^n + {}^nC_1 a^{n-1}b + \dots + {}^nC_r a^{n-r}b^r + \dots + b^n$ `[FB]`
  - ${}^nC_r = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$ `[FB]`
  - General $(r+1)^{\text{th}}$ term: $T_{r+1} = {}^nC_r a^{n-r}b^r$; $0! = 1$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Small $n$ ($n \le 7$) expansions, finding constant term / coefficient of $x^k$ by algebra, solving ${}^nC_2 = k$. **P2:** Larger $n$ ($n \ge 8$) finding unknown $n$ or $r$ via GDC `nCr` or table.

- **Topic 1 Explicit Exclusions:**
  - **HL Only (`AHL 1.10–1.16`):** General permutations ${}^nP_r$ & combinatorics counting problems; binomial expansion for fractional/negative $n \in \mathbb{Q}$; partial fractions; complex numbers ($i$, Argand, polar/Euler, De Moivre, complex conjugate roots); proof by mathematical induction & proof by contradiction; $3\times 3$ linear systems.
  - **AI Only:** Error bounds/percentage error; full amortization schedule problems (`AI SL 1.7`); matrices & Markov transition matrices.

### Topic 2: Functions (`21 Hours` · `SL 2.1`–`SL 2.11`)

*`SL 2.1`–`SL 2.4` are Common SL Core; `SL 2.5`–`SL 2.11` are AA-Specific SL. On exam papers, domain is the largest possible subset of $\mathbb{R}$ unless otherwise stated, and questions may ask students to graph functions not explicitly named in Topic 2.*

#### `SL 2.1` — Equations of a Straight Line *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Gradient, $x$- and $y$-intercepts, parallel lines ($m_1 = m_2$), perpendicular lines ($m_1 \times m_2 = -1$), converting between gradient-intercept, general ($ax+by+d=0$ usually integer coefficients), and point-gradient forms. Contexts: road/bridge inclines.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $y = mx + c;\; ax + by + d = 0;\; y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ `[FB]`
  - $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$ `[FB]`
  - Parallel: $m_1 = m_2$; Perpendicular: $m_1 m_2 = -1$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Perpendicular bisectors of segments (`Prior Learning` midpoint + `SL 2.1`), finding integer general form. **P2:** Linear intersections and geometric modeling.

#### `SL 2.2` — Concept of a Function, Domain, Range & Inverse Concept *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Domain, range, graph, function notation ($f(x), v(t), C(n)$), functions as models. Informal inverse as reversing/undoing $f$; reflection in $y = x$; notation $f^{-1}(x)$. Solving $f(x) = 10 \iff x = f^{-1}(10)$. Awareness that inverse exists **only for one-to-one functions**, and $\text{Domain}(f^{-1}) = \text{Range}(f)$ (and $\text{Range}(f^{-1}) = \text{Domain}(f)$).
- **Key Formulas:** Reflection in $y = x$: $(a, b) \in f \iff (b, a) \in f^{-1}$; $\text{Dom}(f^{-1}) = \text{Ran}(f)$ `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sketching $f^{-1}$ by reflecting key points across $y = x$; stating domain/range of $\sqrt{2-x}$ or $\frac{1}{x-3}$. **P2:** Determining range visually from GDC graph.

#### `SL 2.3` — Graphing Functions (`Draw` vs. `Sketch`) *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Understanding equation $y = f(x)$; strict distinction between **draw** (accurate, to scale, plotted points, ruler for straight lines) and **sketch** (general shape with key features labeled). Transferring a GDC screen graph cleanly to paper; graphing sums/differences $f(x) \pm g(x)$ using technology. All axes and key features must be labeled.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sketching known shapes (`linear, quadratic, reciprocal, exp, log, trig`) analytically. **P2:** Graphing unfamiliar functions (e.g., $x e^{-x} + \sin x$) on GDC, choosing an appropriate viewing window, and sketching onto paper with labeled features.

#### `SL 2.4` — Key Features of Graphs & Technology Intersections *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Determining local/global maxima & minima, $x$- and $y$-intercepts, axes of symmetry, vertices, zeros/roots, vertical and horizontal asymptotes, and intersection points of two curves/lines using graphing technology.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P2 Dominant:** `ZERO`, `MINIMUM`, `MAXIMUM`, `INTERSECT` GDC workflows.

#### `SL 2.5` — Composite Functions & Analytic Inverse Functions *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Evaluating and simplifying $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$; identity function; finding $f^{-1}(x)$ algebraically by swapping $x$ and $y$ and isolating $y$; verifying $(f \circ f^{-1})(x) = (f^{-1} \circ f)(x) = x$; restricting domain if needed to ensure one-to-one.
- **Key Formulas:** $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$; $(f \circ f^{-1})(x) = x$ `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Algebraic derivation of $f^{-1}(x)$ (including rational functions $\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}$, quadratics on restricted domains $x \ge h$, exponentials/logs) and solving $(f \circ g)(x) = k$.

#### `SL 2.6` — Quadratic Functions & Forms *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Standard form $ax^2+bx+c$, factored/intercept form $a(x-p)(x-q)$, vertex form $a(x-h)^2+k$, parabola symmetry, converting cleanly between all three forms (completing the square, expanding, factoring). Links to transformations (`SL 2.11`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $f(x) = ax^2+bx+c \implies$ axis of symmetry $x = -\frac{b}{2a}$ `[FB]`
  - Vertex form $a(x-h)^2+k$ (vertex $(h, k)$); factored form $a(x-p)(x-q)$ ($x$-intercepts $p, q$, axis $x = \frac{p+q}{2}$) `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Completing the square (`a ≠ 1` especially!), finding $a$ from vertex + 1 point or $x$-intercepts + 1 point.

#### `SL 2.7` — Quadratic Equations, Inequalities & Discriminant *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Solving quadratic equations & inequalities via factorization, completing the square, and quadratic formula; roots/zeros terminology; discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ governing nature of roots (2 distinct real, 2 equal real, no real roots), often with unknown parameter $k$ (e.g., $3kx^2 + 2x + k = 0$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a},\; a \neq 0$ `[FB]`
  - $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$ `[FB]`
  - $\Delta > 0$ (2 distinct real), $\Delta = 0$ (2 equal real / tangent), $\Delta < 0$ (no real roots); note $\Delta \ge 0$ for "has real roots" `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Parameter inequalities on $k$ (e.g. $k^2 - 4 > 0 \implies k < -2 \text{ or } k > 2$), disguised quadratics. **P2:** Numerical roots via polynomial solver/graph.

#### `SL 2.8` — Reciprocal & Linear-over-Linear Rational Functions *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Reciprocal function $f(x) = \frac{1}{x}\;(x \neq 0)$ and its self-inverse property ($f^{-1}(x) = f(x)$); rational functions $f(x) = \frac{ax+b}{cx+d}$ ($c \neq 0$), vertical asymptote, horizontal asymptote, axial intercepts, sketching with dashed asymptotes, and linking via transformations of $\frac{1}{x}$ (`SL 2.11`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - Vertical asymptote: $x = -\frac{d}{c}$ (denominator $= 0$) `[MEM]`
  - Horizontal asymptote: $y = \frac{a}{c}$ (ratio of leading coefficients) `[MEM]`
  - $y$-intercept: $\left(0, \frac{b}{d}\right)$; $x$-intercept: $\left(-\frac{b}{a}, 0\right)$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Sketching hyperbolas, finding asymptotes, algebraic inverse $f^{-1}(x) = \frac{dx-b}{-cx+a}$, rewriting $\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}$ as $p + \frac{q}{cx+d}$ to describe transformations.

#### `SL 2.9` — Exponential and Logarithmic Functions *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Graphs & features of $f(x) = a^x\;(a > 0)$ (horizontal asymptote $y = 0$, $y$-int $(0, 1)$) and $f(x) = e^x$; $f(x) = \log_a x\;(x > 0)$ and $f(x) = \ln x\;(x > 0)$ (vertical asymptote $x = 0$, $x$-int $(1, 0)$). Exponential and logarithmic functions as inverses of each other. Links to compound interest/growth (`SL 1.4`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $a^x = e^{x \ln a}$ `[FB]`
  - $\log_a a^x = x = a^{\log_a x}$ where $a, x > 0, a \neq 1$ `[FB]`
  - Asymptotes & domains: $\text{Dom}(e^x) = \mathbb{R},\; \text{Ran}(e^x) = (0, \infty)$; $\text{Dom}(\ln x) = (0, \infty),\; \text{Ran}(\ln x) = \mathbb{R}$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sketching transformed exp/log curves, finding asymptote/intercepts/inverse analytically. **P2:** Fitting exponential growth/decay models and finding intersections.

#### `SL 2.10` — Solving Equations Graphically & Analytically *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Analytical methods (including hidden quadratics like $e^{2x} - 5e^x + 4 = 0$ or log equations checking extraneous roots!) alongside GDC graphical/numerical solving for transcendental or polynomial equations with no elementary closed form (e.g., $e^x = \sin x$, $x^4 + 5x - 6 = 0$). Real-world modeling contexts (radioactive decay, population, braking distance, projectile motion).
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Quadratic-in-$e^x$, quadratic-in-$\ln x$, or log equations (must reject roots violating log domain $> 0$). **P2:** Graphing LHS and RHS (or LHS $-$ RHS $= 0$) to find all intersection roots to 3 sf.

#### `SL 2.11` — Transformations of Graphs *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Translations ($y = f(x)+b$, $y = f(x-a)$), reflections in $x$-axis ($y = -f(x)$) and $y$-axis ($y = f(-x)$), vertical stretch scale factor $p$ ($y = pf(x)$), horizontal stretch scale factor $\frac{1}{q}$ ($y = f(qx)$), and composite transformations (including order sensitivity!). **Explicit Guide Note:** *"Not required at SL: transformations of the form $f(ax + b)$."* *(Note: While $f(ax+b)$ as an arbitrary combined horizontal transformation is `AHL 2.16`, circular functions `SL 3.7` explicitly use $f(x) = a\sin(b(x+c))+d$ in factored form.)*
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $(x, y) \mapsto (x + a, y)$ for $f(x - a)$; $(x, y) \mapsto (x, y + b)$ for $f(x) + b$ `[MEM]`
  - $(x, y) \mapsto (x, -y)$ for $-f(x)$; $(x, y) \mapsto (-x, y)$ for $f(-x)$ `[MEM]`
  - $(x, y) \mapsto (x, py)$ for $p f(x)$; $(x, y) \mapsto \left(\frac{x}{q}, y\right)$ for $f(qx)$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Mapping discrete coordinates $(x_0, y_0)$, describing transformations geometrically in exact order, writing new equation after stated transformations.

- **Topic 2 Explicit Exclusions:**
  - **HL Only (`AHL 2.12–2.16`):** Cubic/quartic polynomials, Factor/Remainder Theorems, Vieta's sum/product of roots; higher-degree rational functions with oblique asymptotes; odd/even function proofs; modulus inequalities/equations & $|f(x)|, f(|x|), 1/f(x), [f(x)]^2, f(ax+b)$ graph transformations.
  - **AI Only:** Sinusoidal/cubic/logistic regression models; log-log / semi-log linearization.

### Topic 3: Geometry and Trigonometry (`25 Hours` · `SL 3.1`–`SL 3.8`)

*`SL 3.1`–`SL 3.3` are Common SL Core; `SL 3.4`–`SL 3.8` are AA-Specific SL. Radians are assumed on exams unless degrees are shown.*

#### `SL 3.1` — 3D Coordinate Geometry, Mensuration & Right-Angled 3D Trig *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** 3D distance and midpoint; volume and surface area of right-pyramids, right cones, spheres, hemispheres, and compound solids; angle between two intersecting lines or between a line and a plane. **Explicit SL Restriction:** *"In SL examinations, only right-angled trigonometry questions will be set in reference to three-dimensional shapes."*
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $d = \sqrt{(x_1-x_2)^2 + (y_1-y_2)^2 + (z_1-z_2)^2}$ `[FB]`
  - Midpoint: $\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}, \frac{z_1+z_2}{2}\right)$ `[FB]`
  - Right-pyramid volume: $V = \frac{1}{3}Ah$; Right cone volume: $V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$; Cone curved surface area: $A = \pi r l$ `[FB]`
  - Sphere volume: $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$; Sphere surface area: $A = 4\pi r^2$ `[FB]`
  - Hemisphere: $V = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^3$, curved SA $= 2\pi r^2$, total closed SA $= 3\pi r^2$; Cone total SA $= \pi r^2 + \pi r l$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Leaving volumes/surface areas in exact terms of $\pi$ or surds; finding unknown radius/height algebraically. **P2:** 3D angles (line-to-plane projection using right triangles) to 3 sf.

#### `SL 3.2` — Triangle Trigonometry: Right & Non-Right *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Right-triangle SOH-CAH-TOA; Sine rule; Cosine rule; Area of a triangle $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. **Explicit Clarification:** `SL 3.2` does *not* cover the ambiguous case of the sine rule (that is introduced specifically for AA SL in `SL 3.5`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$ `[FB]`
  - $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C;\; \cos C = \frac{a^2+b^2-c^2}{2ab}$ `[FB]`
  - $A = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$ `[FB]`
  - SOH-CAH-TOA `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact-value trig angles ($30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 120^\circ, 135^\circ$) or cosine rule giving rational $\cos C$, finding $\sin C$ via Pythagorean identity. **P2:** General non-exact lengths, angles, and areas to 3 sf.

#### `SL 3.3` — Applications of Trigonometry (Elevation, Depression, Bearings) *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Constructing well-labeled 2D/3D diagrams from word problems; angles of elevation and depression; 3-figure bearings (measured clockwise from North $000^\circ$ to $360^\circ$); Pythagoras' theorem applications.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P2 Dominant:** Multi-triangle navigation/surveying problems using Sine/Cosine rules and parallel North-line alternate/interior angles.

#### `SL 3.4` — Radians, Arc Length & Sector Area *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Converting degrees $\leftrightarrow$ radians; arc length and sector area in radians (exact multiples of $\pi$ or decimals); perimeter of sector ($l + 2r$) and area of segment (sector minus triangle).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $l = r\theta$ ($\theta$ in radians) `[FB]`
  - $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ ($\theta$ in radians) `[FB]`
  - Conversion: $\pi \text{ rad} = 180^\circ$; Area of circular segment: $A_{\text{seg}} = \frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact $\theta$ in $\pi$ multiples, shaded areas involving segments/sectors/triangles. **P2:** Solving for $\theta$ numerically in overlapping circle/chord problems (often combined with GDC intersect).

#### `SL 3.5` — Unit Circle, Exact Ratios & Ambiguous Case *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Unit circle definitions $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$; $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$; quadrant symmetry ($\cos(-x) = \cos x$, $\sin(\pi+x) = -\sin x$, $\tan(3\pi-x) = -\tan x$); straight line through origin $y = x\tan\theta$; exact trig ratios for $0, \frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{\pi}{4}, \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{\pi}{2}$ and multiples; **ambiguous case of the sine rule** (recognizing when two triangles exist: $\theta$ and $180^\circ - \theta$ where sum with known angle $< 180^\circ$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$ `[FB]`
  - Exact values table ($0, \pi/6, \pi/4, \pi/3, \pi/2$), ASTC/CAST quadrant signs, $m = \tan\theta$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Exact trigonometric evaluations without GDC, finding all solutions or acute/obtuse values from unit circle. **P2:** Finding both possible triangles in the ambiguous case of the Sine Rule.

#### `SL 3.6` — Trigonometric Identities *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Pythagorean identity $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$; double-angle identities for $\sin 2\theta$ and $\cos 2\theta$; deducing one trig ratio from another using quadrant sign without finding $\theta$ (e.g., given $\cos x = 3/4$, $x$ acute, find $\sin 2x$ or $\tan x$); proving simple trig identities (`LHS` to `RHS`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$ `[FB]`
  - $\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta$ `[FB]`
  - $\cos 2\theta = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1 = 1 - 2\sin^2\theta$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Exact triangle/quadrant ratio deduction and `Show that` trig identities.

#### `SL 3.7` — Circular Functions & Sinusoidal Modeling *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Graphs & properties of $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\tan x$ (amplitude, period, asymptotes of $\tan x$ at $x = \frac{\pi}{2} + k\pi$); composite sinusoidal model $f(x) = a\sin(b(x+c)) + d$ (and $\cos$ equivalent); domains in degrees or radians; links to transformations (`SL 2.11`). Real-life modeling (Ferris wheel, tides, seasonal temperatures). Awareness that GDC sinusoidal regression may output $a\sin(bx+c)+d$ rather than factored $b(x+c)$.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - For $f(x) = a\sin(b(x+c))+d$ ($b > 0$): Amplitude $= |a| = \frac{\max - \min}{2}$, Principal axis $y = d = \frac{\max + \min}{2}$, Period $T = \frac{2\pi}{b}$ (rad) or $\frac{360^\circ}{b}$ (deg), Horizontal shift $-c$ `[MEM]`
  - For $f(x) = \tan(bx)$: Period $= \frac{\pi}{b}$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Extracting parameters $(a, b, c, d)$ from a given graph or sketching transformed trig graphs. **P2:** Modeling tides/Ferris wheels and solving $f(t) \ge k$ intervals via GDC.

#### `SL 3.8` — Solving Trigonometric Equations *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Solving trig equations analytically and graphically on a **finite interval** ($0 \le x \le 2\pi$, $-\pi \le x \le 3\pi$, $0^\circ \le x \le 180^\circ$), including double-angle substitutions ($2\sin 2x = 3\cos x$, $2\sin x = \cos 2x$) and equations quadratic in $\sin x, \cos x, \tan x$ (e.g. $2\sin^2 x + 5\cos x + 1 = 0$). **Explicit Note:** *"Not required: The general solution of trigonometric equations."*
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Factorable quadratics in $\sin/\cos/\tan$, domain adjustment ($u = b(x+c)$ interval scaling!), exact radian roots. **P2:** Graphical intersections for equations like $\sin(2x) = x - 1$.

- **Topic 3 Explicit Exclusions:**
  - **HL Only (`AHL 3.9–3.18`):** Reciprocal trig ($\sec, \csc, \cot$), inverse trig functions ($\arcsin, \arccos, \arctan$), compound angle identities ($\sin(A\pm B)$, etc.), $\tan 2\theta$, general trig solutions, **ALL 2D/3D Vectors** (dot/cross products, vector lines, planes, skew lines).
  - **AI Only:** Voronoi diagrams (`AI SL 3.6`), transformation matrices, graph theory (`AI HL`).

### Topic 4: Statistics and Probability (`27 Hours` · `SL 4.1`–`SL 4.12`)

*`SL 4.1`–`SL 4.9` are Common SL Core; `SL 4.10`–`SL 4.12` are AA-Specific SL. At SL, datasets are treated as the **population** ($\sigma_x$) unless explicitly stated as a sample ($s_x$).*

#### `SL 4.1` — Sampling, Data Types, Bias & Outliers *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Population vs. sample, random sample, discrete vs. continuous data; reliability of data sources, sampling bias, missing data/recording errors; outlier definition & context interpretation (valid extreme vs. measurement error); sampling techniques and effectiveness (**simple random, convenience, systematic, quota, stratified**).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - Outlier fences: $\text{Lower} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$, $\text{Upper} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1/P2 Conceptual:** Naming sampling technique, identifying bias, computing outlier boundaries from box plots or quartile summaries.

#### `SL 4.2` — Data Presentation (Tables, Histograms, Ogives, Box Plots) *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Frequency tables (continuous class intervals written as gapless inequalities, e.g., $10 \le x < 20$); **frequency histograms with equal class intervals only** (*frequency density histograms NOT required*); cumulative frequency tables/curves (ogives) to read median, $Q_1, Q_3$, percentiles, range, IQR; box-and-whisker plots (outliers marked with a cross `×`), comparing two distributions via symmetry, median, IQR, range; assessing approximate normality from box-plot symmetry.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$ `[FB]`
  - $\text{Range} = \max - \min$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Reading cumulative frequency graphs or box plots, checking symmetry/outliers. **P2:** Computing quartiles/box-plot values from raw data lists on GDC.

#### `SL 4.3` — Measures of Central Tendency & Dispersion *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Mean, median, mode; estimating mean of grouped data using mid-interval values; modal class (equal intervals only); IQR, standard deviation, and variance ($\text{Var} = \sigma^2$). Standard deviation and variance calculated via **technology** (`1-Var Stats`). **Effect of linear data shifts/scaling** ($x + k$ shifts mean/median by $+k$, leaves IQR/SD/variance unchanged; $kx$ scales mean/median/IQR/SD by $|k|$, scales variance by $k^2$). Quartiles of discrete data via technology (note differing hand vs. calculator conventions exist).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - Mean: $\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k f_i x_i}{n}$, where $n = \sum_{i=1}^k f_i$ `[FB]`
  - $\text{Variance} = (\text{Standard deviation})^2$; Linear transform $Y = aX + b \implies \bar{y} = a\bar{x}+b,\; \sigma_y = |a|\sigma_x,\; \text{Var}(Y) = a^2\text{Var}(X)$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Finding missing value/frequency given $\bar{x}$, applying linear transformation rules ($ax+b$) to given mean & SD/variance without raw data. **P2:** GDC `1-Var Stats` ($\bar{x}, \sigma_x, Q_1, \text{Med}, Q_3$).

#### `SL 4.4` — Linear Correlation & Regression ($y$ on $x$) *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Scatter diagrams, line of best fit by eye passing through mean point $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$; Pearson's product-moment correlation coefficient $r$ via technology (meaningful **only for linear relationships**; critical values given if needed); positive/zero/negative, strong/weak/none; **correlation $\neq$ causation**. Regression line of **$y$ on $x$** ($y = ax+b$) via GDC; interpreting gradient $a$ and $y$-intercept $b$ in context; **dangers of extrapolation**; aware you cannot reliably predict $x$ from $y$ using a $y$-on-$x$ line.
- **Key Formulas:** Regression line $y = ax + b$ passes through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sketching line through $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$, interpreting $r$, $a$, $b$, or explaining why prediction is invalid (extrapolation / wrong regression direction). **P2:** GDC `LinReg(ax+b)` to extract $a, b, r$ and predict $y$.

#### `SL 4.5` — Introductory Probability & Expected Occurrences *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Trial, outcome, equally likely outcomes, relative frequency vs theoretical probability, sample space $U$, event $A$, complementary events $A$ and $A'$, sample space tables/lists, expected number of occurrences ($n \times P(A)$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $P(A) = \frac{n(A)}{n(U)}$ `[FB]`
  - $P(A) + P(A') = 1$ `[FB]`
  - Expected occurrences $= n \times P(A)$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sample space grids (e.g., two dice), exact fractions, complement shortcuts ("at least one").

#### `SL 4.6` — Combined Events, Venn/Tree Diagrams, Conditional & Independence *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, sample space diagrams, tables of outcomes; union/addition rule ("or" is inclusive); mutually exclusive events; conditional probability; probabilities with and without replacement; independent events. Problems can often be solved directly from diagrams without explicit formula manipulation.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$ `[FB]`
  - Mutually exclusive: $P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B)$ `[FB]` *(and $P(A \cap B) = 0$ `[MEM]`)*
  - Conditional probability: $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$ `[FB]` *(alternate form $P(A \cap B) = P(B)P(A|B)$ `[MEM]`)*
  - Independent events: $P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Venn diagram unknown regions, tree diagrams without replacement, exact fraction probabilities. **P2:** Decimal conditional probabilities from tables/trees.

#### `SL 4.7` — Discrete Random Variables & Expectation *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Probability distributions of discrete RV $X$ given via table or piecewise formula $P(X=x)$; normalization $\sum P(X=x) = 1$; expected value $E(X)$; **fair game** criterion $E(X) = 0$ (where $X$ is player's net gain).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $E(X) = \sum x P(X = x)$ `[FB]`
  - $\sum P(X = x) = 1$; Fair game $\iff E(\text{Gain}) = 0$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Solving for unknown constant $k$ (and simultaneous equations using $\sum p_i = 1$ and $E(X) = \mu$), fair game ticket price. **P2:** Expected value from larger tables using GDC `1-Var Stats` (`List 1 = x`, `List 2 = P(X=x)`).

#### `SL 4.8` — Binomial Distribution *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Conditions for $X \sim B(n, p)$ (fixed $n$ independent trials, 2 outcomes, constant $p$); mean and variance (formal proofs NOT required). In exams, binomial probabilities should be found using technology, though Paper 1 can ask small-$n$ cases via Binomial Theorem/combinatorics or expect `E(X)=np` and `Var(X)=np(1-p)`.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $X \sim B(n, p) \implies E(X) = np,\; \text{Var}(X) = np(1-p)$ `[FB]`
  - $P(X = x) = {}^nC_x p^x (1-p)^{n-x}$ `[MEM]` *(for P1 small-$n$ or algebraic solving for $n$ or $p$!)*
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** $E(X), \text{Var}(X)$, small $n \le 4$ exact binomial probability, solving $P(X \ge 1) = 1 - (1-p)^n$. **P2:** GDC `binomPdf` / `binomCdf` and finding smallest $n$ via GDC table/graph.

#### `SL 4.9` — Normal Distribution *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Bell curve properties ($X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ — **note second parameter is variance $\sigma^2$, NOT $\sigma$!**); diagrammatic sketches; **empirical rule** (~68% in $\mu \pm \sigma$, ~95% in $\mu \pm 2\sigma$, ~99.7% in $\mu \pm 3\sigma$). Normal probability and inverse normal calculations (when $\mu, \sigma$ are given) must be found using technology (no $z$-transformation needed here).
- **Key Formulas:** Symmetry about $x = \mu$, total area $= 1$, empirical rule 68–95–99.7% `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sketching normal curve, empirical rule estimates, symmetry comparisons ($P(X < \mu - a) = P(X > \mu + a)$). **P2:** GDC `normalCdf` and `invNorm` (careful with left-tail vs right-tail!).

#### `SL 4.10` — Regression Line of $x$ on $y$ *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Equation of the regression line of $x$ on $y$ (swap lists on GDC so $x$ is predicted from $y$: $x = cy + d$); predicting $x$ from a given $y$; awareness that you cannot reliably predict $y$ from $x$ using an $x$-on-$y$ line.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P2:** Swapping $L_1, L_2$ in `LinReg` to get $x$ on $y$ and noting $(\bar{y}, \bar{x})$ passes through it.

#### `SL 4.11` — Formal Conditional Probability & Independence Testing *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Formal equations $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$, $P(A \cap B) = P(B)P(A|B)$, and formal test for independence: **$P(A|B) = P(A) = P(A|B')$** or $P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$; $P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B)$ `[FB]`
  - $P(A|B) = P(A) = P(A|B')$ (independence test) `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** Algebraic proofs/tests of independence from Venn/symbolic probability statements (e.g., showing whether $A$ and $B$ are independent).

#### `SL 4.12` — Standardization ($z$-values) & Unknown $\mu, \sigma$ *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Standard normal $Z \sim N(0, 1)$, interpreting $z$ as number of standard deviations from $\mu$, inverse normal calculations where **$\mu$ and/or $\sigma$ are unknown** (setting up simultaneous linear equations in $\mu, \sigma$ from two percentiles/probabilities!). Probabilities/quantiles found using technology (`invNorm(area, 0, 1)`).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Writing $z$-equations or comparing two scores via $z$-values. **P2 Dominant:** `invNorm` on $N(0, 1)$ + solving simultaneous equations for unknown $\mu$ and $\sigma$.

- **Topic 4 Explicit Exclusions:**
  - **HL Only (`AHL 4.13–4.14`):** Bayes' theorem; variance/SD calculation formulas $\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$ for general discrete RVs; linear combinations $E(aX+b), \text{Var}(aX+b)$ for RVs (though `SL 4.3` covers data linear transforms!); **all continuous random variables & PDFs** beyond normal curves.
  - **AI Only:** Spearman's rank correlation; Chi-squared ($\chi^2$) tests; Student's $t$-test; Poisson distribution; confidence intervals; hypothesis testing (Type I/II errors); Markov chains.

### Topic 5: Calculus (`28 Hours` · `SL 5.1`–`SL 5.11`)

*`SL 5.1`–`SL 5.5` are Common SL Core; `SL 5.6`–`SL 5.11` are AA-Specific SL.*

#### `SL 5.1` — Concept of a Limit & Derivative as Rate of Change *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Intuitive concept of a limit; estimating a limit from a table or graph. **Explicit Exclusion:** *"Not required: Formal analytic methods of calculating limits."* *(No algebraic limit evaluation and NO derivative from first principles at SL!)* Derivative interpreted as gradient function and instantaneous rate of change; notation $\frac{dy}{dx}, f'(x), \frac{dV}{dr}, \frac{ds}{dt}$.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** Interpreting rate-of-change statements with units (e.g., $V'(5) = -2\text{ L min}^{-1}$), reading limits from tables/graphs.

#### `SL 5.2` — Increasing & Decreasing Functions *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Graphical interpretation of $f'(x) > 0$ (increasing), $f'(x) = 0$ (stationary), $f'(x) < 0$ (decreasing); finding intervals where $f$ is increasing or decreasing via sign diagrams or derivative inequalities.
- **Key Formulas:** Increasing: $f'(x) > 0$; Decreasing: $f'(x) < 0$; Stationary: $f'(x) = 0$ `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Solving $f'(x) > 0$ (quadratic/rational sign test). **P2:** Reading intervals from GDC graph of $f'(x)$.

#### `SL 5.3` — Power Rule for Integer Powers *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Derivative of $f(x) = ax^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$) and polynomials $f(x) = ax^n + bx^{n-1} + \dots$ (rewrite fractions like $\frac{3}{x^2}$ as $3x^{-2}$ before differentiating).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $f(x) = x^n \implies f'(x) = n x^{n-1}$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Polynomial & negative integer power differentiation, solving $f'(x) = k$.

#### `SL 5.4` — Tangents and Normals *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Finding equations of tangent and normal lines at a given point $(x_0, y_0)$ both analytically and with technology; horizontal tangents ($f'(x)=0$); parallel/perpendicular tangent problems.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - Tangent: $y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a)$ `[MEM]`
  - Normal: $y - f(a) = -\frac{1}{f'(a)}(x - a)$ ($f'(a) \neq 0$) `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact tangent/normal equations, finding second point where tangent/normal re-intersects curve. **P2:** GDC `nDeriv` + `Tangent` line intersections.

#### `SL 5.5` — Introduction to Integration & Boundary Conditions *(Common SL Core)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Integration as anti-differentiation of polynomial terms $n \in \mathbb{Z},\; n \neq -1$; **using a boundary condition to find $+C$**; link between anti-derivative, definite integral, and area; definite integrals using technology; area enclosed by $y = f(x)$ and $x$-axis where $f(x) > 0$. **Crucial Requirement:** Students must first write a valid integral expression (e.g., $\int_2^6 (3x^2+4)\,dx$) before computing via GDC.
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C,\; n \neq -1$ `[FB]`
  - Area ($f(x) > 0$): $A = \int_a^b y\,dx$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Indefinite integrals with $+C$ and initial point condition. **P2:** Numerical definite integrals (`fnInt`) after writing integral setup.

#### `SL 5.6` — Differentiation Rules & Transcendental Derivatives *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Derivatives of $x^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Q}$, i.e., fractional roots like $\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$), $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $e^x$, $\ln x$; sum and constant multiple; **Chain rule**, **Product rule**, and **Quotient rule** (and combining multiple rules, e.g., $x^2 e^{\sin x}$ or $\frac{\ln(2x+1)}{x^2}$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$; $\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x$; $\frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x$; $\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x}$ `[FB]`
  - Chain rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \times \frac{du}{dx}$ `[FB]`
  - Product rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx}$ `[FB]`
  - Quotient rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{v\frac{du}{dx} - u\frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$ `[FB]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 (Core Testing Ground):** Heavy algebraic differentiation, simplifying rational/exponential derivatives to match `Show that` forms, stationary points of $xe^{-x}$ or $\ln x / x$.

#### `SL 5.7` — Second Derivative & Graph Relationships ($f, f', f''$) *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Second derivative notation $\frac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ and $f''(x)$; graphical correspondence between $f$, $f'$, and $f''$ (e.g., $x$-intercepts of $f'$ $\leftrightarrow$ stationary points of $f$; extrema of $f'$ $\leftrightarrow$ points of inflexion of $f$; sign of $f''$ $\leftrightarrow$ slope behavior of $f'$ and concavity of $f$).
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Sketching $f'$ from $f$ (or $f$ from $f'$), multiple-statement matching between $f, f', f''$. **P2:** Computing second derivatives numerically or verifying concavity graphically.

#### `SL 5.8` — Extrema, Concavity, Points of Inflexion & Optimization *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Local maxima and minima; testing via **first derivative sign change** or **second derivative test** ($f''(x) > 0 \implies$ local minimum; $f''(x) < 0 \implies$ local maximum); **points of inflexion** (both zero and non-zero gradient!) defined by **$f''(x) = 0$ AND sign change of $f''(x)$** (counterexample highlighted in guide: $y = x^4$ has $f''(0)=0$ but NO sign change $\implies$ NOT an inflexion point!); terminology **concave-up** ($f''(x) > 0$) and **concave-down** ($f''(x) < 0$); **applied optimization** (profit, area, volume, surface area, minimum distance).
- **Key Formulas:** Stationary point $f'(x)=0$; Max $f''<0$, Min $f''>0$; Inflexion $f''=0$ with sign change `[MEM]`.
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Proving max/min/inflexion via sign table or $f''$, 3D box/cylinder optimization `Show that` + exact derivative. **P2:** Finding optimal dimensions numerically when solving $V'(r) = 0$ requires GDC.

#### `SL 5.9` — Kinematics ($s, v, a$ and Total Distance) *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Displacement $s(t)$, velocity $v(t)$, acceleration $a(t)$, speed $= |v(t)|$; particle at rest ($v=0$); changes direction ($v$ changes sign); **net displacement** vs **total distance travelled** (splitting integral at $v(t)=0$ roots on P1, or integrating $|v(t)|$ directly on P2 GDC).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $v = \frac{ds}{dt}$; $a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}$ `[FB]`
  - $\text{displacement} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} v(t)\,dt$ `[FB]`
  - $\text{distance} = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} |v(t)|\,dt$ `[FB]`
  - Speed $= |v(t)|$; speeding up when $v(t) \cdot a(t) > 0$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact analytical integration of $v(t)$ with initial condition $s(0)=s_0$, splitting total distance across roots of $v(t)=0$. **P2:** Direct GDC `fnInt(abs(v(t)), t, t1, t2)` for distance and `nDeriv` for acceleration.

#### `SL 5.10` — Indefinite Integration, Linear Composites & Reverse Chain Rule Substitution *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:**
  1. Standard indefinite integrals of $x^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Q},\; n \neq -1$), $\sin x$, $\cos x$, $\frac{1}{x}$ ($\ln|x|+C$), and $e^x$.
  2. Composites with linear function $ax+b$: e.g., $\int \cos(2x+3)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\sin(2x+3) + C$.
  3. **Integration by inspection (reverse chain rule) or by $u$-substitution ONLY for expressions of the form $\int k\, g'(x)\, f(g(x))\,dx$** (Guide examples: $\int 2x(x^2+1)^4\,dx$, $\int 4x\sin(x^2)\,dx$, $\int \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}\,dx$).
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + C$ `[FB]`
  - $\int \sin x\,dx = -\cos x + C$; $\int \cos x\,dx = \sin x + C$; $\int e^x\,dx = e^x + C$ `[FB]`
  - Linear composites: $\int f(ax+b)\,dx = \frac{1}{a}F(ax+b) + C$; $\int \frac{g'(x)}{g(x)}\,dx = \ln|g(x)| + C$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1 Dominant:** All symbolic anti-derivatives, $u$-substitutions of form $k g'(x) f(g(x))$, and boundary-condition problems ($f''(x) \to f'(x) \to f(x)$).

#### `SL 5.11` — Definite Integrals & Areas Between Curves *(AA-Specific SL)*
- **Learning Outcomes & Guidance:** Analytical evaluation via Fundamental Theorem of Calculus $\int_a^b g'(x)\,dx = g(b) - g(a)$ (alongside GDC definite integrals when no elementary antiderivative exists); area between curve $y = f(x)$ and $x$-axis **where $f(x)$ can be positive or negative, without technology** (split at roots and take absolute values!); **areas between two curves**; writing the correct integral expression before computing. *(Note: Trapezoidal rule / Simpson's rule are Enrichment only, NOT examined in AA SL.)*
- **Key Formulas:**
  - $A = \int_a^b |y|\,dx$ `[FB]`
  - $\int_a^b g'(x)\,dx = g(b) - g(a)$ `[MEM]`
  - Area between $f(x)$ and $g(x)$: $A = \int_a^b |f(x) - g(x)|\,dx = \int_a^b (\text{upper} - \text{lower})\,dx$ `[MEM]`
- **P1 vs P2 Emphasis:** **P1:** Exact analytical areas (splitting signed regions below $x$-axis!), algebraic integral properties ($\int_a^b 2f(x)\,dx$, $\int_a^c + \int_c^b$). **P2:** Finding curve intersections $a, b$ on GDC, then computing $\int_a^b (f(x) - g(x))\,dx$ via `fnInt`.

- **Topic 5 Explicit Exclusions:**
  - **HL Only (`AHL 5.12–5.19`):** Continuity/differentiability proofs; **First Principles differentiation**; L'Hôpital's rule; implicit differentiation & related rates; derivatives/integrals of $\tan x, \sec x, \csc x, \cot x, a^x, \log_a x$, inverse trig ($\arcsin, \arccos, \arctan$); general substitution where $x$ must be solved in terms of $u$; **integration by parts**; areas bounded by $y$-axis ($\int |x|dy$); **volumes of revolution**; differential equations & Euler's method; Maclaurin series.
  - **AI Only:** Trapezoidal rule (`AI SL 5.8`), slope fields / phase portraits / coupled DEs (`AI HL`).

### 3.4 IB Math Command Terms, ISO Notation Rules & Rounding Standard

#### Official IB Math AA Command Terms *(IB Guide pp. 89–90)*

| Command Term | Official Definition | Exam Action & Working Rule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Write down** | Obtain answer(s), usually by extracting info. Little/no calculation. | **No working required.** Don't waste time deriving; extract directly. |
| **State** | Give a specific name, value or brief answer without explanation/calculation. | State exact term/value concisely. |
| **Identify** | Provide an answer from a number of possibilities. | Pick/state the exact element or feature. |
| **List** | Give a sequence of brief answers with no explanation. | Enumerate cleanly. |
| **Find** | Obtain an answer showing relevant stages in the working. | Must show method/steps (`M` marks!). Even with GDC, write equation/integral/distribution setup. |
| **Calculate** | Obtain a numerical answer showing the relevant stages in the working. | Show numerical substitution/stages; check 3 sf vs exact requirement. |
| **Determine** | Obtain the **only possible** answer. | Logical/algebraic steps narrowing down to a single unique answer (often discarding extraneous roots). |
| **Show** | Give the steps in a calculation or derivation. | Write every logical/algebraic step explicitly. |
| **Show that** | Obtain the required result (possibly using given info) without formality of proof. Does not generally require GDC. | **Answer is Given (`AG`)!** Never work backwards from the printed result; never substitute the given value to "check" instead of deriving it. |
| **Prove** | Use a sequence of logical steps to obtain the required result in a formal way. | Strict `LHS = ... = RHS` (or deductive chain), clear `≡` vs `=` handling. |
| **Deduce** | Reach a conclusion from the information given. | Connect prior facts/results logically to state what follows. |
| **Demonstrate** | Make clear by reasoning or evidence, illustrating with examples/application. | Especially critical in IA Criterion E ("demonstrate understanding"). |
| **Verify** | Provide evidence that validates the result. | Substitution check IS valid here (unlike `Show that`!). |
| **Justify** | Give valid reasons or evidence to support an answer or conclusion. | Explicitly cite mathematical tests (e.g., "$f''(2) > 0$, hence local minimum"; "$|r| < 1$"). |
| **Explain** | Give a detailed account including reasons or causes. | Clear prose + mathematical reasoning. |
| **Comment** | Give a judgment based on a given statement or result of a calculation. | Interpret meaning in real-world or statistical context (e.g., validity of extrapolation). |
| **Compare / Contrast / Compare and contrast** | Similarities (`Compare`), differences (`Contrast`), or both (`Compare and contrast`), referring to all items throughout. | Compare two distributions using *both* center (median/mean) and spread (IQR/SD) in context! |
| **Construct** | Display information in a diagrammatic or logical form. | Build tree diagram, Venn diagram, table, or labeled geometry diagram. |
| **Describe / Distinguish** | Detailed account (`Describe`); make clear differences between items (`Distinguish`). | Use exact IB terminology (e.g., "translation by $\binom{3}{-2}$"). |
| **Draw** | Labelled, **accurate** diagram/graph using pencil and ruler for straight lines, **to scale**, points accurately plotted and joined smoothly/linearly. | **Scale matters!** Plot exact coordinates accurately on grid. |
| **Sketch** | General idea of shape/relationship, labelled with **key features** (intercepts, asymptotes, max/min, endpoints). | Ruler not needed, grid scale not exact, but **relative positions and key features MUST be accurate and labeled**. |
| **Plot / Label** | Mark position of points (`Plot`); add labels to diagram (`Label`). | Mark clearly (`×` or dot) and annotate axes/features. |
| **Estimate / Predict** | Approximate value (`Estimate`); expected result (`Predict`). | Often from ogive, mid-intervals, or regression line. |
| **Interpret / Investigate** | Recognize trends & draw conclusions (`Interpret`); systematic examination to establish facts (`Investigate`). | Connect parameters (gradient $m$, $y$-int, $r$) to context units. |
| **Solve** | Obtain answer(s) using algebraic and/or numerical and/or graphical methods. | Show equation setup + method; give all solutions in stated domain! |
| **Suggest** | Propose a solution, hypothesis, or possible answer. | Concise reasoned proposal (e.g., bias source, model improvement). |
| **Differentiate / Integrate** | Obtain derivative (`Differentiate`); obtain integral (`Integrate`). | Don't forget $+C$ on indefinite integrals! |
| **Hence** | **Use the preceding work** to obtain the required result. | **Mandatory constraint:** Alternative methods that bypass the previous part earn **0 marks**! |
| **Hence or otherwise** | Preceding work suggested, but other valid methods receive credit. | Alternative methods accepted, though prior part is usually the intended shortcut. |

#### Official IB ISO Notation Rules *(IB Guide pp. 91–93)* — Critical Traps
1. **Natural Numbers Start at Zero:** In IB ISO notation, $\mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\}$ whereas $\mathbb{Z}^+ = \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$! *(Wait: note `SL 1.9` applies $(a+b)^n, n \in \mathbb{N}$ where $n=0$ gives $1$, while sequence term indices $n \in \mathbb{Z}^+$ start at $1$.)*
2. **Calculator Notation Banned:** `5.2E30`, `X^2`, `binomcdf(...)`, `log(x)` without base ambiguity: students must write $5.2 \times 10^{30}$ (`SL 1.1`), $\ln x$ for $\log_e x$, and standard math notation (`IA Crit B` penalizes raw calculator/computer syntax unless software-generated output).
3. **Geometry Notation:** `[AB]` = line segment with endpoints $A, B$; `AB` = length of `[AB]`; `(AB)` = infinite line through $A, B$; $\hat{A}$ or $C\hat{A}B$ = angle at $A$; $\triangle ABC$ = triangle.
4. **Equality vs. Identity:** `=` denotes equation equality; `≡` denotes algebraic identity true for all valid values (`SL 1.6`).
5. **Probability & Statistics Symbols:** $P(A')$ = complement ("not $A$"); $P(A|B)$ = conditional; $\mu, \sigma^2, \sigma$ = population parameters; $\bar{x}$ = sample mean; $X \sim B(n, p)$; $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$ *(second parameter is variance $\sigma^2$, NOT $\sigma$)*.

#### The 3-Significant-Figure & Exact-Value Rule
- **Default Accuracy Rule:** Unless otherwise stated in the question, **all non-exact numerical answers must be given either EXACTLY or correct to three significant figures (3 sf)** (e.g., $0.0452$, $12.3$, $1450$).
- **When Exact Is Required:**
  - All **Paper 1** questions where exact values arise ($\frac{7}{3}$, $2\sqrt{5}$, $3\pi + 1$, $\ln 4$, $e^{-2}$).
  - Any question saying *"Find the exact value..."* or *"Write in the form $a\sqrt{b}$ / $p\pi + q$ / $\ln k$"*. Writing a 3 sf decimal when exact is asked loses the `A1` mark.
- **No Premature Rounding:** Store full calculator precision (`ANS` or alpha variables `A, B, C`) during multi-step calculations! Rounding intermediate numbers to 3 sf before the final step causes cascading rounding drift and loses the final `A1` mark.
- **Special Exceptions:** Money answers when cents are implied/specified (e.g., *"to the nearest dollar"* or *"to 2 decimal places"*); integer counts ($n$ terms, people, trials — check whether question asks *"at least"* / *"to the nearest integer"*).

4. `section === 'A'` questions typically span `4–8 marks`; `section === 'B'` questions span `12–17 marks` with ascending sub-part difficulty (`Incline of difficulty`) and multi-topic synthesis.

## 5. Mathematical Exploration (IA) Criteria A–E Rubric Breakdown

### 5.1 Structural & Submission Requirements *(IB Guide pp. 80–88)*
- **Weight & Marks:** **20% of final DP grade**, marked out of **20 marks** across Criteria A–E (whole numbers only; partial/decimal marks disallowed).
- **Length & Format:** Recommended **12–20 pages** (double-spaced, including embedded diagrams, graphs, tables, and formulas; **excluding bibliography**). Overly long papers risk Criterion A conciseness penalties.
- **Cover Page Required Details:** Title of the exploration and number of pages.
- **Single-Draft Feedback Rule:** Teachers may read and give oral/written advice on **one draft** without editing it; the next submission is final.
- **Academic Honesty & Citations:** All external formulas, datasets, diagrams, and code/software tools must be cited in a bibliography. Uncited sources trigger malpractice review even though references themselves are not scored. Cannot overlap with an Extended Essay (EE).

### 5.3 SL-Level Criterion E Expectations & AI Essay Studio Calibration

1. **Prior Learning Cap:** If an exploration relies purely on Prior Learning (basic percentages, bar charts, mean/median/mode, simple linear equations, Pythagoras), it is **NOT commensurate with SL** and cannot score above **1–2** in Criterion E!
2. **Depth Over Unnecessary Complexity ("Do a few things well"):** The IB Guide explicitly emphasizes that **using 1–2 SL topics thoroughly and accurately** (e.g., calculus optimization + area/integration, or sinusoidal/exponential modeling + regression residuals, or binomial/normal inference + conditional probability) can earn **6/6**! Overly complex HL/university math bolted on without justification is flagged as *irrelevant* or shows *limited understanding* (`Level 3`).
3. **Demonstration Rule:** Simply plugging numbers into a formula or pasting GDC/Excel regression output **does NOT demonstrate understanding** (caps Criterion E at Level 1–2). Every step must explain *why* the method applies, show intermediate algebraic logic, and verify assumptions.
4. **Difference from HL Criterion E:** Notice HL levels 5–6 explicitly require **"sophistication and rigour"** (HL-level math or advanced proofs). SL levels 5–6 do **NOT** require sophistication/rigour beyond SL—only **relevance, SL-level parity, correctness (minor non-detracting slips permitted), and thorough demonstrated understanding throughout**.
5. **High-Scoring SL Exploration Archetypes (Zero HL Dependency):**
   - *Calculus Curve-Fitting & Volume/Area:* Photographing an object (vase, bottle, arch, rollercoaster profile), piecewise modeling via quadratics/exponentials/trig (`SL 2.6, 2.9, 3.7`), finding areas (`SL 5.11`), inflection points & steepness (`SL 5.7, 5.8`). *(Careful: SL does NOT cover Volumes of Revolution `AHL 5.17`—if an SL student uses disc integration $\int \pi y^2 dx$, they MUST briefly explain/derive the cylindrical slice limit concept in their own words to demonstrate understanding!)*
   - *Optimization from Real Geometry:* Designing optimal packaging, minimal surface area/cost containers, optimal viewing angles, or trajectory angles (`SL 3.2, 5.6, 5.8`).
   - *Financial & Exponential/Series Modeling:* Modeling depreciation vs. investment inflation (`SL 1.4`), fractal perimeters/areas via infinite geometric series (`SL 1.8`), cooling/epidemic/population curves (`SL 2.9, 2.10`).
   - *Probability & Statistical Modeling:* Testing whether real-world sports/game/weather data follows a Binomial (`SL 4.8`) or Normal (`SL 4.9, 4.12`) distribution, comparing linear $y$-on-$x$ vs transformed exponential models (`SL 4.4`), or analyzing fair games (`SL 4.7`).

### 6.2 SL Derivative & Integral Flashcards Matrix (`SL 5.3, 5.5, 5.6, 5.10`)

*Strictly SL-only (excludes $\tan x$, $\sec x$, $a^x$, $\log_a x$, inverse trig).*

| Function $f(x)$ | Derivative $f'(x)$ (`FB` / `MEM`) | Indefinite Integral $\int f(x)\,dx$ (`FB` / `MEM`) | Common SL Pitfall |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $k$ (constant) | $0$ | $kx + C$ | Writing $C$ alone or forgetting $x$ when integrating $k$ |
| $x^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Q}$) | $n x^{n-1}$ `[FB]` | $\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + C\;(n \neq -1)$ `[FB]` | Forgetting negative/fractional sign on $n-1$ or $n+1$ |
| $\frac{1}{x} = x^{-1}$ | $-x^{-2} = -\frac{1}{x^2}$ | $\ln|x| + C$ `[FB]` | Integrating $x^{-1}$ with power rule ($x^0/0$ undefined!) |
| $e^x$ | $e^x$ `[FB]` | $e^x + C$ `[FB]` | Treating $e^x$ like $x e^{x-1}$ |
| $e^{ax+b}$ | $a e^{ax+b}$ *(Chain)* | $\frac{1}{a} e^{ax+b} + C$ `[MEM]` | Multiplying by $a$ instead of dividing by $a$ on integral |
| $\ln x$ | $\frac{1}{x}$ `[FB]` | *(Not in SL syllabus!)* | Differentiating $\ln(2x)$ as $\frac{1}{2x}$ instead of $\frac{2}{2x} = \frac{1}{x}$! |
| $\sin x$ | $\cos x$ `[FB]` | $-\cos x + C$ `[FB]` | Swapping $\pm$ signs between derivative and integral! |
| $\cos x$ | $-\sin x$ `[FB]` | $\sin x + C$ `[FB]` | Forgetting negative on $\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)$ |
| $\sin(ax+b)$ | $a\cos(ax+b)$ | $-\frac{1}{a}\cos(ax+b) + C$ `[MEM]` | Missing chain factor $a$ or $1/a$ |
| $\cos(ax+b)$ | $-a\sin(ax+b)$ | $\frac{1}{a}\sin(ax+b) + C$ `[MEM]` | Missing negative or $1/a$ |
| $(ax+b)^n$ ($n \neq -1$) | $a n (ax+b)^{n-1}$ | $\frac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)} + C$ `[MEM]` | Forgetting inner derivative $a$ in denominator |
| $\frac{1}{ax+b}$ | $-\frac{a}{(ax+b)^2}$ | $\frac{1}{a}\ln|ax+b| + C$ `[MEM]` | Omitting $\frac{1}{a}$ factor |
| $k\, g'(x)\, f(g(x))$ | *(Product + Chain)* | $k\, F(g(x)) + C$ (by $u=g(x)$) `[MEM]` | Failing to check if $g'(x)$ matches numerator/outside factor |
| $\frac{g'(x)}{g(x)}$ | *(Quotient)* | $\ln|g(x)| + C$ (e.g., $\int \tan x\,dx = -\ln|\cos x|+C$) `[MEM]` | Attempting polynomial/quotient rules directly inside $\int$ |

### 6.3 Exponent & Logarithm Laws Cheatsheet (`SL 1.5, 1.7, 2.9`)

#### Valid Laws
1. **Exponents:** $a^m a^n = a^{m+n}$ · $\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$ · $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ · $(ab)^m = a^m b^m$ · $a^0 = 1$ ($a\neq 0$) · $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ · $a^{1/m} = \sqrt[m]{a}$ · $a^{p/q} = (\sqrt[q]{a})^p$.
2. **Definition & Identities:** $a^x = b \iff \log_a b = x$ ($a,b > 0, a \neq 1$) · $\log_{10} x = \log x$ · $\log_e x = \ln x$ · $\log_a 1 = 0$ · $\log_a a = 1$ · $\ln e^x = x$ · $e^{\ln x} = x$ ($x > 0$).
3. **Log Laws (`FB`):**
   - Product: $\log_a(xy) = \log_a x + \log_a y$
   - Quotient: $\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_a x - \log_a y$
   - Power: $\log_a(x^m) = m \log_a x$
   - Change of Base: $\log_a x = \frac{\log_b x}{\log_b a}$ (especially $\log_a x = \frac{\ln x}{\ln a}$)
   - Exponential Conversion: $a^x = e^{x\ln a}$

#### Illegal Algebra Traps (Highlight in Red in Cheatsheets)
- $\ln(x + y) \neq \ln x + \ln y$
- $\frac{\ln x}{\ln y} \neq \ln(x - y)$ and $\neq \ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)$
- $(\ln x)^2 \neq 2\ln x$ *(only $\ln(x^2) = 2\ln x$ when $x > 0$!)*
- $a^x + a^y \neq a^{x+y}$
